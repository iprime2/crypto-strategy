The strategy this trader should build is the one already in the repository, run smaller and slower than its author intended: a **long/flat, volatility-scaled time-series momentum rule on nine large Delta Exchange India perpetuals, executed by hand once a week at a 25% target-volatility setting, with 70% of the capital in a bank deposit**, and nothing else. It wins by default rather than by brilliance: it is the only engine in the evidence pack that survived its own after-cost test on this venue, while market making lost **−3.6 bps net per fill** in its first real-money run, option selling showed no out-of-sample edge, funding carry's Sharpe collapsed from 6.45 to negative in 2025, and every machine-learning, on-chain, sentiment, event and lead-lag idea failed the cost bar (`cex-mm/artifacts/research/2026-10-13-live-run-pickoff.md`; `options-research/README.md`; [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14435) [UNVERIFIED]). The honest expectation, after correcting the winning design's three optimistic headline numbers, is a **forward Sharpe of 0.3–0.8 (central 0.5), +4% to +9% a year at about 12% volatility, a maximum drawdown of 15–25%**, and over 2022–26 roughly an Indian fixed deposit before tax: on $1,000 that is **+$40 to +$90 a year**, on $5,000 five times that, and under the unfavourable 30% VDA tax reading flat years turn negative (`evidence/backtest_results.md` T1, T6b; `evidence/av_runs/av_universe_9.txt`, `av_sub.txt`). What the rule buys is shape, not return: **half the drawdown of holding the same coins vol-scaled (−17% vs −37%)** and a flat book in bear years like 2022. The prop-firm route (Breakout Classic, $25k first, the same signal run daily at half the native size with resting stops) is the only leg that can pay four figures, but every rule it rests on is a search snippet nobody has read on the firm's page, its forward expected value is **+$0.3–0.75k per $25k attempt and +$1.2–2.5k per $100k attempt** after a 15% non-payment haircut, and about a third of that is option value a disciplined zero-edge trader would collect (`evidence/pv_runs/payout_rule_shifted.txt`). It is therefore deferred until 26 live weeks, a written chartered-accountant opinion and written answers from the firm are in hand. Nothing in the research supports a larger number for a few thousand dollars of retail capital; the value of the next six months is a track record and a tested process, not profit.

## Executive summary: a small, cheap, drawdown-controlled exposure, not a money machine

The recommendation is the winning design D3 ("One Rule, Once a Week", `evidence/designs/D3.md`) with seven grafts from the runner-up designs and every correction from the adversarial verification applied. Three judges scored D3 49/46/46 against D2's 40/41/39 and D1's 31/32/32 on evidence, cost robustness, prop fit, implementability, ruin risk and simplicity; they agreed it is the only design whose exact executed configuration was run end to end at the deployment size, the least cost-sensitive by a factor of three, the simplest to run (two or three market orders a week from a spreadsheet, no bot) and the lowest ruin risk (70% off-venue, no prop fee for 26 weeks). They also agreed on its two weaknesses, a prop leg that was under-specified and a weekday choice that sits at the bottom of the luck distribution, and the grafts below address both. The verification then found that D3's headline numbers do not survive: the nine-coin universe is a hindsight set (DOGE's Binance perp dates from 2020-07-10 and BNB's from 2020-02-10, not January 2020 as the memo states), 64% of the backtest's log-return comes from May 2020 to December 2021, the "signal carries the result" claim overstates a 0.1–0.2 Sharpe increment, the only venue-native Delta number was computed for the wrong weekday, the after-tax column used a net basis the VDA reading does not allow, and the prop leg's "0% daily breach" is a knife-edge artefact of hourly data at exactly one leverage setting. This report headlines the corrected numbers.

## The evidence supports a long/flat trend rule, but at Sharpe 0.5, not 1.25

The rule under test is the one the user wrote on 2026-10-08: for each coin, the mean of the signs of the 20-, 60- and 120-day returns, floored at zero (long or flat), sized to a 40%/N target volatility over 60-day realized vol, nine coins, rebalanced at the 00:00 UTC close at taker 5.31 bps plus 2 bps slippage per side with funding charged. The memo reports CAGR +24.2%, Sharpe 1.25 and max drawdown −21.9% on Binance USDT-M data from 2020-05 to 2026-09, all 18 lookback-by-target-vol settings positive (Sharpe 0.99–1.25), and Sharpe 1.15 on Delta's own prices from 2024-08 with daily returns correlated 0.999 to the Binance run (`cex-mm/artifacts/research/2026-10-08-trend-following-backtest.md`). Those figures reproduce exactly in this session's engine (`evidence/backtest_results.md` T0: Sharpe 1.25, DD −21.9%, by year 53/47/−5/16/47/3/8) and in the evidence pack (`evidence/local_evidence.md`). The academic support is real but bounded: time-series momentum is the most-replicated crypto anomaly and the one that survives realistic frictions best while cross-sectional momentum does not ([Han, Kang & Ryu](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf) [UNVERIFIED]; [Liu & Tsyvinski, NBER w24877](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf); [Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003)), the short leg is where crypto momentum crashes ([Grobys et al. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9) [UNVERIFIED]; repo: long/short Sharpe 0.78 vs 1.25 and shorts lost 7% in 2023), Swinkels reported the effect "seems to have disappeared after the introduction of bitcoin futures" ([Tinbergen abstract](https://utilities.tinbergen.nl/?p=66235) [UNVERIFIED]), and the one investable live comparator, the Barclay Cryptocurrency Traders Index, lost 12.3% in 2025 after +54% in 2024 ([The Full FX](https://thefullfx.com/ctas-end-2025-on-a-positive-note/) [UNVERIFIED]).

The word "pre-registered" has to go. The script and the memo with its results were added in a single commit (`347d37d`, 2026-10-08 22:00 IST, `git log` on `cex-mm/scripts/trend_backtest.py`), so there is no timestamped registration separate from the results, and the memo chooses long-only after seeing the long/short run ("Shorts hurt … Long-only it is", memo line 41). The multiple-testing haircut for that is small: a deflated-Sharpe calculation over 18 to 200 correlated trials gives an expected maximum null Sharpe of 0.28–0.69 and a probability that the true Sharpe is positive of 86–99% for both the 1.25 daily and 1.12 weekly lines (`evidence/av_runs/av_dsr.txt`). The haircuts that matter are universe and regime, and they are large.

The universe row is the largest single correction. The memo says the nine coins are "the 9 large coins that had perps in January 2020 and are all listed on Delta India, so the test does not pick coins known to have risen later" (memo line 8); the Binance archive shows DOGE's perp first daily bar on 2020-07-10 and BNB's on 2020-02-10, and the set is exactly the 2026 large-cap survivors that Delta listed in April 2024. Run on every USDT-M perp that existed by 2020-02-20 the daily rule earns **Sharpe 0.86 and CAGR +13.8% instead of 1.25 and +24.2%**, the weekly design earns **0.82 and +8.8% instead of 1.12 and +14.1%**, and DOGE plus BNB alone add about 0.2 of Sharpe (`evidence/av_runs/av_universe_9.txt`). The nine-coin set is kept in the specification below because all nine have Delta contracts, known contract values and a running paper trader, but it is a hindsight universe and every expectation in this report is set from the ex-ante row. The cleaner design, a yearly ex-ante re-set to the nine largest Delta perps by 30-day turnover with at least 121 closes, has not been backtested in that form and must not be adopted without a run. Its closest feasible proxy, UNI-1 (the top nine Binance USDT-M perps by 30-day median turnover, at least 365 closes, keep-rank 18, with a 121-close neighbour), was run in the 2026-10-22 study and failed (rejected table below; `resource/08-trend-rule-improvement-research.md`).

Regime dependence is the second correction. On the design line, 2020–21 alone has Sharpe 2.01 and supplies 64% of the total log-return; **excluding 2020–21 the Sharpe is 0.66 (CAGR +6.5%), excluding 2020–21 and 2024 it is 0.31 (+2.4% a year over 3.75 years), 2022–23 is 0.35 and 2025–26 is 0.26**, while leave-one-year-out Sharpes run 0.94–1.34 (`evidence/av_runs/av_sub.txt`). Sampling error swamps all of this: the full-sample Sharpe of 1.13 has a Lo standard error of 0.50 (t = 2.2, block-bootstrap 95% interval 0.30 to 1.92), the 2022–26 window's 0.67 has an interval of −0.28 to 1.58, and the 2025–26 window's 0.26 has −1.06 to 1.36, with excess kurtosis of 14–15 that makes even those intervals optimistic (`backtest_results.md` T6f). The post-2021 edge is a point estimate, not an established fact, and twenty-six live weeks at 12% vol carry a Sharpe standard error of about 1.8 (T6e), so the live gate in the plan measures adherence, not edge.

The third correction is what the signal contributes. D3 claimed the signal, not the vol scaling, carries the result. At the same target vol, cadence and costs the always-long nine-coin basket has Sharpe 0.99 full-sample against the rule's 1.12, 0.57 against 0.66 in 2022–26 and 2.18 against 2.01 in 2020–21, when the basket beat the rule; the rule minus the vol-matched basket has Sharpe 0.18 full, 0.11 in 2022–26 and −0.37 in 2020–21 (`evidence/av_runs/av_sub.txt`, signal-contribution block). Most of the Sharpe is long crypto beta, vol-scaled. **The signal adds about 0.1–0.2 of Sharpe and halves the drawdown (−16.9% vs −36.6%) and the 2022 loss (−6.9% vs −19.1%).** That is still worth having, since a 37% drawdown on a few thousand dollars is the kind of loss that ends retail trading, but it should be understood as what is being bought. Summitward's independent test found the same shape, 0.74 to 0.79 of Sharpe from the signal on top of vol scaling ([Summitward](https://summitward.com/learn/crypto-trend-following) [UNVERIFIED]).

The weekly cadence is a modification of the tested rule, chosen after results were visible, and it tests worse on the chosen line. At TV 0.25 the daily rule has Sharpe 1.25, hourly max drawdown −14.5% and worst week −5.9% against the weekly design's 1.12, −17.7% and −7.4%, and in 2024–26 the gap widens to 1.11 against 0.86 (`backtest_results.md` T1). Across the seven possible decision weekdays the full-sample Sharpe runs 1.12–1.34 (Monday-candle 1.26, Tuesday 1.18, Wednesday 1.34, Thursday 1.31, Friday 1.23, Saturday 1.16, the design's Sunday candle 1.12), 2024–26 runs 0.86–1.20 with the design lowest, and 2022 runs from −0.3% (Thursday) to −7.1% (Monday-candle) (T3b). The design's weekday is also the lowest of six decision hours (00:00 UTC 1.12 vs 1.16–1.32 at 04/08/12/16/20 UTC, T3k) and the worst of seven on Delta (0.70 vs 0.92–1.15, T5). D3 kept the worst weekday deliberately to avoid curve-fitting and quoted the seven-weekday mean (1.23) as the expectation; that is a defensible choice, but the user realizes one path, and every kill level and money figure in this report is therefore derived from the design weekday, not the mean. The cost of weekly over daily is about 0.13 of Sharpe and three points of drawdown, paid for 346 fewer orders a year and no need for a bot; the repo's documented failures are operational (a Binance reference feed that froze silently for 3.5 days on 8–12 October, `cex-mm/artifacts/research/2026-10-12-live-stop-rule.md`), which is why the trade is accepted.

D3's only venue-native number was computed for the wrong weekday. Its "offset 3" counts rebalance days from the first trading day, which lands on the Sunday candle (Monday 00:00 UTC decision) on the Binance window but on the Monday candle (Tuesday 00:00 UTC decision) on the Delta window, because 2024-08-10 is a Saturday; the Monday-candle line reproduces D3's Delta figures exactly (Sharpe 1.01, +11.3% at $1,000), while the design's actual slot on Delta prices from 2024-08-10 to 2026-10-12 is **Sharpe 0.70, CAGR +7.0%, max DD −9.1% unrounded at TV 0.25 and 0.81 / +8.3% / −9.5% at $1,000 in whole contracts** (2024 +14.5%, 2025 +4.5%, 2026 −0.6%), the weakest of the seven weekdays on Delta as well (`backtest_results.md` T5, `evidence/qd_runs/T5c_delta_weekday_check.txt`). The Binance archive is nonetheless a valid price proxy: Delta and Binance same-day closes differ by a median of 0–2.5 bps with daily log-return correlations of 0.998–1.000 over 782 days, and the 00:00 UTC candle boundary is confirmed (`evidence/av_runs/av_delta.txt`). Funding is the exception: Delta charges longs more than Binance on the majors, BTC +8.92% against +4.97% a year of notional, ETH +9.15% against +4.85%, BNB +13.48% against +0.78%, with BTC/ETH sitting at the 0.01%/8 h cap 61–86% of intervals (`av_delta.txt`; `evidence/adv_ops/checks.py`), which adds about 0.2–0.5 pp a year of cost on Delta over what the Binance series shows. D3's framing of the cap as a structural advantage for longs is withdrawn.

Put together: a long/flat, vol-scaled trend rule on large-cap perps was positive after Delta-level costs in every year except 2022 on every universe tested, with half the drawdown of holding the vol-scaled basket, and no look-ahead or data-boundary error was found (the signal uses closes to day d only, execution lag of 4–24 hours costs nothing measurable at Sharpe 1.09–1.15, T3k; an archive gap of five days in XRP and LTC in 2022 changes nothing, T7 #4). The honest central forward Sharpe is about 0.5, with a range of 0.3–0.8, which is where every size, gate and expectation below is set.

## The rule, specified so another quant can run it

**Venue.** Delta Exchange India mainnet, INR-funded, USD-quoted perpetuals, FIU-IND registered per the exchange's own statements [UNVERIFIED against the register]. No foreign venue (FEMA Schedule III bars margin to overseas exchanges and AD banks refuse LRS for crypto, `evidence/research_notes/Crypto strategy for prop trading/funding_carry_basis.md` KQ5), no spot, no options, no P2P USDT.

**Universe (fixed, 9 coins).** BTCUSD, ETHUSD, XRPUSD, BNBUSD, ADAUSD, LTCUSD, LINKUSD, DOGEUSD, BCHUSD. Contract values from Delta `/v2/products` as recorded in the memo: BTC 0.001, ETH 0.01, XRP 1, BNB 0.1, ADA 1, LTC 0.1, LINK 1, DOGE 100, BCH 0.01 (`backtest_results.md` T5). This is a hindsight universe (see above); fewer coins test worse (BTC only Sharpe 0.85 with 2025 −4.5%, BTC+ETH 0.95, T3f), and no coin is ever added for performance reasons. A coin leaves only if Delta delists it or its 24-hour turnover is below $0.5M for 30 consecutive days *(start)*; BCH at roughly $1M a day is within 2x of that threshold (memo), so the set may become eight.

**Signal and sizing** (identical to `cex-mm/scripts/trend_backtest.py::target_weights`; evaluated at the decision time T on Delta daily closes up to and including the candle that closes at T, at least 121 closes per coin):

```
s_i   = mean( sign(P_T/P_{T-20} - 1), sign(P_T/P_{T-60} - 1), sign(P_T/P_{T-120} - 1) )   # in {-1, -1/3, 1/3, 1}
sig_i = max(s_i, 0)                                                                    # long/flat: {0, 1/3, 1}
vol_i = sqrt( 365 * mean_{k=0..59} ln(P_{T-k}/P_{T-k-1})^2 )                            # 60-day realized vol, annualized, floor 0.05
TV    = 0.25 (start)                                                                   # the code's TARGET_VOL; 0.40 is the memo's native setting
w_i   = sig_i * (TV / 9) / max(vol_i, 0.05)                                            # fraction of total strategy capital E (on-venue + off-venue)
if sum(w_i) > 1.0: scale all w_i by 1.0/sum(w_i)                                       # gross cap 1.0x (start); never bound, max 0.56x
target_i    = round( w_i * E / (contract_value_i * P_T) )                              # whole Delta contracts
trade_i     = target_i - held_i, but skip if |trade_i| < 1 or |trade_i * contract_value_i * P_T| < 10% of |target notional|   # no-trade band (start)
```

Lookbacks (20, 60, 120) and the 60-day vol window are not tuned: alternatives give Sharpe 0.90–1.21 with the design second of six, and vol windows 30/60/90 give 1.16/1.12/1.08 (T3d, T3e); the single 120-day lookback scores 1.21 but lost 3.1% in 2025 and choosing it would be selection. TV 0.25 leaves Sharpe unchanged across 0.15–0.40 (1.12–1.13) and scales drawdown and worst day linearly (−10.5% / −3.0% at 0.15, −16.9% / −4.9% at 0.25, −26.2% / −7.8% at 0.40, T3a); it is a drawdown-tolerance choice. The 10% band removes 86 of 278 orders a year at identical Sharpe and drawdown (T3c) and at $1,000 removes one-contract flips in BTC (~$82) and BNB (~$73) that cost 5.31 bps plus spread for no signal content. Realized vol at TV 0.25 is about 12% (the rule is flat 16% of days, T6c), average gross 0.12x, maximum 0.56x.

**Capital.** Minimum $1,000 *(start)*: at $500 BTC and BNB round to zero most weeks and average gross is 0.09x instead of 0.12x, i.e. about 75% of the intended exposure; from $1,000 rounding is noise (Sharpe 0.72 / 0.81 / 0.70 / 0.69 at $500 / $1,000 / $2,500 / $5,000 vs 0.70 unrounded on Delta, T5). When every signal is at one-third, BTC and BNB targets of $21–23 round to zero entirely, so the two largest coins drop out of the book on weak-signal weeks (`evidence/adv_ops/checks.py`). Keep **at most 30% of strategy capital on Delta** *(start)* and the rest in a bank FD or liquid fund; sizing uses total E. At the backtest's −17% max drawdown the on-venue sleeve falls by about 57% and initial-margin utilisation rises toward 86%, so the top-up must be pre-funded (a standing transfer the user can make within a day), not left to the next Monday.

**Margin and leverage.** Set **margin mode to cross** on the Delta account and set the leverage selector on every product to 5x or lower, never upward. The design never specified the mode; under isolated margin at 5x a position liquidates near −19.75% with a penalty, and on Binance hourly lows **166 of 1,532 long coin-weeks (11%) wicked at least 19.75% below the Monday 00:00 open, 52 at least 30% and 14 at least 50%** (the weeks of 2021-05-17 and 2025-10-06 had alt lows of −57% to −71%), which the backtest assumes are survived and recovered (`evidence/adv_ops/liq_check.py`; Delta marks on an index, so its lows may be less extreme [UNVERIFIED]). With cross margin and the whole on-venue balance as collateral, liquidation needs about a 53% loss of the on-venue sleeve. Check every Monday that gross notional divided by on-venue equity is **≤ 2x** (graft from D2); at 30% on venue and 0.56x maximum gross the implied figure is 1.9x, so the check binds exactly where it should and survives a 40%+ adverse move during a one-to-two-hour exchange lockout, which Binance demonstrated on 10–11 October 2025 when reduce-only orders were rejected for more than 100 minutes ([CoinEdition](https://coinedition.com/binance-system-failure-reduceonly-order-outage/) [UNVERIFIED]).

**Execution.** Decision time is the Delta daily candle that closes at **Monday 00:00 UTC (05:30 IST)**; all cached Delta candles are stamped 00:00 UTC and funding has been exchanged at 05:30/13:30/21:30 IST since 2025-09-08 ([Delta support article](https://www.delta.exchange/support/solutions/articles/80001183756-update-on-funding-exchange-schedule-for-perpetual-contracts); `evidence/adv_ops/checks.py`). Execute between 05:35 and about 07:00 IST by hand on the Delta web or app: compute the nine contract targets (a 20-line call into `target_weights` on the Delta candles, or the spreadsheet in the plan), compare with the open positions, and send the differences as **market orders** (taker 5.31 bps incl. GST with the referral tier, 5.90 without, plus 2 bps modelled slippage; 2 / 5 / 10 bps slippage gives Sharpe 1.12 / 1.11 / 1.10, T3l; BTC/ETH touch 0.06 / 0.20 bps, XRP 1.47, DOGE 3.59 bps on the 2026-10-13 L2 snapshot, `research_notes/.../risk_execution_india_regime.md` KQ2). At $1,000 that is about 131 orders a year, 2.5 a week (T5). **Late-execution rule:** if the 05:30 slot is missed, execute by 09:30 IST on the 04:00 UTC hourly close, otherwise on the next day's 00:00 UTC close; both cost nothing measurable on the weekly series (+4 h Sharpe 1.09, +24 h 1.15, T3k). Never wait for a better price. Two consecutive missed Mondays are an operational kill.

**Reconciliation.** The venue is the source of truth (CLAUDE.md). Every Monday, after the orders, reconcile the contract count per coin against the order list and record order time, fill price, fee and funding in the weekly log; the execution gate is **median fill versus the 00:00 UTC decision price worse than 2 × 7.31 bps over the last ten orders**, not an equity-divergence test, because the modelled 30-day cost of 0.19% of equity ($3.88 on $1,000) is one-tenth of a month's standard deviation and the size of rounding noise (T6d).

**Risk rules.** No intraday stop on the own account: every day-stop tested costs Sharpe once both the flatten and the next-day re-entry are charged (1.5% / 2% / 3% → 1.05 / 1.02 / 0.99 from 1.12, with 2024–26 falling to 0.62–0.76 from 0.86, T3i); the stops do buy about two points of drawdown and 1.5 points of worst day, so this is a trade-off, and the reason to take it is that a watcher is a bot and the bot is the repo's largest documented operational risk. No drawdown de-leveraging (Sharpe 1.13–1.23 vs 1.25 at every dd_max, `evidence/calc/d2_checks2_output.txt`), no regime filter (the BTC-below-200-day filter helps this weekly line at 1.18 but was neutral on the daily line and is post hoc, T3j), no shorts, no discretionary overrides. **Drawdown pause** *(start)*: if strategy equity is 20% below its peak at a Monday check, flatten with reduce-only limit orders inside Delta's 5% (majors) / 10% (alts) price band, stay flat four weeks, write one page on why, then restart at half TV for 13 weeks. It is a pause, not a kill, because at the design's own forward Sharpe it fires on luck: within two years on 11% of paths at Sharpe 0.7 and 18% at Sharpe 0.5, within three years on 21–32%, while at zero edge it fires within two years on only 45% (T6g). The level is 1.13x the backtest's hourly drawdown and would have fired in-sample at TV 0.30 (T3h). A second pause within 12 months is a stop. A flatten during a cascade may take hours and is not required for the weekly rule to survive.

**What the repo already provides and what changes.** Signal and sizing are `cex-mm/scripts/trend_backtest.py::target_weights` (read-only use); `scripts/trend_paper.py` fetches Delta closes, L2 books and FUNDING candles and sizes whole contracts off paper equity. Two small changes are required rather than optional: a `--weekly` decision flag and a print-only order-list mode, run in a second state directory at TV 0.25 so the paper gate measures the configuration that will trade (judge graft). Not used: `mm-bot`, `mm-oems`, the DISM profile, the Binance anchor, the web UI, any taker order path in the market-making bot. If the user later automates, the daily rule with the 10% band is the bot version (538 orders a year, Sharpe 1.25, hourly max DD −14.5% vs −17.7%, T1) and the thinner tails are the reason to switch; nothing in this design depends on it.

## In plain English

**What we do.** Nine of the biggest cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin, Ether, XRP, BNB, Cardano, Litecoin, Chainlink, Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash) trade on Delta Exchange India as perpetual futures, which let you hold a position with a small deposit. Every Monday at 5:30 in the morning, just after the exchange's daily candle closes, we look at each coin and ask three questions: is the price higher than it was 20 days ago, 60 days ago and 120 days ago? Three yeses means a full position, two means a third, and one or none means nothing. "Full" is small on purpose. The size is set so that a jumpy coin gets a smaller position than a calm one, and so that the whole account moves about a tenth as much as Bitcoin; on a typical day only about 12% of the money is in the market, and the most it has ever been is a bit over half. We type the handful of orders by hand, write them in a spreadsheet, check that the exchange shows exactly those positions, and do nothing until next Monday. Seventy percent of the money never touches the exchange; it sits in a bank deposit, and only the slice needed as margin is on Delta.

**Why it should make money.** Crypto prices tend to keep going the way they have been going for the last one to four months slightly more often than chance, and when they have been falling the rule holds nothing, so it sidesteps the 60–80% crashes that buy-and-hold investors sit through. That is the whole idea, and it is the only idea in this project that still made money after we charged Delta's real fees, funding payments and slippage; the market-making bot, option selling, funding harvesting and every clever signal we researched failed their own tests. Honestly, most of the rule's return is simply being long crypto in a controlled way. The trend signal adds a little return and, more importantly, halves the worst fall (about −17% instead of −37% for holding the same coins at the same size) and keeps you out in years like 2022.

**What can go wrong.** The big test numbers (about +14% a year) come mostly from 2020–21 and 2024, when crypto trended for a year; strip those out and the rule made about 2–6% a year, which is a bank deposit's return with more hassle, and the last two years have been like that. A crash while you are long costs a bad day of 3–5%, and because we only act on Mondays there is no intraday escape hatch; we accept that for simplicity and size so it is survivable. Indian tax is unsettled: if these contracts are taxed like crypto purchases (30% on each winning trade, losses not deducted), flat years become losing years after tax, so a written accountant's opinion comes before any real money. The exchange can freeze or fail, which is why most of the cash stays in the bank. The biggest risk is the person running it: skipping a Monday, doubling up after a good month, or adding a clever extra rule.

**How much to expect.** On $1,000 (about ₹88,000) a realistic year is +$40 to +$90 before tax, plus roughly $45 of deposit interest on the other $700; a good year like 2024 about +$230; a bad year like 2022 about −$70. On $5,000, five times that. That is pocket money, and it is meant to be: the first six months are a test of whether you can run the rule exactly, every week, with costs and positions matching the model. If you can, the same rule can be run daily on a "funded account" from a prop firm such as Breakout: you pay $275 for a $25,000 account, and if you make 10% without ever losing 3% in a day or 6% in total, you keep 80% of future profits. Our simulations say the rule passes that test roughly four to five times in ten at a realistic edge (six in ten on the full backtest, fewer than two in ten in a 2025-type market), typically after four to five months, and a passed $25k account is worth a few hundred dollars a year to you; the $100k account is worth low four figures. Every one of those rules comes from review websites, not the firm's own page, and must be confirmed in writing before paying. If the account ever falls 20% from its best level we stop for a month and restart at half size; if that happens twice, we stop for good and rethink.

## Limits of this research

Every web fact in this report comes from search-engine snippets. The sandbox proxy returned 403 or NXDOMAIN for every external host, so no firm rules page, regulator notice, tax circular, academic PDF or review site was opened; the only primary source reached was Delta India's public REST API, which supplied contract specs, fees, order-book depth, funding history and daily candles. The [UNVERIFIED] tag is concentrated on exactly the claims the prop leg depends on: Breakout's 00:30 UTC balance-based reset, 6% static floor, in-evaluation costs, inactivity rule, bot policy, coin list and India eligibility; HyroTrader's static-versus-trailing limits, which alone move its EV from +$2.8k to +$0.8k; the FPFX "14% pass, 7% paid" base rate, which is a vendor dataset and not a census; the cash and FD benchmarks; and the Barclay, Swinkels, Summitward and Borri et al. figures. None of the own-capital backtest numbers relies on a snippet, so the own-capital conclusions survive; the prop section is entirely conditional.

The backtest is on Binance USDT-M data as a Delta proxy. That proxy is good for prices (same-day closes within 0–2.5 bps median, return correlation 0.998–1.000, 00:00 UTC boundary confirmed) and conservative-to-neutral for Delta funding in the current regime, but Delta's funding on the majors runs 1.8–17x Binance's and the Delta-native window is only 2.2 years, of which the first five months (August–December 2024, +14.5% to +16%) carry the whole result; the Binance daily archive ends 2026-09-30, so every "2026" figure is nine months; XRP and LTC are missing five daily closes in 2022, which `target_weights` turns into a 120-day exclusion of both coins (immaterial to Sharpe, but undocumented in the memo); and today's contract values are applied to 2024–26 history. The universe is a hindsight set, the "pre-registration" is one commit containing the results with long-only chosen after seeing long/short, and the weekly cadence was chosen after results were visible, which is why expectations are set from the ex-ante universe and the design weekday rather than from the memo's headline or the weekday mean.

The simulators approximate the firms' rules. Hourly mark-to-market misses intra-hour wicks, so every "0% daily breach" is a lower bound and the intra-hour low path gives up to about one breach a year at k = 0.5; the daily limit is measured against the 00:00 UTC equity rather than Breakout's 00:30 balance excluding open positions (a balance-based variant was run and changes trips, not outcomes); the original `prop_sim.py` used the 01:00 equity, a lenient bug behind every D1 and D3 figure; the default payout rule pays after losing months, and the firm-realistic sweep rule cuts EV by about 20%; "historical" mode pairs real evaluation windows with a bootstrapped funded phase; the day-stop flatten is charged 7.31 bps on Breakout's 4 bps schedule; the pass test is on equity rather than closed balance; inactivity, consistency, the 5% payout cap and the refundable fee were absent from the original runs and are quantified only in the verification; the Breakout cost series is 1.5–2 pp a year cheaper than the Delta-cost series, so S_b EVs are the optimistic end; and the bootstrap mixes regimes with excess kurtosis of 12–15, re-samples the 638-day 2025–26 window into 365-day paths (one regime describing itself), and overlaps 1,979 historical windows that contain about 30 independent outcomes. The own-account tails are also understated for the same hourly reason, and the liquidation check uses Binance lows where Delta marks on an index.

Finally, the research cannot establish the edge after 2021 and the plan cannot either. The 2022–26 confidence interval includes zero, 26 live weeks have a Sharpe standard error near 1.8, and the only kills that can fire within the horizon are operational, tax and venue kills. What the next six months can establish is whether the user can run a fixed rule exactly, at a known cost, with positions that reconcile; that is a necessary condition for the prop route and the only thing the evidence lets anyone promise.

## Conclusion

The question was which robust, systematic crypto strategy an India-based trader with a few thousand dollars should build; the answer the evidence supports is smaller than the question. One rule, long/flat vol-scaled trend on nine Delta perps, run weekly by hand at a quarter of its native risk with most of the money in a bank deposit, is robust in the sense that matters: it was positive after Delta costs in every year but 2022 on every universe tested, it keeps the account out of the 37–80% drawdowns that end retail trading, it costs 0.25% a year in fees, and it needs no bot, which the repo's own history shows is the thing most likely to fail quietly. It is not robust in the sense the user may have hoped: after the hindsight universe and the 2020–21 regime are removed, its forward Sharpe is a coin's width above zero, its return since 2022 is a fixed deposit's, and the unfavourable tax reading makes flat years losing years. Understanding has moved from "a Sharpe-1.25 strategy that justifies a prop route" to "a cheap, controlled long-crypto exposure with a modest trend overlay, whose honest product is a six-month track record".

The implication going forward is a change of objective. The money, if any, is in renting capital at Breakout, where the same rule at half size has a 42–52% chance per attempt of turning a $275 ticket into a few hundred dollars a year and a $999 ticket into low four figures, with a third of that value owed to the account structure rather than to any edge, under rules nobody in this research has read. Everything that could make that bet rational, cross margin, resting stops, a daily routine, written firm answers, a CA opinion that covers the remittance and the payout, and a reconciled 26-week log, is cheap and takes about eight months; everything that could make it irrational, a fee bought on in-sample EVs in a 2025-type regime, is one impatient decision away. The next step is therefore not to trade harder but to commission the tax opinion, run the paper trader at the real settings, and treat the first live Monday as the start of a test the trader can actually pass.
