## KQ1. On-chain signals (exchange netflows, SOPR, MVRV, whale flows, stablecoin supply, miner flows)

### Takeaway
Post-2022 evidence that on-chain metrics predict BTC returns after costs at daily/weekly horizons is thin and mostly in-sample or cycle-timing in nature; the one recent predictive result with a clean design is that stablecoin (USDT) net inflows to exchanges predict intraday BTC/ETH returns, while BTC's own exchange netflows do not. ML studies on on-chain features that correct look-ahead bias land at ROC-AUC ~0.6, which the authors say cannot beat transaction costs.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 Elsevier-hosted study tests three rule-based on-chain signals (NUPL, MVRV Z-score, CVDD) over Dec 2013–Apr 2025 as cycle-identification tools, not return forecasts; all three "consistently outperform buy-and-hold" over that sample. It also notes that most recent on-chain work (2023–2025) is ML on many on-chain variables at short horizons. The search result did not resolve the paper's own URL; the nearest listed link is a University of Vaasa repository item — [osuva.uwasa.fi](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/bitstreams/6575b90c-6140-44e7-a798-011a1793d134/download) [UNVERIFIED, URL-to-paper mapping uncertain]
- Glassnode's STH/LTH note splits SOPR and MVRV by coin age at a 155-day threshold; LTH-SOPR "moves in much lower frequencies and shows significantly larger deviations from the 1-line". Descriptive industry note, no predictive test — [Glassnode Research](https://research.glassnode.com/sth-lth-sopr-mvrv/) [UNVERIFIED]
- arXiv working paper (IDEAS listing 2411.06327): USDT net inflows into exchanges positively predict BTC and ETH returns at multiple intraday intervals; BTC net inflows "generally lack predictive power for BTC returns (except at 4 hours)" and are negatively associated with volatility across intraday intervals — [IDEAS/RePEc 2411.06327](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2411.06327.html) [UNVERIFIED]
- arXiv 2211.08281 uses CryptoQuant whale, exchange and miner flow data with Synthesizer Transformer models to forecast BTC volatility spikes, not returns — [arXiv 2211.08281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.08281) [UNVERIFIED]
- Glassnode "Whale Watching" (week 30, 2023): whale-to-exchange netflows have oscillated within about ±5k BTC/day over five years; used as a supply/demand proxy, no predictive test reported — [Glassnode](https://insights.glassnode.com/the-week-onchain-week-30-2023) [UNVERIFIED]
- Entity labelling is unreliable: Glassnode's lead analyst ("Checkmate") is quoted that ETFs and exchanges "often masquerade as significant whale wallets" — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-exchange-outflows-mimic-2023-whales-buy-retail-btc) [UNVERIFIED, secondary]
- Stablecoin supply: CryptoQuant researchers claim stablecoin supply correlates with BTC more than global M2 does, and that in "both the 2021 bull market and the 2024–2025 recovery, rising stablecoin supply clearly preceded Bitcoin's upside" (two episodes, no statistical test) — [MEXC news syndication](https://www.mexc.com/news/205044) [UNVERIFIED]; the NY Fed finds the reverse direction, with flows into stablecoins rising after large BTC price increases — [Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/?p=34861) [UNVERIFIED]; LSEG reports stablecoin growth is correlated with US M2 and broader monetary conditions — [LSEG/FTSE Russell](https://www.lseg.com/en/ftse-russell/research/stablecoins-effective-bridge-between-fiat-and-crypto-or-a-bridge-too-far) [UNVERIFIED]; a 2021 study (reported second-hand) found Tether issuance lifts volume "without directly altering Bitcoin or Ethereum returns" — [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2224632) [UNVERIFIED]
- ML on on-chain features: an MDPI *Forecasting* paper evaluating five classifiers on 2025 holdout data found a multi-timeframe look-ahead bias that inflated ROC-AUC by about 0.20; after correction models reach 0.57–0.61, and the authors conclude models at ROC-AUC ≈ 0.60 "cannot reliably generate economically significant returns once transaction costs are accounted for" — [MDPI Forecasting 8(3):40](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-9394/8/3/40) [UNVERIFIED]
- Omole and Enke (Engineering Applications of AI, Aug 2025) test ML/DL on price, on-chain and technical features; out-of-sample results were not visible in the search result — [Missouri S&T Scholars' Mine](https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/engman_syseng_facwork/1602) [UNVERIFIED]
- A 2026 arXiv review of peer-reviewed BTC prediction work says the rigorous designs all use a single contiguous out-of-sample period and none test multiple non-overlapping holdouts across regimes — [arXiv 2606.00071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00071) [UNVERIFIED]
- Data cost reference point (repo): Kaiko L2 aggregations from $2,000/month, L2 tick from $2,500/month — [resource/04](/home/user/crypto-strategy/resource/04-open-source-tools-and-data.md) citing [kaiko.com](https://www.kaiko.com/products/l1-l2-data)

### Inferences
- SOPR/MVRV/NUPL "outperform buy-and-hold" results are cycle-timing claims over one or two full cycles (2013–2025), with a handful of effective independent observations; they say nothing about daily/weekly tradable edge after fees and are vulnerable to threshold selection. Treat as regime context at best, not a signal.
- The only on-chain quantity with a defensible intraday predictive result is stablecoin inflow to exchanges, which is closer to an order-flow signal than a "fundamental" one; its magnitude and after-cost value were not visible in the snippet, so it is a candidate for in-house replication, not adoption.
- Entity-labelled flow metrics (whale, miner, exchange) carry labelling error and vendor-specific construction, so published results do not transfer between data vendors.

### Gaps
- No replication or debunking study of SOPR/MVRV/netflow predictability from 2022–2026 was found; the search found no study at all on miner flows as a return predictor.
- Glassnode/CryptoQuant API pricing and latency for 2026 could not be retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- The magnitude (bps per unit of inflow) and cost-adjusted profitability in arXiv 2411.06327 could not be read.

## KQ2. Sentiment and social (Fear & Greed, social volume, Google Trends, news NLP)

### Takeaway
Fear & Greed and search/social attention show in-sample lead relationships at one-week to one-month horizons in several small-sample academic studies, but the most recent news-NLP study found in-sample gains that are not statistically significant out of sample, and no post-2022 study reports a cost-adjusted trading result.

### Cited Findings
- Albrecht, Pastorek and Maňoušek (Czech Journal of Economics and Finance, 2025): Crypto Fear & Greed Index with wavelet coherence on the five largest cryptocurrencies 2018–2023; sentiment acts as a leading indicator for returns "within an investment horizon of one week to one month" with notable shifts in trading volume — [journal.fsv.cuni.cz](https://journal.fsv.cuni.cz/mag/article/show/id/1548) [UNVERIFIED]
- Cavalheiro, Vieira and Thue (Review of Behavioral Finance, 2024): weekly data July 2022–June 2023, smooth quantile regression, tests whether the index predicts BTC and ETH returns; the abstract seen states the question but not the result — [Emerald RBF-08-2023-0224](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/RBF-08-2023-0224/full/html) [UNVERIFIED]
- Monthly 2016–2021 ARDL study: positive, statistically significant relation between the Bitcoin Sentiment Index (earlier name of Fear & Greed) and BTC returns; a separate daily 2018–2022 study finds rising greed (fear) raises (lowers) BTC returns — [Virtus Interpress](https://virtusinterpress.org/How-does-the-Bitcoin-Sentiment-Index-of-Fear-Greed-affect-Bitcoin-returns.html) [UNVERIFIED]
- 2025 Shahrood University thesis: extreme greed/fear levels shift volatility structure rather than returns — [shahroodut.ac.ir](https://shahroodut.ac.ir/en/thesis/thesis.php?thid=HA508) [UNVERIFIED]
- News NLP, most recent: ChatGPT-labelled Bitcoin headlines fed into AR, HAR, BMA, LASSO and SVR models "significantly improve in-sample accuracy, but not out-of-sample forecasting", which the authors read as consistent with semi-strong efficiency (found only via a university seminar page; venue and date unconfirmed) — [WNE UW seminar listing](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/en/research/conferences-and-seminars/upcoming-events/lecture-researchers-nicolaus-copernicus-university-torun) [UNVERIFIED]
- Chen, Guo and Renault (SSRN, 2019, dated): crypto-specific lexicon sentiment predicts CRIX returns with daily in-sample R² 2.74% and out-of-sample R² 3.15% — [SSRN 3398423](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3398423) [UNVERIFIED]
- Nasekin and Chen (Digital Finance, 2020, dated): sentiment from ~1.22M StockTwits messages on 425 coins, 2013–2018, informative for index returns and volatility — [Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/214034) [UNVERIFIED]
- Cross-sectional news study (Quantpedia summary): pessimism about technical news is positively priced and pessimism about fundamental news negatively priced in the cross-section — [Quantpedia](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/?p=28913) [UNVERIFIED]
- LASSO-VAR with sentiment for crypto log-returns — [arXiv 2210.00883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00883) [UNVERIFIED, results not seen]
- Google Trends: a 2024 paper targets BTC volatility, not returns — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nea/journl/y2024i64p118-135.html) [UNVERIFIED]; Figà-Talamanca and Patacca (2019, dated) find search attention affects returns and volatility with models chosen on out-of-sample fit — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:spr:decfin:v:42:y:2019:i:1:d:10.1007_s10203-019-00258-7) [UNVERIFIED]; Nasir et al. (2019, dated) find search frequency leads positive returns and a volume surge — [Huddersfield](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/forecasting-cryptocurrency-returns-and-volume-using-search-engine/) [UNVERIFIED]

### Inferences
- The Fear & Greed index is itself built partly from volatility and momentum inputs, so "sentiment predicts returns" results from it may be re-labelled momentum/volatility effects; none of the cited studies strips those components out.
- The pattern across 2019–2025 is: older, smaller-sample studies report OOS R² of ~3%/day; the newest, better-specified study reports no significant OOS gain. That is consistent with decay after publication and with better methodology removing a spurious finding; neither can be separated from the snippets.
- Weekly-to-monthly horizons with modest R² are only usable as a position-sizing tilt on a strategy that already trades at that horizon (e.g. the repo's daily trend rule), not as a stand-alone signal paying 10.6 bps round-trip taker fees.

### Gaps
- No post-2022 study with a cost-adjusted Sharpe for Fear & Greed, social volume or Google Trends was found; no study of signal decay after publication was found.
- The result of Cavalheiro et al. (2024) and the exact magnitudes in Albrecht et al. (2025) could not be read.

## KQ3. Derivatives-implied signals (variance risk premium, skew, put/call, OI, OI-weighted funding, liquidation data)

### Takeaway
The best-documented derivatives edge is the perp funding/carry premium: "Crypto Carry" (BIS/Management Science) shows carry that at times exceeds 40% p.a., and a 2025 review reports a carry Sharpe of 6.45 over 2020–2025 that fell to 4.06 from 2024 and turned negative in 2025. The Bitcoin variance risk premium is documented as positive and regime-dependent (higher in calm regimes) but no credible post-2023 backtest of a short-vol strategy with Sharpe was found; skew, put/call ratio and OI-change predictability rest on commentary only.

### Cited Findings
- "Risk Premia in the Bitcoin Market" (arXiv 2410.15195): builds risk-neutral densities from option prices, clusters them into volatility regimes; the low-volatility regime implies a relatively high Bitcoin variance risk premium (BVRP) and a high share of the premium attributable to positive returns, while high-volatility states carry a lower BVRP — [arXiv 2410.15195](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15195v2) [UNVERIFIED]; a Substack summary of what appears to be the same work gives Deribit data July 2017–Dec 2022 and VRP of 0.17 in the low-vol cluster vs 0.12 in the high-vol cluster — [Harbourfront Quant](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/return-and-variance-risk-premia-in) [UNVERIFIED, mapping to the arXiv paper unconfirmed]
- Alexander and Imeraj (Journal of Alternative Investments, 2021, dated): Bitcoin implied-volatility indices built from Deribit options Mar 2019–Mar 2020 with the CBOE VIX variance-swap formula — [Sussex SRO](https://sro.sussex.ac.uk/id/eprint/91094/) [UNVERIFIED]; related HU Berlin work on pricing kernels and risk premia in Bitcoin options — [edoc.hu-berlin.de](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/18452/27204) [UNVERIFIED]
- DVOL is Deribit's 30-day forward implied-volatility index for BTC and ETH, launched 2021 — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/linked/99971/deribit-bitcoin-volatility-index) [UNVERIFIED]; DVOL futures exist and Deribit's materials describe vol mean-reversion and momentum uses without performance numbers — [Deribit Insights](https://insights.deribit.com/industry/demystifying-dvol-futures/) [UNVERIFIED]
- Block Scholes' public strategy-backtest page lists a "Short Vol Carry" strategy at Sharpe −0.91, drawdown −18.4%, 67 trades, but the table repeats identical dates and instruments across every strategy and states no costs or sizing — [Block Scholes console](https://console.blockscholes.com/products/strategy-backtest) [UNVERIFIED, looks illustrative]; a Seeking Alpha piece is titled "DVOL: Unusual Strategy With Unconvincing Results" (title only) — [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4784455-dvol-unusual-strategy-with-unconvincing-results) [UNVERIFIED]
- Skew / put-call: only market commentary surfaced. Glassnode noted a 25-delta skew of about −10.9% on 9 Aug 2023 (puts at a discount) — [Glassnode Insights](https://insights.glassnode.com/content/navigating-bitcoin-options/amp) [UNVERIFIED]; Deribit's Luuk Strijers is quoted that a put/call ratio above 1 "suggests the market expected short-term downside" — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/274421/irischo.com) [UNVERIFIED]; an FGV-hosted academic abstract reports BTC futures-options put/call volume ratio near 0.5 vs about 2 for S&P 500 options and "an almost symmetric smile" — [FGV abstract](https://eventos.fgv.br/sites/eventos.fgv.br/files/arquivos/u1314/abstract_florent_rouxelin.pdf) [UNVERIFIED]
- Funding/carry: Schmeling, Schrimpf and Todorov, "Crypto Carry" (BIS WP 1087; Management Science): futures-spot carry "can reach exceptionally high levels, sometimes exceeding 40% per annum", attributed to demand from smaller trend-chasing traders and limited arbitrage capital under regulatory and margin frictions — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf), [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069) [UNVERIFIED]
- "Cryptocurrency as an Investable Asset Class: Coming of Age" (arXiv 2510.14435, 2025): crypto carry (short perp, long spot) annualized Sharpe 6.45 over 2020–2025, 4.06 from 2024, negative in 2025; "profit is mostly driven by the funding rate" — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435) [UNVERIFIED]
- Lu et al. (seminar abstract): a cross-sectional carry strategy earns about 43.4% annualized with Sharpe 0.74, unexplained by standard crypto factors — [ZUEL seminar page](https://dtf.zuel.edu.cn/szjsyxdjr-szjs_tzyg/szjsyxdjr_cont_news/details-40034.html) [UNVERIFIED]
- Edinburgh thesis: cross-section of expected returns on crypto perpetuals via a tailored cost-of-carry model with long-short sorts — [ERA Edinburgh](https://era.ed.ac.uk/handle/1842/43608) [UNVERIFIED]; He, Manela, Ross and von Wachter derive no-arbitrage perp prices — [arXiv 2212.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888v2) [UNVERIFIED]
- A Thai study reports funding rates are mean-reverting and ML forecasting of them is "difficult to predict profitably" — [JSTKU](https://ph03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JSTKU/article/view/4724) [UNVERIFIED]
- Oldest funding study (2019, dated): BitMEX funding Granger-causes the inverse perp price; GARCH forecasts — [arXiv 1912.03270](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1912.03270) [UNVERIFIED]
- "OI-weighted funding" is a data construction (Coinalyze's aggregated predicted funding rate), not a tested predictor — [Coinalyze](https://coinalyze.net/blog/aggregated-predicted-funding-rate/) [UNVERIFIED]
- Repo context: funding was negative about 35% of Q2 2026 across six venues, clustering around sell-offs — [resource/05](/home/user/crypto-strategy/resource/05-risk-hedging-pnl-failure-modes.md) citing [Bitsgap](https://bitsgap.com/blog/what-a-spot-hedge-on-perpetuals-really-cost-in-2026); Binance USDⓈ-M funding settles every 8 h or 4 h and switches to 1 h when capped — [resource/02](/home/user/crypto-strategy/resource/02-practitioner-articles.md) citing [Binance announcement](https://www.binance.info/en/support/announcement/detail/e4445d0389ce4defa6009021fcf6ee46)
- Post-10 Oct 2025, market-maker funding-arb yields "fell below ~4%" and MMs were slow to return — [resource/05](/home/user/crypto-strategy/resource/05-risk-hedging-pnl-failure-modes.md) citing [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained)

### Inferences
- Carry/funding harvesting is the one derivatives signal with both a top-journal paper and a documented, dated decay (Sharpe 6.45 → 4.06 → negative in 2025). It needs two legs (spot or a second venue); for a Delta-India-only retail account, a single-venue version would be a directional bet that funding persists, which the 2025 result argues against. The cross-sectional version (long low-funding, short high-funding perps) is in principle feasible on one venue but at taker fees of 5.31 bps per leg and funding intervals of 8 h, a 43% gross annual carry [UNVERIFIED] translates to about 0.04% per 8 h; one round trip costs 10.6 bps + spread, so positions must be held for many funding intervals, which re-exposes the trade to the basis moves that the carry paper itself attributes to trend-chasing flow. [SYNTHETIC: 43.4%/yr ÷ 1,095 funding periods ≈ 4 bps per 8 h]
- The BVRP evidence is a pricing fact (implied > realized on average, more so in calm regimes), not a tradable strategy: the only public "backtest" found is a vendor page with a negative Sharpe and inconsistent data. Selling BTC variance is a short-tail trade; the Oct 2025 cascade is the obvious counterexample scenario.
- Skew and put/call: no evidence of return predictability at all beyond analyst quotes.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of DVOL or skew predicting BTC returns in 2023–2026 was found; no OI-change predictability study was found (search budget exhausted before a targeted query).
- Whether Delta Exchange India lists options usable for a retail VRP trade, and their fees/liquidity, was not checked (egress blocked to delta.exchange).

## KQ4. Liquidation cascades and mean reversion after forced-flow events

### Takeaway
The Oct 10–11 2025 cascade is well documented in size and mechanics (about $9.9–19B liquidated, 70% within 40 minutes, top-of-book depth down >90%), but no study quantifying a tradable post-cascade rebound was found; the two academic papers on cascades find them subcritical and conclude that no early-warning variable is event-invariant, and practitioner accounts say the Oct 2025 event was the start of a sustained drawdown, not a V-shaped reversal.

### Cited Findings
- arXiv 2608.03616: measures the branching ratio of the Oct 2025 crash from the on-chain fill log of a fully transparent venue; "it ran deeply subcritical"; three estimators agree on subcriticality but not the level — [IDEAS/RePEc 2608.03616](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2608.03616.html) [UNVERIFIED]
- arXiv 2607.27070: tests rolling variance and lag-1 autocorrelation as early-warning signals across seven BTC cascades and concludes "no variable is event-invariant" — [IDEAS/RePEc 2607.27070](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2607.27070.html) [UNVERIFIED]
- Lincoln University (NZ) JFDA paper on Hyperliquid's public L2 archive studies how spreads widened and recovered after the Oct 2025 crash and whether recovery was symmetric; results not visible — [journals.lincoln.ac.nz](https://journals.lincoln.ac.nz/index.php/JFDA/article/download/1395/965/3881) [UNVERIFIED]
- Amberdata: $9.89B in leveraged positions erased within 14 hours, 70% of losses in 40 minutes; "$3.21B vanished in 60 seconds" — [Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/how-3.21b-vanished-in-60-seconds-october-2025-crypto-crash-explained-through-7-charts) [UNVERIFIED]; after the flush the 30-day annualized basis fell from 6.63% to 4.47% while stablecoin supply rose — [Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/the-leverage-purge-how-8.55b-in-liquidations-reset-the-market) [UNVERIFIED]
- Other tallies put liquidations above $19B in about a day, roughly nine times a typical episode; BTC fell ~14%, SOL >40% intraday, Worldcoin −70% before partial rebounds — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/october-10-crypto-crash-explained), [Insights4VC](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/inside-the-19b-flash-crash?open=false) [UNVERIFIED]
- FTI Consulting: BTC top-of-book depth shrank by more than 90% on key venues; forced liquidations and auto-deleveraging turned the sell-off into a margin-driven spiral; it was "the start of a broader crypto sell-off that has continued into December" — [FTI Consulting](https://fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity) [UNVERIFIED]
- RockawayX: "the October crash did not resolve into a V-shaped recovery. Instead, it marked the beginning of a sustained drawdown" — [RockawayX Q4 2025](https://rockawayx.com/insights/quarterly-insights-q4-2025) [UNVERIFIED]
- Repo-collected facts on the same event: 10-bp BTC depth fell from $48M to $26M in 48 h and was still ~40% below peak three months later; Binance perp OI fell ~50% on average; Binance reduce-only rejections (`-1008`, `-4118`, HTTP 503) reported for >100 minutes; Wintermute could buy on Binance and sell elsewhere but could not withdraw to rebalance, so quoting was "impossible, not unwilling"; Binance's post-mortem cites MM automated risk controls pulling liquidity and ~75% of liquidations occurring before the USDe depeg — [resource/05](/home/user/crypto-strategy/resource/05-risk-hedging-pnl-failure-modes.md) citing [Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/the-liquidity-that-vanished-inside-octobers-40-depth-collapse), [Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5206861), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/boazsobrado/2025/10/21/locked-out-and-liquidated-traders-blame-binance-for-19-billion-crash/), [The Block podcast](https://www.theblock.co/podcasts/big-brain/2025-10-21-breaking-down-cryptos-biggest-ever-liquidation-event-with-wintermute-founder-evgeny-gaevoy-375483), [Bankless Times](https://www.banklesstimes.com/articles/2026/01/31/october-10-crypto-crash-binance-releases-post-mortem-findings/)
- Generic short-term reversal (JIFMIM 2021, dated): daily reversals are strongest among illiquid coins and attributed to illiquidity; the largest, most tradeable coins show daily momentum instead — [Medeniyet repository](https://earsiv.medeniyet.edu.tr/items/23a96c9f-3952-4bdf-aec5-abafb3aaab70/full) [UNVERIFIED]
- Real-time detection feeds named in the repo: Bybit `allLiquidation`, Binance `forceOrder`, perp-spot basis change, funding prediction, cross-venue mid dispersion, own 10 s markouts; Amberdata's observation that on 10 Oct 2025 "liquidity collapsed before price did" — [resource/05](/home/user/crypto-strategy/resource/05-risk-hedging-pnl-failure-modes.md)
- Analyst commentary only: CryptoQuant's Amr Taha argues long/short liquidation ratios foreshadow reversals — [The Informer Post](https://theinformerpost.beehiiv.com/p/bitcoins-liquidation-data-signals-a-possible-trend-reversal-heres-why) [UNVERIFIED]; Cointelegraph describes liquidation "maps" as clusters that accelerate moves — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-liquidation-data-point-to-absurd-potential-rally-to-dollar100k-analyst) [UNVERIFIED]

### Inferences
- The literature found is about cascade mechanics and detection, not about a rebound premium. The one systematic reversal result (illiquid coins revert daily, large coins do not) argues against a BTC/ETH fade-the-cascade rule and for caution on thin alts, where the reversal exists but execution during the event is the problem.
- The operational record (reduce-only rejections, depth −90%, cross-venue basis 7× normal in an earlier event per [econstor](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/266345/1/1822049504.pdf) cited in resource/05) means a retail taker cannot assume fills at displayed prices during a cascade; any "buy the forced flow" backtest on candle data will overstate capture.
- A defensive use is well supported by evidence: Amberdata's "liquidity collapsed before price did" and the Binance post-mortem both suggest depth and liquidation-feed rate are leading indicators for pulling quotes, which is the only cascade signal the repo already plans to use.

### Gaps
- No quantified post-cascade rebound size/persistence (bps over minutes/hours after a liquidation spike) with costs was found; no Coinglass/Hyperliquid liquidation-feed pricing or latency figures were retrieved.
- The Lincoln/Hyperliquid paper's recovery numbers could not be read.

## KQ5. Event-driven (ETF flows, FOMC/CPI, token unlocks, listings/delistings, index rebalances)

### Takeaway
Token unlocks (Keyrock, 16,000+ events: ~90% negative, priced in from ~30 days ahead, team unlocks ~−25%) and exchange listings (positive day-0 abnormal returns, larger on Coinbase than Binance, but 2025 Upbit cohorts show first-day buyers losing ~70%) have the largest documented effect sizes; ETF flows explain a large share of contemporaneous BTC variance but the evidence is contemporaneous, not predictive; FOMC/CPI effects are concentrated inside one hour and are mixed or null in academic event studies.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Unlocks are the most tradable event for a small account because the direction is known 30 days ahead and the documented effect (−16% median vs BTC over a month [UNVERIFIED]) dwarfs a 10.6 bps taker round trip; the constraints are whether the token has a liquid perp on the trader's venue, funding paid on the short, and borrow/liquidation risk on thin alts (see KQ7). Delta India's alt perp list and depth decide feasibility, which was not checked.
- Listings: the capturable part is the announcement-to-listing window, which requires being able to buy before the listing venue opens trading; the 2025 Korean cohorts show that buying at or after day-0 close loses heavily. A retail trader on a venue that lists after Binance/Upbit is structurally the exit liquidity.
- ETF flows are published after the US close and the strong numbers are contemporaneous betas; none of the sources shows lagged flows predicting next-day returns, so the signal is useful as a regime/flow descriptor, not an entry trigger.
- FOMC/CPI: with the effect inside one hour and academic null results at the event window, a retail taker paying 5.31 bps per side on a venue that lags Binance by 2–3 s (KQ6) is on the wrong side of the latency ladder.

## KQ6. Lead-lag and latency (Binance leading other venues, BTC leading alts, Coinbase premium) and whether a taker on a lagging venue can beat 5.31 bps

### Takeaway
Binance's lead over other venues is well established at seconds-to-sub-second scale (repo: a >3 bps Binance move over 2 s is followed by Delta at slope 0.98 with 77% directional agreement within 5 s; cross-venue leads of ~100–700 ms to perp DEXs; Binance-OKX unresolvable at 1 s), but the lead is a pick-off risk for a maker, not a taker edge: [SYNTHETIC] a taker round trip on Delta costs 10.6 bps in fees plus the spread, while the conditional follow-through is the size of the Binance move (typically 3–5 bps), and the literature finds 88–91% of cross-exchange price gaps sit inside the no-trade region after costs.

### Inferences
- [SYNTHETIC] Taker-on-Delta arithmetic. Entering and exiting as taker costs 2 × 5.31 = 10.62 bps in fees plus the full touch spread (0.06–0.18 bps on BTC/ETH; ~12 bps on ADA) plus slippage. Exiting as maker instead costs 5.31 + 2.12 = 7.43 bps plus the adverse selection the repo measures at −1 to −2.8 bps per maker fill, so roughly 9–10 bps either way. The repo's conditional signal (Binance >3 bps in 2 s) fires ~23 times per hour (1,177 / 50.6 h) and Delta then moves about one-for-one with the Binance move; the expected follow-through is therefore the size of the triggering move, typically 3–5 bps, i.e. about half the cost. Only Binance moves above ~10–12 bps within seconds would clear costs, and those are the moments when other takers are already sweeping the Delta book (the live memo shows full-size fills, i.e. informed sweeps, carrying −2.77 bps markout), so the displayed Delta price is not available to a 250 ms REST poller on a 1 s-skewed clock.
- The evidence therefore supports the lead as a defensive input (anchor fair to Binance, pull the exposed side), which is what the Binance-anchored counterfactual shows (exposure 16–21% → ~1%), and does not support a stand-alone taker strategy on Delta at retail fees.
- The literature's "Binance leads at lower frequencies, not integrated at high frequency" plus "no permanently leading exchange" means any fixed lag constant will drift; the repo's own 3 s/0.8 lead term already went stale (fair lag 2–3 bps median). Rolling per-pair estimation is the only robust form.
- Coinbase premium and BTC→alt minute-scale leads have no post-2022 tradable evidence; the 57 s BTC→ADA lead was measured in 2019–2021 and would need re-measurement on today's Delta ADA book, where the touch is 12 bps.

- No measurement of Delta India's lag to Binance for BTC/ETH on mainnet with real orders exists in the repo (the 50.6 h test was on demo ETH; the live test was ADA).

## KQ7. Cross-sectional alt strategies vs BTC (relative strength, beta-neutral baskets, altseason regime) and small-venue costs

### Inferences
- For a small Delta-India account the ordering is: keep the trend rule and the defensive Binance anchor (both have repo-measured, after-cost numbers); consider unlock shorts only after checking which unlock tokens have Delta perps with enough depth; treat carry as a monitored opportunity rather than a strategy given the 2025 collapse; ignore on-chain, sentiment, skew, Coinbase premium and altseason signals until an in-house out-of-sample test exists.
- A recurring pattern across sources is that the credible numbers are from pre-registered or venue-specific tests (repo trend memo, BIS carry), while the large headline effects (listings +41%, "90% of unlocks negative") come from vendors or small samples; the report should weight accordingly.
