## Key question 1: Pass rates, funded-trader survival and payout rates (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
No major firm publishes an audited pass rate except HyroTrader (crypto), whose dashboard is reported at 13.37% (1-step) and 8.86% (2-step); the only cross-firm dataset (FPFX Tech, Sept 2024, 300k accounts) found 14% of traders pass, 45% of those ever get a payout, so 7% of all entrants are ever paid, with an average payout of 4% of plan size. ### Inferences
- Combining FPFX (7% of entrants paid, average payout 4% of plan) with a typical $100k plan gives an expected payout of roughly $280 per entrant per challenge ($100,000 x 4% x 7%), which is below most $100k challenge fees (see Q6). The average entrant is therefore paying for the lottery; only traders with a pass probability several times the base rate have positive EV.
- The two-phase structure explains the gap between phase-1 (8-15%) and end-to-end (<10%) estimates: end-to-end pass is the product of the two phase probabilities, and phase 2 is easier only because the target is smaller.
- HyroTrader's 1-step rate (13.4%) being higher than its 2-step rate (8.9%) is consistent with the product-of-phases effect even though 1-step products usually carry tighter drawdown.

### Gaps
- No audited pass rate from FTMO, FundedNext, Breakout or Crypto Fund Trader. HyroTrader's dashboard values could not be read at source.
- No funded-trader survival curve (time from funding to breach) from any firm; FPFX gives only "45% of funded get at least one payout".
- No third-party academic study of pass rates was found; the Belgian FSMA/CONSOB notices (Q8) may contain data but were not opened.

## Key question 2: Breach causes (daily DD vs max DD vs time vs rule violations)

### Takeaway
Every dataset that reports causes puts the daily loss limit first (OneFunded: 78.7% of failed evaluations; hoc-trade 500k accounts: ~70% loss-limit breaches, mostly in the first week), but the figures come from single firms or vendors, and one aggregator's "firm-published" column puts daily limits at 26% with time/minimum-day rules at 46%, so the mix depends heavily on which rules a firm has.

### Cited Findings
- OneFunded (single firm): 78.7% of failed evaluations ended on a daily loss limit breach, 15.0% on an overall (maximum) loss breach, 6.3% on inactivity and other risk rules. Sample size and period not given in the snippet. — [OneFunded](https://onefunded.com/blog/trading-psychology/why-traders-fail-prop-firm-challenges/)
- hoc-trade analysed "500,000+ accounts"; its headline claim is that most failures "happen in the first week, on a loss-limit breach, before traders get near the profit target", and are behavioural rather than strategic. — [hoc-trade](https://hoc-trade.com/blogs/trading-psychology/prop-firm-challenge-why-fail). Velotrade's 2026 transparency report attributes "around 70% of failures were loss-limit breaches" to the hoc-trade study (second-hand). — [Velotrade report](https://velotrade.com/reports/prop-firm-transparency)
- A widely copied "71% of first-phase failures are daily drawdown breaches" figure appears on several blogs with no traceable origin and may not be independent of the FPFX dataset. — [Cleo.finance blog](https://blog.cleo.finance/biggest-reasons-traders-breached-fail-prop-firm-challenges/) [UNVERIFIED]
- PropFirmBacktester lists two breakdowns: "estimates" of ~45% daily loss limit and ~35% trailing/max drawdown, versus "firm-published reasons" of 26% daily loss limit and 46% time/minimum days. The categories and sources clearly differ from the 71-79% figures. — [PropFirmBacktester statistics](https://www.propfirmbacktester.com/prop-firm-statistics) [UNVERIFIED which firms published]
- Daily limits are typically 4-5% of starting capital; FTMO uses 5% daily on 2-phase and 3% on 1-phase accounts. Equity-based measurement means "even if you never close any positions your max equity may keep growing, so a profitable day can still trigger a breach" when the limit trails the day's equity high. — [TradeClaris daily DD explainer](https://tradeclaris.com/blogs/daily-drawdown-rules-explained); [TradeClaris challenge overview](https://www.tradeclaris.com/blogs/how-prop-firm-challenges-work-rules-phases-and-why-80-fail)
- Some firms pause trading on a daily-limit hit rather than failing the account, while a max-drawdown hit ends the account; rules differ by firm. — [ProLens guide](https://proplens.trading/learn/how-to-pass-a-prop-firm-challenge/) [UNVERIFIED attribution]

### Inferences
- The daily limit dominates because it is the tightest constraint per unit time: a 5% daily limit on 2% daily vol is a 2.5-sigma event every day (see Q3 table F), whereas a 10% static max DD is reached only by a sustained run. The [SYNTHETIC] Monte Carlo in Q3 reproduces the ordering: at daily vol >= 2-3% the daily limit accounts for 40-80% of all failures, max DD for 2-25%, and time-outs for the rest.
- The 26%/46% "firm-published" split is plausible for firms with short time limits and minimum-day rules; the 70-79% split is plausible for firms with no time limit (FTMO-style). The cause mix is a function of rule design, not of traders alone.

### Gaps
- No firm publishes a breach-cause table with sample sizes; none of the crypto firms (Breakout, HyroTrader, CFT) publish one.
- Share of failures from rule violations (news trading, copy trading, prohibited strategies, IP/multi-account) is not reported anywhere found; OneFunded's 6.3% "inactivity and other" is the only hint.
- Community (Reddit r/propfirms, Forex Factory) breach surveys were not retrieved before the search budget ran out.

## Key question 3: The mathematics (probability of +T before -D, daily limit, optimal volatility for a given Sharpe)

### Takeaway
With drift mu and vol sigma per day the no-time-limit pass probability is P = (1 - e^(-2 mu D / sigma^2)) / (1 - e^(-2 mu (T+D) / sigma^2)), which depends only on daily Sharpe and the barrier distances in vol units; it is monotonically decreasing in leverage (lower vol always helps if time is unlimited), bounded below by D/(T+D) at infinite leverage, and a time limit creates an interior optimum where daily vol is just large enough to reach T in the allowed days while staying 3+ sigma inside the daily limit. Published treatments (Delphic Alpha) reach the same conclusion: an 8% target / 5% drawdown challenge needs Sharpe >= 1.6 and "realistically about 3.0".

### Cited Findings
- Two-barrier exit probability for X_t = mu t + sigma W_t from 0, upper barrier a, lower barrier -b, via the exponential martingale e^(lambda X_t) with lambda = -2 mu / sigma^2 and optional stopping: P(hit upper first) = (1 - e^(2 mu b / sigma^2)) / (e^(-2 mu a / sigma^2) - e^(2 mu b / sigma^2)). — [QuantVault problem 416](https://quantvault.org/problem-416-first-passage-probabilities-and-optimal-barriers-for-brownia.html); one-sided case P(ever reach -b) = e^(-2 mu b / sigma^2) for mu > 0 — [QuantVault problem 3677](https://quantvault.org/problem-3677-brownian-motion-with-unit-drift-probability-of-ever-reaching.html). Discrete gambler's ruin background: [Columbia FE notes](http://www.columbia.edu/~ks20/FE-Notes/4700-07-Notes-GR.pdf), [LibreTexts 12.2](https://stats.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Probability_Theory/Introductory_Probability_(Grinstead_and_Snell)/12:_Random_Walks/12.02:_Gambler's_Ruin).
- Delphic Alpha, "Prop Firm Math": models the challenge as a race between target and drawdown with daily P&L = drift + noise; closed-form pass probability; when target equals drawdown the result "reduces to a logistic function of an 'edge-to-noise' ratio that scales with Sharpe"; defines theta from strategy quality, target distance and daily volatility; gives pass-probability tables at 10% target / 10% drawdown with daily sigma 1.5%; notes a 40% win rate with 3:1 payoff has the same Sharpe as 70% win rate with 0.8:1; states an 8% target / 5% drawdown challenge "needs a Sharpe of at least 1.6, and realistically about 3.0"; argues consistency rules can fail a strategy that is profitable overall and that two-phase challenges compound the penalty. — [Delphic Alpha](https://delphicalpha.substack.com/p/prop-firm-math-what-the-rules-actually). Companion post lists break-even Sharpe of about 1.1 to 1.5 depending on the firm. — [Delphic Alpha, Prop Firms Decoded](https://delphicalpha.substack.com/p/prop-firms-decoded-the-complete-numbers)
- Moontower (Kris Abdelmessih) poses the problem for Sharpe 1.5, 15% vol, 20% profit target and 2% daily loss limit, treats a bust as a reset to zero, and says the exact solution "requires solving partial differential equations and tracking fees across attempts"; no final number in the excerpt. — [Moontower](https://moontower.substack.com/p/vol-orders-and-discussion-on-option)
- Copilink's survival calculator uses daily P&L mean and standard deviation as inputs with a simplified approximation. — [Copilink](https://copilink.com/articles/prop-firm-challenge-survival-calculator-probability-math)
- Busseti, Ryu and Boyd, "Risk-Constrained Kelly Gambling" (arXiv 1603.06183, Journal of Investing 25(3):118-134, Fall 2016): adds a drawdown-probability constraint to Kelly betting; the bounded version is a convex problem that guarantees the risk limit; Monte Carlo shows the bound is reasonably tight; a single risk-aversion parameter trades growth against drawdown risk; the resulting bets beat fractional Kelly at the same drawdown risk; a quadratic approximation connects to Markowitz. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.06183); [Stanford page](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/kelly.html); [QuantInsti summary](https://blog.quantinsti.com/risk-constrained-kelly-criterion/). The constraint form (recalled, not re-read in this session [UNVERIFIED]): P(W_min < alpha) <= beta is implied by E[(r^T b)^(-lambda)] <= 1 with lambda = log(beta)/log(alpha).
- Grossman and Zhou (1993), "Optimal Investment Strategies for Controlling Drawdowns", Mathematical Finance 3(3):241-276: constraint W_t >= alpha M_t (M = running max); with CRRA utility the optimal risky allocation is proportional to the surplus W_t - alpha M_t. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/mathfi/v3y1993i3p241-276.html); generalised with explicit value functions by Cherny and Obloj — [arXiv 1110.6289](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.6289); a 2005 note shows the strategy loses its optimal long-run growth property in discrete time. — [Lund University](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/37b108be-d2da-4ec8-a331-1b0c7cb46310)
- Lopez de Prado: time under water (TuW) at confidence 1-alpha is the period an investment may stay below its target NAV at that confidence — [Lopez de Prado and Peijan 2005](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/fin/papers/0503/0503010.pdf); Bailey and Lopez de Prado's "triple penance" rule: expected maximum time under water is about three times the time to reach maximum drawdown under IID normal returns, regardless of Sharpe; breaks down with autocorrelation. — [Opalesque](https://www.opalesque.com/new-managers/202/peter-urbani-statistics-ignoring-serial.html); a code fragment gives max TuW of the form (Z_alpha sigma / mu)^2. — [R-forge commit](https://lists.r-forge.r-project.org/pipermail/returnanalytics-commits/2013-July/000745.html) [UNVERIFIED against the 2013 paper]
- Free Monte Carlo / EV tools exist (inputs: win rate, R:R, risk per trade, or daily mean/sd; 10,000-path simulations). — [PropFirmsData EV calculator](https://www.propfirmsdata.com/tools/ev-calculator/); [TradeLens](https://www.tradelens.vip/tools/prop-firm-calculator); [GodLove University simulator](https://godloveuniversity.com/prop-firm-challenge-simulator/); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/mcp/prop-firm-sim/ftmo-100k-2step/)

### Derived formulas and worked numbers [SYNTHETIC unless cited]

1. No time limit, no daily limit (closed form, consistent with the QuantVault derivation above). Start at 0, target +T, static drawdown -D, daily drift mu, daily vol sigma, k = 2 mu / sigma^2:
   P(pass) = (1 - e^(-kD)) / (1 - e^(-k(T+D))).
   Writing s = mu/sigma (daily Sharpe) gives kD = 2 s (D/sigma): only the Sharpe and the barrier distances in vol units matter. Scaling position size by L multiplies mu by L and sigma by L, so k scales as 1/L: pass probability falls monotonically with leverage, tends to 1 as L -> 0 (but takes forever) and to D/(T+D) as L -> inf. D/(T+D) is also the zero-edge pass probability at any leverage: 55.6% for T=8%/D=10%, 38.5% for T=8%/D=5%, 37.5% for T=10%/D=6%. This is why firms need daily limits, consistency rules, time limits and fees: a pure martingale trader would pass a 10/10 challenge half the time.

   Table A (sigma_daily = 1%, annual Sharpe S on 365 days, mu = S/sqrt(365) x 1%):
   | S | T8/D5 | T10/D10 | T8/D10 | T10/D6 | T6/D6 |
   | 0.5 | 0.466 | 0.628 | 0.668 | 0.475 | 0.578 |
   | 1.0 | 0.548 | 0.740 | 0.765 | 0.574 | 0.652 |
   | 1.5 | 0.625 | 0.828 | 0.842 | 0.664 | 0.720 |
   | 2.0 | 0.695 | 0.890 | 0.897 | 0.741 | 0.778 |
   | 3.0 | 0.806 | 0.959 | 0.960 | 0.854 | 0.868 |

   Table B (leverage, S=1.5, base sigma 1%, T=8%, D=10%): L=0.25 -> p=0.998 (about 406 days expected); L=0.5 -> 0.960 (185 d); L=1 -> 0.842 (66 d); L=2 -> 0.719 (19 d); L=3 -> 0.668 (9 d); L=5 -> 0.624 (3 d); L=10 -> 0.590 (1 d); limit 0.556.

2. Daily loss limit d. If daily P&L ~ N(mu, sigma_d^2) and the limit is on the closing balance, per-day breach probability is Phi((-d - mu)/sigma_d); if it is on intraday equity (the usual rule) the reflection principle roughly doubles it: about 2 Phi(-d/sigma_d). Table F: sigma_d 1%, d 3%: 0.13%/day close vs 0.27%/day intraday, P(no breach in 30 days) 0.96 vs 0.92; sigma_d 1.5%, d 3%: 2.3% vs 4.6%/day, 30-day survival 0.50 vs 0.25; sigma_d 2%, d 5%: 0.6% vs 1.2%/day, 30-day survival 0.83 vs 0.69; sigma_d 2.5%, d 5%: 2.3% vs 4.6%/day, survival 0.50 vs 0.25; sigma_d 3%, d 5%: 4.8% vs 9.6%/day, survival 0.23 vs 0.05. Rule: keep daily vol at or below d/3 (d/2.5 at the very most); crypto fat tails push the real numbers up further.

   Reading: with a 30-60 day limit the optimum is around sigma_d = 2% (about 40% of the 5% daily limit), and above that the daily limit becomes the dominant failure (40-55% of all paths). With no time limit the optimum moves to sigma_d <= 1%. Even a Sharpe-3 strategy at the optimum passes only 70-78% of the time within 30-60 days.

   Tight 1-step (Breakout-like: T=10%, D=6% static, d=3%): S=2, N=60: L0.5 0.06 (time-out .91); L1 0.43 (.06, .18, .33); L1.5 0.38 (.47, .13, .02); L2 0.26 (.68, .06, 0). S=3, N=60: L1 0.58; L1.5 0.48; L2 0.30. S=2, no limit: L0.5 0.87; L1 0.69. The 3% daily limit caps usable daily vol at about 1%; at 1.5% the daily limit alone kills 41-52% of paths.

4. Rule of thumb for the Sharpe a time-limited challenge demands. To reach T in N days you need mu_d >= T/N; to stay z sigma inside the daily limit you need sigma_d <= d/z; so daily Sharpe s >= zT/(Nd) and annual S >= sqrt(365) zT/(Nd). With z=3: T8/d5: N=30 -> S >= 3.1, N=60 -> 1.5, N=90 -> 1.0; T10/d3: N=30 -> 6.4, N=60 -> 3.2, N=90 -> 2.1; T10/d5: N=30 -> 3.8, N=60 -> 1.9; T6/d4: N=30 -> 2.9, N=60 -> 1.4. This matches Delphic Alpha's cited "1.6 at least, realistically 3.0" for an 8%/5% product.

5. Per-trade (discrete) version, T=8%, D=10%, no daily/time limit, risk r per trade: win rate 55% at 1:1 (edge 0.10R, per-trade Sharpe 0.10): r=0.5% -> P=0.983, mean 152 trades to pass (152 days at one trade a day, 51 at three); r=1% -> 0.913, 61 trades; r=2% -> 0.761, 17 trades. 40% at 3:1 (edge 0.6R, Sharpe 0.31): r=0.5% -> 0.999, 28 trades; r=1% -> 0.982, 15; r=2% -> 0.860, 7. 70% at 0.8:1: r=1% -> 0.999, 32 trades. 52% at 1:1 (edge 0.04R): r=0.5% -> 0.846, 250 trades; r=1% -> 0.752, 76; r=2% -> 0.646, 18. Reading: a one-trade-a-day strategy with a thin 1:1 edge cannot pass a 30-day limit at any risk level without pushing ruin probability above 25%; it needs either a larger per-trade edge or 3+ trades a day.

6. Consecutive losses vs the daily limit (55%/1:1, d=5%): r=1% (5 losses needed): 5 trades/day -> 1.9%/day breach, 30-day survival 0.56; r=1.67% or 2% (3 losses): 3 trades/day -> 9.2%/day, 30-day survival 0.06; 5 trades/day -> 16%/day, survival 0.005; r=2.5% (2 losses): 3 trades/day -> 21%/day. So the "risk one-third of the daily limit per trade" heuristic (Q4) is only safe if you also stop after two losses or take at most two trades a day.

7. Consistency rule (best day <= 40% of total profit at pass), T=8%, D=10%, d=5%, N=60, S=2: sigma_d 1%: pass 0.57 -> 0.55 (2% consistency fails); sigma_d 2%: 0.70 -> 0.18 (52% fail the rule); 3%: 0.53 -> 0.05; 4%: 0.47 -> 0.02. The rule therefore removes the time-limit optimum and forces daily vol down to about 1% (best day must stay below 0.4 x 8% = 3.2%), i.e. it converts a 60-day product into a de facto 100+ day product for anything but very high Sharpe.

8. Fractional-Kelly lifetime breach probability (lognormal wealth, fraction f of full Kelly, drawdown to fraction alpha of the peak, no time limit): P = alpha^(2/f - 1). alpha=0.90 (10% trailing DD): f=1 -> 0.90; 0.5 -> 0.73; 0.25 -> 0.48; 0.10 -> 0.135; 0.05 -> 0.016. alpha=0.94 (6% DD): f=0.25 -> 0.65; 0.10 -> 0.31; 0.05 -> 0.09. To keep lifetime breach <= 10% you need f <= 0.088 of Kelly on a 10% DD and f <= 0.052 on a 6% DD. Consistent with Grossman-Zhou: at the account's start the surplus W - alpha M is 10% (or 6%) of wealth, so the drawdown-optimal policy bets about 10% (6%) of the unconstrained Merton/Kelly fraction.

### Inferences
- Without a time limit, "trade as small as the minimum-days rule allows" is the dominant strategy; with a 30-60 day limit, size so that daily vol is roughly 35-40% of the daily limit (2% on a 5% limit; 1% on a 3% limit) and accept 20-35% failure even with a Sharpe-2 to 3 strategy.
- Consistency rules and 1-step tight-drawdown products are, in expectation, equivalent to a much lower allowed vol; a low-frequency strategy (one trade a day) with a modest edge should avoid them or budget 3-6 months.
- The per-attempt pass probability for a genuine Sharpe-2 strategy sized correctly (55-70%) is 4-8x the FPFX base rate (14%); this is the whole source of positive fee EV (Q6).

## Key question 4: Practical sizing rules reported by practitioners

## Key question 5: Time limits, consistency rules and low-frequency strategies

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- [SYNTHETIC] From Q3 item 5: a one-trade-a-day 55%/1:1 strategy at 1% risk needs about 61 trades on average (91% pass if unlimited time); at 0.5% risk 152 trades (98%). Under a 30-day limit it must either take 2-5 trades a day or run 2% risk (76% pass, 17 trades) which then collides with a 3-5% daily limit after 2-3 losses. The frequency needed to pass a 30-day product with a realistic edge is therefore roughly 3+ independent trades a day at 1% risk, or an annualised Sharpe >= 3 at the daily level (Q3 item 4).
- Under a 40% consistency rule the best single day must be below 0.4T, so with T = 8-10% any day above 3-4% fails the rule; for a market-making bot with smooth P&L this is nearly free (2% consistency failures in the simulation at 1% daily vol), while for a trend/breakout strategy it is the binding constraint.
- The rational product choice for a low-frequency or smooth-P&L bot is a no-time-limit, static-drawdown 2-step product; the rational choice for a high-Sharpe intraday bot is a 1-step with a static 6% drawdown only if daily vol can be held near 1%.

### Worked EV example [SYNTHETIC, inputs cited above]
- Fee F = $600 ($100k crypto 2-step). Population inputs: p_pass = 10%, P(payout | funded) = 45%, E[payout] = $4,000 (4% of plan). EV = 0.10 x 0.45 x 4,000 - 600 = -$420. Break-even p_pass = 600 / (0.45 x 4,000) = 33%.
- Expected fee spend per funded account: p=10% -> $6,000; 20% -> $3,000; 33% -> $1,818; 50% -> $1,200. With fee refund on first payout the fee is recovered in 45% of funded cases, raising EV by 0.10 x 0.45 x 600 = +$27 at base rates (small) but by +$180 for a trader with p_pass = 60% and P(payout) = 50%.
- For a sized Sharpe-2 strategy (p_pass about 60% from Q3 table C, N=60, sigma_d 2%): expected fee cost per funded account $1,000; if the funded account then runs the same parameters it has a 60% chance of reaching an 8% payout (about $6,400 at 80% split) before breaching, giving EV per attempt about 0.6 x 0.6 x 6,400 - 600 = +$1,700 before resets, slippage and funding costs. The same strategy at 3% daily vol (p_pass 0.54, but then 38% daily-limit failures) is markedly worse.

### Inferences
- Because the firm's rule set converts a Sharpe-2 strategy into a 55-70% per-attempt pass rate at best, the EV is positive only when the trader's pass probability is 3-5x the population's; resets and the second phase each multiply the fee cost by 1/p again.
- The economically correct comparison for an automated strategy is fee-per-funded-dollar versus the cost of the trader's own margin: at p=0.6 the challenge buys $100k of notional allocation (80% split) for about $1,000 expected, which is cheaper than financing the same risk capital, but the funded account can be withdrawn by the firm at any time (Q8).

### Inferences
- Counterparty risk is the dominant unpriced risk: the fee and the funded balance are unsecured claims on an unregulated offshore company, and the 2024 shutdown wave shows payout halts precede closures. Fee EV calculations (Q6) should haircut the expected payout by a firm-survival probability; none of the sources quantifies it.
- For an India-based trader the cleanest route is USDT payout to a self-custodied wallet, immediate INR fair-value recording, business-income treatment at slab rates for the payout and separate 30%/1%-TDS treatment for any subsequent gain on the USDT itself; the receipt-vs-conversion ambiguity needs a chartered accountant. LRS should not be used to fund margin; the challenge fee is defensible as a service payment but is not endorsed by any RBI text found.
- The FTMO-OANDA transaction and US firms seeking CFTC registration suggest the larger firms are converging on a broker-plus-prop model; HyroTrader-style real-exchange sub-accounts are closer to that model than CFD-feed products.
