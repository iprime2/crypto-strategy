## Key question 1 — Sizing and drawdown control (vol targeting, fractional Kelly, drawdown de-leveraging, regime filters, portfolio heat)

### Takeaway
Published practice converges on: size positions to a volatility target (institutional trend funds run ~10–15% portfolio vol; the repo's own crypto trend backtest shows target vol scales return and drawdown proportionally and leaves Sharpe unchanged, so for a small account the target is a drawdown-tolerance choice, not an alpha choice), deploy at most a quarter-to-half Kelly, add a linear drawdown de-leveraging rule with a hard stop, and gate exposure with realized-vol ratio / percentile filters that have hysteresis so they do not flap. For a $1,000-class account on Delta India, contract rounding (not margin) is the binding constraint.

### Cited Findings
- Man Group ("In Crypto We Trend", Dec 2024) argues crypto suits trend-following because of liquidity, volatility and the lack of a fundamental anchor; it finds peak risk-adjusted returns for a trend-following crypto portfolio at about 10–15 coins, beyond which transaction costs and liquidity constraints outweigh diversification — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend); a third-party summary adds that adjusting exposure to volatility helps manage risk and that high cross-coin correlation limits diversification — [The Idea Farm summary](https://theideafarm.com/alternative-investment/real-assets/crypto/in-crypto-we-trend/).
- Man AHL's trend ETF (AHLT) is described as reducing exposure to a market when that market becomes turbulent, with an overall annualized volatility target of 15% (multi-asset futures, not crypto-specific; blog description, not a Man document) — [th3vjp Substack](https://th3vjp.substack.com/p/how-i-evaluate-trend-following-funds).
- Academic caveat: Benjamin Hood's "Volatility Targeting Is Trendy" finds the alpha of volatility targeting mainly accrues to trend exposure for equity-index futures but not for commodity, fixed-income or currency futures (crypto not tested) — [UMN seminar abstract](https://cse.umn.edu/node/165016).
- A 2026 arXiv paper on systematic crypto trend-following notes that non-stationary volatility regimes make fixed lookback windows suboptimal in crypto, motivating volatility-calibrated parameters — [arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1).
- Repo backtest (2026-10-08, long-only trend on 9 Delta perps, costs taker 5.31 bps + 2 bps slippage, funding included): target vol does not change the Sharpe ratio; it scales return and drawdown together — on Binance data 2020–2026, 25% target gives +15%/yr with −14% max DD, 40% gives +24%/−22%, 60% gives +36.5%/−32%; all 18 pre-registered settings (lookbacks × target vol) were positive, Sharpe 0.99–1.25 on Binance 2020–2026 and 0.48–1.02 on Delta 2024–2026; long/short lost 7% in 2023 and has lower Sharpe than long-only; average gross exposure was 0.19× equity so margin was not a constraint; at $280 of capital BTC and BNB positions round to zero on most days, from about $1,000 rounding costs under 1.5%/yr — repo file `cex-mm/artifacts/research/2026-10-08-trend-following-backtest.md`.
- Fractional Kelly: half Kelly keeps roughly three-quarters of the optimal growth rate while cutting log-wealth volatility in half — [QuantVault Monte Carlo note](https://quantvault.org/problem-112-monte-carlo-backtest-of-kelly-and-fractional-kelly-strategie.html); because the growth penalty for betting above the true optimum is severe while betting below it costs little, practitioners commonly use a half or quarter of the computed fraction, and applying single-trade Kelly to many correlated positions systematically oversizes — [LuxAlgo Kelly concept page](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion/).
- Full Kelly has a 50% chance of a 50% drawdown even under Gaussian assumptions; half-Kelly ~12.5%, quarter-Kelly a fraction of a percent; add hard caps (max per position, max leverage, max drawdown) on top — [marketmaker.cc](https://marketmaker.cc/en/blog/post/kelly-criterion-strategy-sizing/) (cited in repo `resource/05-risk-hedging-pnl-failure-modes.md` §6.4). Continuous Kelly leverage f* = μ/σ² — [QuantStart](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Money-Management-via-the-Kelly-Criterion/). Institutional practice: shrink the edge estimate first, compute raw Kelly, impose an explicit drawdown-probability constraint (Busseti–Ryu–Boyd), then external hard caps — [Atlas Peak Research](https://www.atlaspeakresearch.com/report/07bf72).
- Drawdown-based de-leveraging rule used in the repo's risk design: `daily_loss_limit = 1–2% × capital`; `size_multiplier = clip(1 − dd_t/dd_max, 0.25, 1)` with `dd_max ≈ 8%`; hard stop when `dd_t ≥ dd_max` or daily PnL ≤ −limit; resume only after reconciled positions, written root cause, and next UTC day or manual override — repo `resource/05-risk-hedging-pnl-failure-modes.md` §6.2. Hummingbot's global kill switch (e.g. `kill_switch_rate: -5`) measures session PnL only and excludes unrealized PnL, so define your own limit on total realized + MTM PnL with a daily reset — [Hummingbot docs](https://hummingbot.org/client/global-configs/kill-switch/).
- Regime filters in the repo's parameter table: volatility-regime triggers at σ_short/σ_long = 1.5 (L1) and 2.5 (L2); event pause around CPI/FOMC (T−2 min to T+5 min); capital per venue ≤ 35–40%; inventory soft/hard 20–30% / 40% of pair capital — repo `STRATEGY.md` §12.
- Hysteresis matters: Wintermute's internal circuit breakers tripped "almost every minute" on 10 Oct 2025 and the firm considered making them less aggressive; the repo's rule is to resume only after `ratio_RV < 1.5` for ≥ 5 min — [BlockBeats](https://en.theblockbeats.news/news/59932) via repo `resource/05` §3.
- Portfolio heat / cross-venue caps: cap per-venue exposure at ≤ 35–40% of capital; keep only working capital plus margin buffer on-venue; size the buffer so a full day of one-sided fills at the hard limit needs no intraday deposit (transfers fail in crashes) — repo `resource/05` §4.8, citing the Bybit hack post-mortem [NCC Group](https://www.nccgroup.com/research/in-depth-technical-analysis-of-the-bybit-hack/).
- Current realized-vol percentile (computed from Delta India daily closes, 2026-10-13): BTC 30-day realized vol 38.6% annualized = 44th percentile of rolling 30-day RV over the last 400 days (range 21.6%–82.6%); ETH 30-day RV 44.7% = 22nd percentile (range 27.8%–104.3%) — [Delta India candles API](https://api.india.delta.exchange/v2/history/candles?symbol=BTCUSD&resolution=1d) [SYNTHETIC from API data].

### Inferences

## Key question 3 — Validation standards (DSR, PBO, CPCV, walk-forward, minimum backtest length, pre-registration, honest reporting)

### Takeaway
The literature's standard is: report the number of trials and deflate the Sharpe for multiple testing and non-normality (Bailey–López de Prado DSR; Harvey–Liu haircut, which rejects a flat 50% rule), estimate the probability of backtest overfitting with CSCV/CPCV, fix the rule before testing and report every setting tried, require stress days in-sample, and treat demo/paper PnL as plumbing validation, not edge — the repo already practises most of this (pre-registered 18-setting grid, all reported; mainnet gate on markout evidence).

### Cited Findings
- Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado, 2014, JPM): corrects for selection bias under multiple testing and non-normal returns; asks whether the best observed Sharpe clears the level luck alone would produce across all trials, adjusting for the number of strategies tested, skewness and kurtosis, and backtest length — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); summary at [TradingInterview](https://www.tradinginterview.com/courses/machine-learning/lessons/backtest-overfitting-and-the-deflated-sharpe-ratio/).
- Probability of Backtest Overfitting (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu, J. Computational Finance 2015/17): estimated by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation over the matrix of period returns for all N trials; PBO is the share of splits where the in-sample winner lands in the bottom half out of sample; non-parametric; it only sees the trials it is given, so omitting failed experiments biases it downward; block reshuffling weakens temporal order, so it complements rather than replaces walk-forward and live confirmation — [LuxAlgo PBO page](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/probability-of-backtest-overfitting/).
- CPCV (combinatorial purged cross-validation) produces C(N, k) backtest paths (e.g. N=6, k=2 gives 15 paths) with purging and embargo of overlapping labels — [walk-forward validation skill page](https://skillselion.com/skills/agiprolabs/claude-trading-skills/walk-forward-validation) (secondary; the primary is López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, ch. 12 [MEMORY]).
- Harvey & Liu, "Backtesting" (JPM 2015): backtests should be discounted because of data mining by the researcher and by prior researchers; convert the Sharpe to a t-ratio, assume a number of prior tests (they use at least 316 factors), derive an adjusted p-value and Sharpe; the haircut is nonlinear (the highest Sharpes are moderately penalised, marginal ones heavily); they call the common flat 50% discount "a serious mistake"; the paper also gives a minimum profit hurdle — [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF); [CME copy](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/education/files/backtesting.pdf); [Man Institute note](https://www.man.com/insights/backtesting); R implementation in [quantstrat sandbox](https://rdrr.io/github/braverock/quantstrat/src/sandbox/Harvey_Liu_Backtesting/50a170e4e37da951ea1192888418c82a/Haircut_SR%20with%20sample_random_multests.R).
- Report Sortino and Calmar alongside Sharpe, compute on the full timeline including flat periods (not per trade), and shrink small-sample ratios by n/(n+k) — [marketmaker.cc](https://marketmaker.cc/en/blog/post/objective-function-design-backtest/) via repo `resource/05` §6.3; include crash days (19 May 2021, 9–12 May 2022, 8–10 Nov 2022, 5 Aug 2024, 10–11 Oct 2025, 5–6 Feb 2026) in every backtest and report PnL on those days separately — repo `resource/05` §6.3.
- Pre-registration practice in the repo: the trend rule was "fixed before testing"; all 18 grid settings are reported, with the Sharpe range given rather than the best; a 4-week paper trade on mainnet public data has pre-stated success (every rebalance executes; costs within 2× of the 7.31 bps model) and stop (missed/partial rebalances on two days, or costs above 2× model) criteria — repo `cex-mm/artifacts/research/2026-10-08-trend-following-backtest.md`.
- Promotion gate: "Testnet validates plumbing; mainnet validates edge. Do not promote on testnet PnL alone"; small live requires ≥ 4 weeks with signed markouts at 1 s/5 s/30 s, toxicity ratio < 0.7 at 10 s, no unreconciled positions, and an exchange realized-PnL export — repo `cex-mm/docs/MAINNET_GATE.md` and `STRATEGY.md` §11.3.

### Inferences
- A practical honest-reporting checklist for this project: (1) log every parameter set and signal variant tried (N), (2) report DSR with that N and the observed skew/kurtosis, (3) run CSCV/CPCV over the grid and report PBO, (4) walk-forward with a purge/embargo of at least the signal lookback, (5) keep the 2025-10-10 and 2026-02 crash windows in every test and report them separately, (6) state the Harvey–Liu-style haircut rather than a flat 50%, (7) treat the 4-week paper trade as a plumbing/cost check and the 4-week small-live as the edge test. [SYNTHETIC synthesis of the sources above]
- Minimum backtest length: Bailey et al.'s MinBTL result says the required length grows with ln(N) and falls with the square of the target Sharpe (roughly MinBTL ≈ 2 ln N / E[max Sharpe]² years for N trials) — [AMS Notices 2014, "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism"](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf) [MEMORY, UNVERIFIED formula]; with ~2 years of Delta data (since 2023-12) and an 18-setting grid, the Delta-only sample is short, which is why the repo uses the 2020–2026 Binance archive as the longer proxy.

## Key question 4 — India context: tax, legality of foreign exchanges/prop firms, payout rails

### Takeaway
Tax treatment of crypto perps in India is genuinely unsettled: exchanges and some advisers apply the flat 30% VDA regime (s.115BBH, with no loss set-off) and the 1% TDS (s.194S), while tax commentators argue non-delivery, cash-settled perps are speculative business income under s.43(5)/s.73 (losses set off within speculative business, ITR-3 Schedule BP); settlement currency (USDT, itself a VDA, vs INR) is the pivot. Using FIU-IND-registered venues is clearly lawful; offshore unregistered exchanges have been URL-blocked (25 more in Oct 2025) and banks make LRS remitters declare funds will not buy crypto, though no statute expressly bans it. P2P USDT→INR disposals are taxable VDA transfers with 1% TDS mechanics that are poorly enforced but still owed.

### Cited Findings
Income-tax treatment of perps:
- KoinX (2026): Indian crypto futures traders are filing under two different systems. For a USDT-settled perpetual, what changes hands at settlement is USDT, and USDT is itself a VDA, so the conservative view is that s.115BBH applies; for INR-settled contracts there is a credible argument that no VDA transfer occurred, so s.115BBH does not apply; derivative income belongs in Schedule BP of ITR-3, and reporting INR-settled derivative profits in Schedule VDA misclassifies business income as a VDA disposal — [KoinX](https://www.koinx.com/in/crypto-tax-stories/crypto-futures-traders-in-india-are-filing-taxes-under-two-different-systems); see also [KoinX derivatives guide](https://www.koinx.com/in/tax-guides/is-crypto-derivatives-taxable-in-india).
- TaxGuru analysis: profits from crypto perpetual futures "should ordinarily be treated as speculative business under sections 43(5) and 73, rather than income from transfer of the underlying cryptocurrency under section 115BBH"; the position rests on the contract being non-delivery based and settled in a specified stablecoin; the Income-tax Act does not expressly deal with cryptocurrency perpetual futures; the legislation does not specifically refer to crypto futures, options, derivative contracts or contracts settled otherwise than by delivery — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/taxation-crypto-futures-options-income-tax-act-1961.html).
