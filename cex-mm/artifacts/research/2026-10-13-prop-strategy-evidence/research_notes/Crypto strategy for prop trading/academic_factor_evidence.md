# Academic evidence on crypto return predictability, 2018–2026: what survives out of sample after 2022 and after costs

Research-process note for the writer: direct page fetches (arXiv, NBER, SSRN, BIS, RePEc, Quantpedia, Alpha Architect) were all blocked by the egress proxy in this environment, and the shared web-search budget was exhausted after ~20 searches. Every number below therefore comes from search-engine page extracts of the primary paper or of a secondary summary, not from reading the results tables directly. Where only an abstract was visible, the finding is labelled "abstract-level". Where a number came from a secondary summary (blog, news item, another paper quoting it), that is stated. Numbers I could not corroborate are tagged [UNVERIFIED]. Each finding carries tags: Period / Universe / Freq / Costs / IS-OOS / Post-2022 evidence.

Convention: "Post-2022: YES" means the sample or the test extends past Dec 2022. "Post-2022: NO" means the sample ends 2022 or earlier.

## Key question 1: Time-series momentum / trend following on BTC, ETH and large alts

### Takeaway
Time-series momentum (TSMOM) is the single most-replicated crypto anomaly and is repeatedly found more robust than cross-sectional momentum, but every study that applies realistic frictions (costs, intraday liquidation, robust inference) shrinks it sharply, and there is a documented claim that the 2013–2018 TSMOM effect weakened after bitcoin futures launched. Post-2022 evidence exists (2020–2025 samples, a 2022–2025 self-reported trend strategy with Sharpe 2.41) but none of it comes from a top-journal paper with full cost accounting.

### Inferences
- The Liu–Tsyvinski 3.16%-per-SD weekly BTC autocorrelation is large but comes from 2011–2018, when BTC weekly vol was ~15–20%; no paper found re-estimates it on 2022–2026 data with costs, so its current size is unknown.
- Two independent lines (Swinkels' post-futures disappearance; Han–Kang–Ryu's liquidation-adjusted results) both point to TSMOM at 1-day to 1-month horizons being much weaker than headline tables imply once the asset became shortable/hedgeable and once frictions are applied.
- For a small live account the only post-2022 TSMOM numbers found are from non-peer-reviewed or low-tier sources (Sharpe 2.41 preprint; Gbadebo 2026), so TSMOM should be treated as "plausible, must be re-validated in-house" rather than "established".
- Long-only vs long/short: Han–Kang–Ryu's "losers often rebound sharply" and Grobys et al.'s crash finding both imply the short leg is where crypto momentum blows up; a long-only or long/flat trend overlay is the lower-risk implementation.

### Gaps
- No study found that reports BTC/ETH TSMOM Sharpe and drawdown by the sub-periods 2017–2021 / 2022 / 2023–2026 with a stated cost assumption. The Borri–Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu 2025 update (see Q8) covers through Sep 2025 but its momentum tables were not visible in extracts.
- Lookback-specific results (e.g., 20-day vs 100-day vs 200-day MA) for BTC after 2022: not found. Detzel–Liu–Strauss–Zhou–Zhu (technical analysis in bitcoin) and Hudson & Urquhart (15,000 technical rules) could not be searched before the budget ran out.
- Han–Kang–Ryu's exact cost assumptions and sample end-date: not visible.

## Key question 2: Cross-sectional factors — momentum, short-term reversal, size, volume/liquidity, idiosyncratic volatility, skewness

### Takeaway
The Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu three-factor structure (market, size, momentum) and large weekly long-short premia (2.5–4.1%/week for 1–4 week momentum) are in-sample 2014–2018/2020 numbers with no cost adjustment; replications through 2022–2023 find the premia survive statistically but are concentrated in small, illiquid, volatile coins, collapse to 2–3 effective factors (dominated by liquidity), and carry crash risk so severe that one coin can wipe out significance. Short-term reversal is best interpreted as compensation for liquidity provision in low-activity pairs. I found no evidence on idiosyncratic-volatility or skewness premia after 2022.

### Inferences
- The three replication-style papers that extend to 2022–2023 (Fieberg et al.; Grobys et al.; Cakici et al.) all agree on the same caveat: crypto cross-sectional premia are driven by small, illiquid, volatile coins and by the long leg. A small account on a single venue (e.g., Delta India perps) cannot hold the small-cap long leg that generates these alphas, and the short leg is the crash-prone side.
- The liquidity-provision reading of short-term reversal (Bianchi et al.) is consistent with a market-making business model rather than a directional signal: reversal pays where adverse-selection fear is highest, which is exactly where a small post-only quoter is most exposed.
- Because 2–3 factors (liquidity-dominated) absorb all alphas (Mercik et al.), most of the 34–36 published anomalies are redundant; "momentum" and "illiquidity/size" are the irreducible ones.

### Gaps
- Idiosyncratic volatility, skewness/lottery, and max-return factors in crypto: no sources retrieved before the search budget ran out (Borri; Jia–Goodell–Shen "Momentum or reversal: the appropriate third factor"; Cong–Karolyi–Tang–Zhao value/network factors were all on the list but not searched).
- No paper found reporting cross-sectional factor Sharpe ratios on a post-2022-only window.
- t-statistics and Sharpe ratios for the Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu tables were not visible; only the weekly return magnitudes were.

## Key question 3: Volatility-managed / vol-targeted crypto portfolios

### Takeaway
Across the academic and practitioner evidence found, volatility scaling in crypto reliably reduces drawdowns, tail risk and time underwater, but Sharpe-ratio gains are modest (Bloomberg "Crypto Insights": "only a minor improvement in risk-adjusted returns"), and Grobys et al. (2025) warn that because crypto momentum's tail variance is undefined, vol-management cannot remove the tail. Drawdown-based (MinMax) control gets a better return-to-drawdown profile than vol control for BTC.

### Cited Findings
- Bloomberg Professional "Crypto Insights: The Impact of Volatility Targeting" (hosted on assets.bbhub.io; chart axis shows 2016–2020): targeting 10% annualized vol, BTC and ETH realized 12.1% and 14.4% respectively, "a minor improvement in risk-adjusted returns"; larger benefit in tail profile ("more positive skew and much less time spent underwater"); equal-weight BTC/ETH at the same target had risk-adjusted return ≈ 1.7; across a range of targets risk-adjusted returns were roughly unchanged. Costs: not stated [UNVERIFIED]. IS. Post-2022: NO. — [Bloomberg Crypto Insights PDF](https://assets.bbhub.io/professional/sites/10/Crypto-Insights-The-Impact-of-Volatility-Targeting.pdf)
- Chassang & Wang (2018), "Managing a Crypto-Currency Portfolio Via Minmax Drawdown Control", as excerpted by Macrosynergy: "both volatility control and MinMax drawdown control considerably improve the risk-reward trade-off of investing in bitcoin as captured by the Sharpe ratio. However, to match the realized drawdowns of MinMax, volatility control must considerably reduce its exposure throughout the investment period, which significantly reduces the corresponding annualized returns." Multi-asset case: BTC, ETH, XRP. Costs: not stated. Post-2022: NO. — [Macrosynergy, Drawdown control](https://macrosynergy.com/research/drawdown-control/)
- Grobys et al. 2025 (FMPM), weekly, top-30, 2016–2023: "volatility management is a useful tool for mitigating cryptocurrency momentum crashes", but tail variance is undefined so "risk management does not change the tail risk." Post-2022: YES. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)
- Kaya & Mostowfi (FRL 2022): low-vol selection + stop-loss "reduced markedly" downside risk and "improved significantly" Sharpe. — [ZHAW PDF](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/23853/3/2022_Kaya-Mostowfi_Low-volatility-strategies-cryptocurrencies_frl.pdf)
- Search-summary of an arXiv study on volatility-scaled position sizing in bitcoin: Sharpe rose but "the risk, however, also increases in terms of Daily VaR and max. drawdown" — paper identity not resolved [UNVERIFIED]. — (no URL resolved; see Gaps)
- "Simple and Effective Portfolio Construction with Crypto Assets" (arXiv 2412.02654, Dec 2024): title only; content not retrieved [UNVERIFIED]. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.02654)
- Talyxion (arXiv 2511.13239, Nov 2025): pipeline with volatility-aware optimization and drawdown-based risk management; headline live result Sharpe 5.72, max DD 4.56% over 30 days — single author, 30-day sample; not evidence. Post-2022: YES (but trivial sample). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13239)

### Inferences
- The consistent message is that vol targeting is a risk tool, not an alpha source, in crypto: it buys lower drawdown/time-underwater at roughly unchanged Sharpe. For a small account the practical value is sizing discipline, not return enhancement.
- The Grobys et al. infinite-tail-variance result implies vol-scaling should be paired with hard position caps or drawdown control rather than relied on alone.

### Gaps
- No Moreira–Muir-style academic test of volatility-managed crypto portfolios (i.e., regressing scaled on unscaled returns and reporting alpha) with a post-2022 sample was found.
- No source reports vol-targeting results net of the extra turnover it generates at 5–10 bps round-trip.

## Key question 4: Funding rate, basis and carry as predictors of perpetual returns

### Takeaway
The strongest documented effect in this whole ledger is crypto carry (short perp / long spot, collecting funding): Borri–Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu (2025) report a 2020–2025 annualized Sharpe of 6.45 that fell to 4.06 from 2024 and turned negative in 2025, and Schmeling–Schrimpf–Todorov document carry reaching 40–60% p.a. with margin-spike/liquidation crash risk. As a directional predictor of perp returns, however, the only evidence found (a Presto Labs note) says single-asset predictive power of funding "diminishes for subsequent periods" and is more useful cross-sectionally; no peer-reviewed paper establishing funding as a contrarian or continuation signal at a specific horizon was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (arXiv 2212.06888; v1 Dec 2022, v6 Aug 2024, v7 exists): derive no-arbitrage perp prices under random-maturity arbitrage and no-arbitrage bounds with trading costs; empirically, "deviations of crypto perpetual futures from no-arbitrage prices are considerably larger than those" in traditional markets (sentence truncated in extract). The implied arbitrage strategy "yields high Sharpe ratios" and the random-maturity version "generates a sizable Sharpe ratio even under high trading costs"; one extract cites Table 6 BTC all-period Sharpe of 11.65 at zero trading costs for one strategy variant. A secondary blog attributes to the paper BTC annualized Sharpe 3.53 at zero fees and 1.80 under high retail costs, with convergence supplying ~63% of BTC and ~65% of ETH strategy return and funding payments a smaller share — these conflict with the 11.65 figure (different strategy/table) [UNVERIFIED]. The paper is about pricing/arbitrage, not funding-as-return-predictor. Period/exchanges not visible [UNVERIFIED]. Post-2022: partially (revisions through 2024). — [arXiv v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888v6); [arXiv HTML v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v7); [sharpe.ai blog citing the paper](https://sharpe.ai/blog/funding-rate-arbitrage)
- Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov, "Crypto Carry" (BIS Working Paper 1087, Apr 2023; CEPR DP 20719, 2025; Management Science): carry defined as the gap between crypto futures and spot; "can reach exceptionally high levels, sometimes exceeding 40% per annum" (BIS summary) / "up to 60% p.a." (WP abstract); attributed to a volatile convenience yield driven by trend-chasing smaller investors seeking leveraged upside and scarcity of arbitrage capital, with cash-and-carry "risky due to spikes in margins and liquidations amid drawdowns." Strategy Sharpe/drawdown not visible [UNVERIFIED]. Post-2022: YES (2025 revisions). — [BIS WP 1087 PDF](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf); [CEPR DP 20719](https://cepr.org/publications/dp20719); [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069)

- Kim & Park, "Designing funding rates for perpetual futures in cryptocurrency markets" (arXiv 2506.08573, Jun 2025): mechanism design, not predictability. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08573)
- "Funding Rate Mechanism in Perpetual Futures" (ResearchGate listing): models a linear funding rule that "induces an endogenous mean-reverting basis in a setting with momentum speculators" — theoretical support for a contrarian basis signal, no empirical horizon given [UNVERIFIED]. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401892113_Funding_Rate_Mechanism_in_Perpetual_Futures)
- Baseline funding statistic (practitioner): funding sits at the 0.01%/8h floor "78% of a quarter" per one analysis; 0.01%/8h ≈ 10.95% p.a. — [Coincub](https://coincub.com/blog/perpetual-futures-funding-rates-78-of-a-quarter-at-0-01/)

### Inferences
- Carry is a delta-neutral strategy (long spot, short perp) and needs two legs on two venues or a spot leg; on a perps-only Indian venue a small account can only harvest it by being short perp against spot held elsewhere, which raises cost and operational risk. Its Sharpe decayed sharply into 2024–2025 per Borri et al., consistent with arbitrage capital arriving (ETF era).
- Directionally, the literature's mechanism (He et al. random-maturity convergence; the mean-reverting-basis model) supports funding/basis being contrarian for the perp–spot basis, but not necessarily for the spot price. No source retrieved pins a horizon or magnitude for price prediction.
- For a 2.12 bps maker / 5.31 bps taker fee schedule, a funding signal would need to predict more than ~5–10 bps per round-trip; the Presto note's "diminishes for subsequent periods" suggests the edge is concentrated in the first funding interval.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper found that tests "high funding → next-8h/24h perp return" with sign, t-stat and cost — this is the central open question for a live strategy and must be measured in-house.
- He et al.'s exchanges, sample period and the exact strategy behind each Sharpe figure could not be read; the 11.65 vs 3.53/1.80 conflict is unresolved.
- Schmeling et al. strategy-level returns/drawdowns and sub-period (2022 crash, FTX) results were not visible.

## Key question 5: Intraday seasonality — time-of-day, day-of-week, weekend, US vs Asia session, funding timestamps

### Takeaway
The only academic study retrieved (Baur, Cahill, Godfrey & Liu; >15M observations, seven exchanges) finds time-varying but no consistent or persistent intraday or calendar return patterns in bitcoin, though it does find a weak weekend-volume and Monday-return effect and higher activity in US hours; practitioner backtests (Quantpedia, Gemini hourly data) report 22:00–23:00 UTC strongest and 03:00–04:00 UTC weakest hours without cost accounting. No source on returns around the 00:00/08:00/16:00 UTC funding timestamps was found.

### Cited Findings
- Baur, Cahill, Godfrey & Liu, "Bitcoin Time-of-Day, Day-of-Week and Month-of-Year Effects in Returns and Trading Volume" (UWA working paper): >15 million price and volume observations from seven global exchanges; "reveal time-varying effects but no consistent or persistent patterns across the sample period. The results suggest that Bitcoin markets are efficient"; "lower weekend volume effect and a higher Monday return effect which are more consistent with currency markets"; "increased trading activity on Bitcoin exchanges at times when U.S. stock exchanges are open and lower trading activity between midnight and the early morning"; USD-denominated exchanges show stronger patterns than JPY/CNY ones. Period not visible [UNVERIFIED]; likely pre-2019. Costs: n/a. Post-2022: NO. — [UWA repository](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/bitcoin-time-of-day-day-of-week-and-month-of-year-effects-in-retu/)

Blog backtest, no transaction costs, no significance tests visible. Post-2022: unknown [UNVERIFIED]. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/)

## Key question 6: Lead-lag across assets and exchanges (BTC leads alts; Binance leads other venues)

### Takeaway
Venue-level evidence (minute data, Jun 2020–Apr 2021) puts Binance's stablecoin spot pair and Binance's linear perp at the head of price discovery, with Coinbase close behind; a 2026 paper (Kurihara & Matsumoto) finds small-cap coins respond with a significant delay to BTC moves and simulates trading on it, but its lag lengths and net-of-cost profits were not visible. Sub-minute cross-venue leads are not resolvable on the 5-second grids used in the newest studies.

### Cited Findings
- NHH master's thesis (2021): minute-by-minute data Jun 2020–Apr 2021; "Binance's stablecoin-denominated pair leads the spot market, with Coinbase in a close second"; "Binance's linear perpetual swap was the leading instrument in the perpetual swap market"; CME futures' role "more pronounced than in earlier studies." Student work; no profitability test. Post-2022: NO. — [NHH open access](https://openaccess.nhh.no/nhh-xmlui/handle/11250/2766908)
- "Price Discovery in Cryptocurrency Markets" (arXiv 2506.08718, 2025): centralized market leads decentralized over the long run, attributed to higher liquidity and faster information incorporation; estimated lead-lag time is zero seconds because the study did not use tick-by-tick data. Post-2022: YES. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08718)
- Research Square preprint rs-10147582 (Binance vs Hyperliquid mid-prices on a five-second grid): warns "the five-second grid cannot resolve a sub-five-second lead", so the estimated lags are directional, not literal economic leads. Post-2022: YES. — [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10147582)
- Kurihara & Matsumoto (Asia-Pacific Financial Markets, 2026, doi 10.1007/s10690-026-09589-z): "small-cap cryptocurrencies exhibit significant delayed responses to BTC price movements"; the paper "simulates trading strategies triggered by recent BTC moves to test whether profits can be achieved in practice." Lag lengths, universe, period and net profits not visible [UNVERIFIED]. Post-2022: likely YES [UNVERIFIED]. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10690-026-09589-z)
- Hackernoon essay "Bitcoin moves first because it's the capital gateway" — opinion, not evidence. — [Hackernoon](https://sia.hackernoon.com/bitcoin-moves-first-because-its-the-capital-gateway-not-because-its-king)

### Inferences
- The exploitable lead-lag is at sub-minute horizons on BTC/ETH (where it is arbitraged quickly and needs low-latency access to Binance data, which is DNS-blocked on the user's network except via the data-stream.binance.vision mirror) or at minutes-to-hours on small caps (where the user's venue may not list the coins and spreads/fees dominate). Neither matches a small, post-only account on Delta India well.
- The absence of any retrieved study showing net-of-cost profitability at minute-to-hour horizons for large alts is itself informative: if it were large, it would likely be in the literature.

### Gaps

## Key question 7: Machine-learning return prediction in crypto (daily/hourly)

### Takeaway
ML papers consistently report directional accuracy only a few points above 50% at daily horizons (52.9–54.1% across the top 100; 55–65% in smaller samples), and the one hourly study that applies a 10 bps cost finds naive sign strategies fail; profitability is restored only by cost-aware trade filters, in selected configurations, with self-reported Sharpe just above 1. Cross-sectional ML (Cakici et al.) stays profitable after costs but its alphas sit in small illiquid coins.

### Cited Findings
- Bysik & Ślepaczuk (Univ. of Warsaw, preprint arXiv 2606.00060, May/Jun 2026): hourly BTC-USDT, ~70,000 observations; "naive sign-based strategies fail once transaction costs of ten basis points are imposed"; "a cost-aware filter that prevents trades [unless] the forecast magnitude exceeds a transaction-cost-based threshold sharply reduces turnover and restores profitability in selected configurations"; "the strongest long-only XGBoost strategy produces annualised returns above 65% with a Sharpe ratio above one." Preprint, not peer-reviewed; the "selected configurations" wording signals selection risk. Period: not visible (~70k hours ≈ 8 years, so likely 2018–2026) [UNVERIFIED]. OOS (ML). Post-2022: YES. — [Emergent Mind listing](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.00060); [ChatPaper listing](https://chatpaper.com/paper/289469)
- Liu et al. (2023; Bentley Univ. listing, "Forecasting cryptocurrency returns with machine learning"): 3,703 cryptocurrencies, 2013–2021, daily; equal-weighted portfolio "7.1% daily return with a 1.95 Sharpe ratio"; capital-weighted "2.4% daily return, 0.27 Sharpe"; OOS R² 4.855%. A 7.1%/day equal-weight figure is only possible with microcaps and no costs. Costs: none visible. OOS (ML). Post-2022: NO. — [Bentley Experts listing](https://experts.bentley.edu/en/publications/forecasting-cryptocurrency-returns-with-machine-learning-5/)
- Jaquart, Köpke & Weinhardt (Journal of Finance and Data Science, Nov 2022): daily direction of the 100 largest cryptos; "average accuracy values … range from 52.9% to 54.1%"; restricting to high-confidence predictions raises this to 57.5–59.5%. Post-2022: NO. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/cfc09801314441198e3e1c92a3914e54)
- Akyildirim, Goncu & Sensoy (Annals of Operations Research, 2021): twelve liquid cryptocurrencies at daily and minute frequency; "55–65% predictive accuracy on average", SVM best. No cost-adjusted P&L visible. Post-2022: NO. — [Bilkent repository PDF](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/bitstream/11693/77044/1/Prediction_of_cryptocurrency_returns_using_machine_learning.pdf)
- Falcon & Lyu (search-summary attribution [UNVERIFIED]): classify next-day sign for BTC, ETH, XRP; "the most significant valuation factors for cryptocurrency returns are price trend variables, seven and thirty-day reversal." — (source link not resolved; candidate [TKU PDF](https://ms.tku.edu.tw/ms/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/M35N301.pdf) [UNVERIFIED])
- Cakici, Shahzad, Będowska-Sójka & Zaremba (IRFA 2024): see Q2 — cross-sectional ML profitable after costs, limited gain from complexity, alpha concentrated in small illiquid coins. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001765.html)

### Inferences
- 53–54% daily directional accuracy on large coins is roughly what a simple 1–7 day momentum/reversal rule delivers, and after 5–10 bps round-trip it is near zero expected value unless trades are filtered by predicted magnitude — the Bysik–Ślepaczuk result is the clearest statement of that.
- The headline ML Sharpe figures (1.95 EW daily; >1 hourly XGBoost) collapse toward the cap-weighted/large-cap numbers (0.27) once small caps are removed, i.e., ML mostly re-discovers the small-cap liquidity premium.

### Gaps
- No ML study found that reports post-2022-only out-of-sample Sharpe on BTC/ETH with explicit fees and slippage from a peer-reviewed venue.
- Critiques specifically about look-ahead bias / data leakage in crypto ML papers were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).

## Key question 8: Meta-evidence — data snooping, multiple testing, replication, and how much of reported performance is 2017–2021

### Takeaway
Crypto-specific multiple-testing work is thin but points one way: of 34–36 published anomalies, only 2–3 liquidity-dominated factors survive joint tests; momentum premia are crash-driven, large-cap-only, and have statistically undefined tail variance; the most-cited carry trade decayed from Sharpe 6.45 (2020–2025) to negative in 2025; and the foundational factor numbers (Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu) are 2014–2018/2020 in-sample with no costs. The updated Borri–Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu (2025) review finds crypto Sharpe ratios "about the same as equities" through Sep 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu (arXiv 2510.14435, Oct 2025; v3/v4 Feb 2026): re-checks Liu & Tsyvinski (2021) through September 2025 — "weekly Sharpe ratios for crypto indices and equities come out similar"; returns and volatility much higher but Sharpe ≈ equities; cross-section summarized by a small set of factors; jumps frequent and large; blockchain information helps drive prices; later versions add a discussion of data quality. Carry decay: Sharpe 6.45 (2020–2025) → 4.06 from 2024 → negative in 2025. Post-2022: YES. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14435); [SSRN 5612870](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5612870); [Quantpedia summary "10 Lessons"](https://quantpedia.com/cryptocurrency-as-an-investable-asset-class-10-lessons/)
- Fieberg, Liedtke, Metko & Zaremba (QF 2023): 34 anomalies, >3,900 coins, 2014–2022; factor autocorrelation "not widespread", mainly size and volatility anomalies. — [ICM repository](https://open.icm.edu.pl/items/d9f44e22-0614-4db8-8585-6dcba8906bb5/full)
- Mercik, Zaremba & Demir: 36 factors; 2–3 liquidity-dominated factors eliminate all alphas. — [ICM bitstream](https://open.icm.edu.pl/bitstreams/21636cf6-61df-4fb5-9ae4-4195d592cd26/download) [attribution UNVERIFIED]
- Grobys et al. (FMPM 2025) and Grobys & Shahzad (IJFE 2026): crypto momentum crash risk; single-coin sensitivity; variance-based performance metrics "not informative". — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ijfe.70036)
- "On survivor cryptocurrency momentum" (FRL 2026): survivorship-bias angle; content not retrieved [UNVERIFIED]. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v92y2026ics1544612326001339.html)
- Han, Kang & Ryu: mean-return t-tests insufficient; many momentum portfolios liquidated under realistic frictions. — [PDF](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)
- Swinkels (2018): TSMOM disappearance after bitcoin futures — early evidence of arbitrage-driven decay. — [Tinbergen](https://utilities.tinbergen.nl/?p=66235)
- Equity "factor zoo" benchmarks used by crypto replicators: Hou, Xue & Zhang replicate 447 anomalies with NYSE breakpoints/value weights and a t ≥ 1.96 bar; Harvey, Liu & Zhu propose multiple-testing thresholds; Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen find 82.4% of anomalies replicate under a Bayesian approach; an international test finds only 19 of 132 anomalies (14%) survive after microcap and multiple-testing controls. — [Hou–Xue–Zhang NBER w23394](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394.pdf); [Alpha Architect international anomalies](https://alphaarchitect.com/international-tests-of-anomalies-most-dont-survive/); [Systematically Biased, Pockets of Replicability](https://systematicallybiased.substack.com/p/pockets-of-replicability-post5)

### Inferences
- Share of reported performance from 2017–2021: the only direct sub-period evidence found is the carry decay (Borri et al.) and the Swinkels post-futures TSMOM disappearance; everything else is a full-sample 2014–2022/2023 number. By construction, the 2014–2018 Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu momentum premia (2.5–4.1%/week) are dominated by the 2017 bubble and the 2018 crash cross-section; no retrieved paper reports them ex-2017.
- Applying the equity factor-zoo survival rate (~14% after microcap and multiple-testing controls) to the 34–36 crypto anomalies would leave ~5; the crypto-specific joint tests (Mercik et al.) leave 2–3, all liquidity-related. That is consistent with momentum and illiquidity/size being the only effects worth building on, and with the caveat that a small account cannot hold the illiquid leg.
- Net for the strategy decision: (1) the one effect with post-2022 top-author evidence of a large realized Sharpe is funding-rate carry, but it is a two-leg, delta-neutral trade whose Sharpe collapsed in 2024–2025; (2) time-series momentum/trend on BTC/ETH is the only directional effect with repeated support and a post-2022 (if unrefereed) Sharpe > 2 claim, but the refereed evidence says it is crash-prone, large-cap-only, and friction-sensitive, so it should be implemented long/flat with vol- or drawdown-based sizing and validated in-house on 2022–2026 Delta/Binance data before any capital.

### Gaps
- No crypto study applying a formal Harvey–Liu–Zhu style t-stat hurdle (e.g., t > 3) to the full anomaly set was found.
- No published decomposition of any crypto anomaly's Sharpe into 2017–2021 vs 2022–2026 sub-periods with costs was retrieved; this is the key measurement the user will need to do themselves.
- Searches not executed because the shared search budget ran out: Caporale & Plastun (day-of-week), Alexander & Heck (venue price discovery), Detzel et al. and Hudson & Urquhart (technical rules), Jia–Goodell–Shen (reversal third factor), Cong–Karolyi–Tang–Zhao (value/network factors), Borri (tail risk), Schwenkler (network returns), idiosyncratic-vol and skewness factor papers, crypto ML data-leakage critiques, and any "funding → next-day return" test.
