## Key Question 1: Who are the main crypto prop firms in 2025-2026 and what are their exact rules?

### Takeaway
The crypto-native firms that keep appearing across independent 2026 roundups are Breakout (owned by Kraken since 1 Sep 2025), HyroTrader (Bybit-linked, simulated accounts on real Bybit data), and Crypto Fund Trader (Bybit-data demo, 550-715 pairs, on FINMA's warning list). FTMO, FundedNext and Hola Prime are forex-first CFD firms with crypto symbols. Newer entrants in 2026 are SizeProp (Cayman, VC-backed), Velotrade (Hong Kong, ex-bank traders) and WenCrypto (Maven, simulated-only). Rule sets converge on a 3-5% daily loss limit and a 3-10% max drawdown with 9-12% targets; the firms differ most on stop-loss mandates, consistency rules, payout caps and whether 2-step products still exist.

### Inferences
- "Real perps vs CFD": none of the three crypto-native firms gives a trader a live exchange sub-account funded by the firm. Breakout runs a proprietary terminal on Kraken infrastructure; HyroTrader runs simulated accounts on real Bybit data (optionally through the trader's own Bybit account); CFT is fully demo on Bybit data. FTMO/FundedNext/Hola Prime are broker-style CFD feeds. The practical difference is fills: Breakout/HyroTrader/CFT quote the real perp order book (so maker fills are plausible to simulate), CFD firms quote a dealer spread with no maker side.
- The 2-step product is disappearing at Breakout (legacy as of Aug 2026) and CFT dropped minimum trading days; the trend is toward 1-step, no-time-limit, static-drawdown evaluations, which favour low-variance systematic strategies.
- Numbers on third-party sites are frequently 6-12 months stale (Breakout 8%/5% listings; CFT min-days). Any rule used for strategy design must be re-read on the firm's dashboard on the day of purchase.

### Gaps
- No firm's own rules/terms page could be opened (network block). Exact reset times (00:30 vs 00:00 UTC), equity-vs-balance definitions, and whether perp funding payments count against drawdown are unconfirmed for all three crypto-native firms.
- Whether Breakout's terminal or HyroTrader's Tealstreet/Tiger terminals expose an API suitable for a bot was not found. This is the single most important gap for a systematic strategy.
- HyroTrader's "consistency"/40% rule, 10- vs 5-day minimum, and 2-step drawdown numbers are contradictory across sources.
- FTMO's crypto symbol list and current challenge numbers were not retrieved (search budget).

## Key Question 2: Which firms accept India-resident traders, and what KYC/payout rails do they use?

### Takeaway
FTMO re-opened to Indian traders on 4 December 2025 after roughly three years of exclusion, but FTMO sits on the RBI Alert List. HyroTrader imposes no country restrictions of its own (Bybit's terms govern if the trader connects a Bybit account; the Tealstreet terminal has none). Breakout's published restricted list does not name India and Kraken hosts an India-localised comparison page. Payouts to India are overwhelmingly in USDT/USDC; direct INR rails are uncommon; Indian VDA tax (30% + cess, 1% TDS) applies when the stablecoin is sold.

### Inferences (design implications)
- Daily-loss is the binding constraint, not max drawdown: with 3% (Breakout) or 4% (HyroTrader/CFT) daily on equity including floating P&L, a market-making bot must cap intraday net loss (realized + unrealized + fees) at roughly half the limit (1.5-2% of account) to survive a fat-tailed day, and must treat the firm's UTC reset as the accounting day, not local time.
- Static max DD of 3-6% means total cumulative loss before a 9-12% gain must stay below that; at Breakout Turbo (3% max DD, 9% target) the required win/loss asymmetry is 3:1, which is hostile to any strategy with Sharpe below ~2 on a daily basis. Classic (6%/10%) is the loosest crypto-native rule set found.
- HyroTrader's 5-minute mandatory stop-loss and 3%-per-trade / 25%-exposure rules are incompatible with a resting-quote market maker unless each fill is wrapped with an attached stop; its 40% consistency rule penalises a single large day. Breakout has none of these, which makes it the most compatible with a systematic/MM style among the three, but Breakout's prohibited-practice list could not be read and may include HFT/arbitrage language similar to CFT's.
- CFT explicitly bans HFT, tick scalping, latency exploitation and arbitrage; a post-only market maker is at risk of being classified as "tick scalping". CFT should be treated as unsuitable for the user's MM strategy unless its support confirms in writing.
- With 5x max leverage on BTC/ETH at Breakout and one-tick spreads on BTCUSD/ETHUSD (per project CLAUDE.md, 0.06/0.18 bps), passive quoting cannot generate a 10% target on account equity in reasonable time; a prop-passing strategy would need to be a lower-frequency directional or basis/funding strategy on altcoin perps where spread exceeds fees, not the current tick-spread MM.
- Fee economics for a small-capital trader: Breakout $5K Classic at $60 risks $60 for a $5K simulated allocation with a $300 (6%) max DD and $150 (3%) daily; HyroTrader $10K at $119-$129 is refundable on first payout but capped at $500 (5%) per payout cycle.

### Gaps
- API/bot access at Breakout and via HyroTrader's terminals is unknown; if the only route is a GUI terminal, a Rust bot cannot be pointed at these accounts without a bridge, and automation may itself be a prohibited practice.
- Breakout's and HyroTrader's prohibited-practice lists (HFT, arbitrage, copy trading, hedging across accounts, martingale, news, weekend, max exposure) were not retrieved.
- Whether perpetual funding payments and exchange trading fees count against daily loss and max drawdown is unconfirmed at all three firms.
- Exact daily-reset timestamps (00:30 vs 00:00 vs 00:05 UTC) are each from a single third-party source.
