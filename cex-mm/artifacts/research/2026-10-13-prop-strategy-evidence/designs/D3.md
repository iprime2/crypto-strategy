## 1. Thesis

The edge is **time-series momentum, long/flat, volatility-scaled, on large-cap crypto perps**, and nothing else in the evidence pack clears the bar. It is the one effect that (a) is the most-replicated crypto anomaly in the literature and the one that survives realistic frictions best, while cross-sectional momentum does not (Han, Kang & Ryu, [AUT working paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf) [UNVERIFIED]; Liu & Tsyvinski, [NBER w24877](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf); Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, [JFE 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003)); (b) was fixed in this repo **before** testing, with all 18 grid settings reported and all positive: CAGR +24.2%, Sharpe 1.25, max DD −21.9% on Binance 2020-05..2026-09 after Delta taker fees + slippage + funding, Sharpe 1.15 on Delta's own prices 2024-08..2026-09 with daily returns correlated 0.999 to the Binance run (`cex-mm/artifacts/research/2026-10-08-trend-following-backtest.md`, reproduced in `local_evidence.md`); and (c) in this session **beat both benchmarks on a risk-adjusted basis without being BTC beta**: BTC buy-and-hold earned Sharpe 0.90 with a −77% drawdown over the same window, vol-targeted BTC with no signal earned 0.74 (−36%), and vol-targeted always-long 9 coins earned 0.96 (−54%); the rule's correlation to BTC is 0.57, its beta 0.19 and its annualized alpha over BTC +13.7% (§4, [SYNTHETIC]). The signal, not the vol scaling, carries the result: the same sizing with the signal switched off loses 0.3–0.5 of Sharpe and doubles the drawdown (Summitward's independent re-test found a smaller but same-signed increment, [Summitward](https://summitward.com/learn/crypto-trend-following) [UNVERIFIED]).

The skeptic's half of the thesis is just as important: the full-sample numbers are carried by 2020–21 (Sharpe 2.09 in that window) and 2024 (1.88); over 2022-01..2026-09 the rule's Sharpe is 0.84 (weekly variant 0.66–0.92 depending on weekday), over 2025-01..2026-09 it is 0.2–0.6, and the only investable live comparator, the Barclay Cryptocurrency Traders Index, lost 12.3% in 2025 after +54% in 2024 ([The Full FX](https://thefullfx.com/ctas-end-2025-on-a-positive-note/) [UNVERIFIED]). Swinkels reported crypto TSMOM "seems to have disappeared after the introduction of bitcoin futures" ([Tinbergen abstract](https://utilities.tinbergen.nl/?p=66235) [UNVERIFIED]), Grobys et al. warn that crypto momentum's tail variance is undefined so Sharpe ratios overstate it ([Springer FMPM 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9) [UNVERIFIED]), and every other candidate engine the user has tried or researched failed its own test: the market-making bot lost −3.6 bps net per fill on its first real-money run because faster traders hit its stale quotes (`2026-10-13-live-run-pickoff.md`), daily option selling has no out-of-sample edge (`options-research/README.md`), and funding carry's Sharpe went from 6.45 (2020–25) to negative in 2025 (Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14435) [UNVERIFIED]). So the design assumes a **forward Sharpe of 0.5–0.9**, not 1.25, sizes for that, and spends its effort on not losing money to complexity, operations and taxes rather than on finding more alpha.

## 2. The rule (complete specification; another quant can implement it from this section alone)

### 2.1 Universe and venue
- **Venue:** Delta Exchange India mainnet (FIU-IND registered per the exchange's own statements [UNVERIFIED against the register]), INR-funded, USD-quoted perpetuals. No foreign venue (FEMA/LRS problem, `risk_execution_india_regime.md` KQ4), no spot, no options.
- **Universe (fixed, 9 coins):** BTCUSD, ETHUSD, XRPUSD, BNBUSD, ADAUSD, LTCUSD, LINKUSD, DOGEUSD, BCHUSD — exactly the pre-registered set (coins with perps since January 2020, all listed on Delta). Contract values (Delta `/v2/products`, per the 2026-10-08 memo): BTC 0.001, BNB 0.1, ETH 0.01, LTC 0.1, LINK 1, DOGE 100, BCH 0.01, XRP 1, ADA 1. **Why not fewer coins:** BTC-only and BTC+ETH match the 9-coin Sharpe over the full sample (1.19 / 1.18 vs 1.25) but fall apart out of sample on Delta 2024-08..2026-10 (0.57 / 0.71 vs 0.99) and in 2025 (−8% / +8% vs +3%) [SYNTHETIC, §4 and Appendix A]. The eight extra coins add robustness, not return, and at this account size their positions are a few dollars each.
- **Removal rule:** a coin leaves only if Delta delists it or its 24 h turnover is below $0.5M for 30 consecutive days *(start)*; it is replaced by nothing (N shrinks). No additions, ever, for performance reasons.

### 2.2 Signal (identical to `cex-mm/scripts/trend_backtest.py::target_weights`)
At the decision time T (§2.5), using Delta daily closes up to and including the candle that closed at T (≥121 closes required):
```
s_i  = mean( sign(P_T/P_{T−20d} − 1), sign(P_T/P_{T−60d} − 1), sign(P_T/P_{T−120d} − 1) )   ∈ {−1, −⅓, ⅓, 1}
sig_i = max(s_i, 0)                                                                         ∈ {0, ⅓, ⅔, 1}   (long/flat, no shorts)
vol_i = sqrt( 365 × mean_{k=0..59} ln(P_{T−k}/P_{T−k−1})² )                                 (60-day realized, annualized)
```
Parameters LOOKS = (20, 60, 120), VOL_WIN = 60, vol floor 5%. **Nothing is tuned.** The memo notes the 60-day lookback alone scored best on Delta; using it would be selection after the fact, so the three-lookback ensemble stays (it also had the better drawdown: single-60 gave max DD −40% on BTC+ETH, Appendix A).

### 2.3 Position sizing
```
TV      = 0.25  *(start)*        # the code's TARGET_VOL parameter; 0.40 is the native setting in the memo
w_i     = sig_i × (TV / N) / max(vol_i, 0.05)          # fraction of STRATEGY CAPITAL E (on-venue + off-venue, §2.5), N = 9
gross   = Σ w_i ;  if gross > 1.0, scale every w_i by 1.0/gross      # hard cap 1.0x *(start)*; historical max ≈ 0.55x at TV 0.25
contracts_i = round( w_i × E / (contract_value_i × P_T) )            # whole Delta contracts, nearest
```
- Realized vol of the rule is about 0.47 × TV (memo; §4), so TV 0.25 means **~10–12% annualized vol, average gross 0.12x of capital, maximum ~0.55x**. This is a drawdown-tolerance choice, not an alpha choice: target vol leaves Sharpe unchanged and scales return and drawdown together (memo grid; Bloomberg Crypto Insights found the same for BTC/ETH [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Capital levels and rounding** (Delta prices, weekly, 2024-08-10..2026-10-12, [SYNTHETIC], run R3c): at TV 0.25, $500 → Sharpe 0.99, CAGR +10.2%, max DD −6.9%, 100 trades/yr; $1,000 → 1.06 / +11.3% / −9.0% / 130 trades/yr; $2,000 → 0.94; $5,000 → 0.99; $20,000 → 1.01; unrounded 1.01. Rounding is noise from $500 up. At $1,000 the per-coin target when a coin is fully "on" is ≈ $55–65, so BTC (~$83/contract) and BNB (~$76) are 0-or-1-contract positions; that is already in the numbers above.
- No per-coin cap (a 25% cap never binds at TV ≤ 0.40, D2's check `calc/d2_checks_output.txt`), no drawdown de-leveraging (D2: `mult = clip(1 − dd/dd_max, 0.25, 1)` at dd_max 0.10–0.30 lowered Sharpe 1.25 → 1.13–1.23 and CAGR; it is a vol cut, not an improvement), no regime filter (§7).

### 2.4 Risk rules (own account)
1. **No intraday circuit breaker.** On the hourly series a 1.5% day-stop cuts the rule's Sharpe from 1.12 to 0.88 (weekly) and 1.25 to 1.05 (daily); 2% and 3% stops are within noise of no stop (Appendix A). The stop only pays when a third party imposes a daily limit (§3). Instead, size so the worst day is survivable: at TV 0.25 the worst backtest day is −5.0% (2021-05-19) and days below −2.5% happen about 2–3 times a year [SYNTHETIC from R3a scaled by 0.625: 16 native days below −4% in 6.4 years].
2. **Leverage ceiling:** set the leverage on every Delta product to **5x or lower**; never change it upward. Gross exposure ≤ 1.0x of strategy capital (rule), which with 30% of capital on-venue is ≤ 3.3x on-venue; typical on-venue utilisation is 0.4x.
3. **Drawdown kill switch:** if strategy equity (on-venue + off-venue) is **20% below its peak** *(start; backtest max DD at TV 0.25 is −14% daily / −17% weekly, so 20% is ~1.3–1.5× the backtest)*, flatten everything with reduce-only market orders, stop, and do not restart for at least 4 weeks or until a written review (§5) says why it happened. Restart only at half size for 13 weeks.
4. **Capital placement:** keep **≤ 30% of strategy capital on Delta** *(start)*; the rest in a bank FD / liquid fund. Top up on-venue cash only after a weekly rebalance if margin utilisation exceeds 50% [SYNTHETIC: max gross 0.55x × 20% margin at 5x = 11% of capital, plus a 30% adverse move on 0.55x = 16.5%, fits inside 30%]. This is also the "do nothing" benchmark built into the design: 70% of the money keeps earning the FD rate.
5. **Missed rebalance rule:** if Monday is missed, execute on the next day using that day's 00:00 UTC close; never "wait for a better price". Two consecutive misses count as an operational failure (§5).
6. **Venue is the source of truth** for positions and PnL (CLAUDE.md). The ledger is Delta's statement export, reconciled weekly against a spreadsheet of the model's weights. `artifacts/economics/*.json` is not used.
7. **No discretionary overrides.** Not for FOMC, not for ETF flows, not for "obvious" tops. The only human decisions are: execute, or stop under rule 3 / §5.

### 2.5 Execution
- **Decision time:** the Delta daily candle that closes at **Monday 00:00 UTC = Monday 05:30 IST** (in the backtest this is "offset 3", the close of the Sunday UTC candle). The weekday is chosen for operations (start of the user's week, funding has just been exchanged at 05:30 IST), **not for performance**: it is in fact the weakest of the seven weekdays over 2022–26 (Sharpe 0.66 vs 0.75–0.92 for the others), and it is kept precisely because picking the best weekday would be curve-fitting (Appendix A, run R2b). Expected performance is the seven-weekday average (full-sample Sharpe 1.23, 2022–26 0.81, Delta 2024–26 0.98), not any single line.
- **Why weekly, not daily:** the pre-registered rule rebalances daily (1,773 trades/yr unrounded, 1.1%/yr fees). Rebalancing the same rule weekly gives the same Sharpe on average (seven weekdays 1.12–1.34, mean 1.23 vs 1.25 daily) with 273 trades/yr and 0.4%/yr fees, and on Delta out of sample 0.69–1.15 (mean 0.98) vs 0.99 daily [SYNTHETIC, R2b]. Daily rebalancing therefore buys nothing except a bot, and a bot is the user's largest documented operational risk (the Binance feed froze silently for 3.5 days on 8–12 Oct; `2026-10-12-live-stop-rule.md`). The cost of weekly is dispersion: which weekday you land on moves the realized path (max DD −15.6% to −27.3% across weekdays at native vol) — that is path luck, not edge, and the sizing in §2.3 already assumes the worse end.
- **How:** manual, on the Delta web/app, Monday between 05:35 and ~07:00 IST. Compute the nine contract counts (a 20-line Python call into `trend_backtest.target_weights` on the Delta candles, or the spreadsheet in §6 step 2), compare with the open positions, and send the differences as **market orders** (taker, 5.31 bps incl. GST + modelled 2 bps slippage; BTC/ETH touch is 0.06/0.18 bps, alts 1–4 bps, so a limit order at the touch is optional, never required). At $1,000 this is 2–3 orders a week. Funding is paid automatically on longs (BTC/ETH capped at 0.01%/8h = 10.95%/yr; alts have higher caps; `risk_execution_india_regime.md` KQ2).
- **Post-only vs taker:** taker. The strategy turns over ~0.6x of capital a year at TV 0.25 (fees 0.2%/yr); maker discipline would save ~0.1%/yr and add a fill-chasing problem.
- **Mapping onto the repo:** the signal and sizing are `cex-mm/scripts/trend_backtest.py::target_weights` (read-only use); `scripts/trend_paper.py` already fetches Delta closes, books and funding and sizes whole contracts off paper equity — a `--weekly` decision flag and a "print the order list, place nothing" mode are the only code changes this design asks for, and they are optional. **Not used:** `mm-bot`, `mm-oems`, the DISM profile, the Binance anchor, the web UI. If the user later automates, the daily pre-registered rule with a 10% no-trade band (D2: identical Sharpe, 531 trades/yr) is the equivalent bot version; nothing in this design depends on it.

## 3. Prop-firm fit

**Position:** a prop challenge is a lottery ticket whose price is positive-EV only if the strategy's forward Sharpe is near 1 and the rules are obeyed to the letter; it is negative-EV in a 2025–26-type regime, and it carries unregulated counterparty risk (80–100 forex/futures firms shut in 2024–25, FundingTicks changed rules retroactively in Dec 2025 and closed in Jan 2026; `prop_firm_landscape.md` KQ3 [UNVERIFIED]). So: **no challenge before 26 live weeks on own money (§6 step 7), then at most one account at a time, at a $25k size first, treated as a consumable.** Every number below is [SYNTHETIC] from `tools/prop_sim.py` on the hourly mark-to-market of the weekly rule (`d3_runs/hourly_9c_weekly_off3.csv`, native TV 0.40, 2020-05..2026-09, stationary block bootstrap, 3,000 paths × 365 days; hourly granularity understates intra-hour breaches; $100k account, 80% split unless stated; the funded phase is 12 months with 80% of profit withdrawn every 30 days). The firm rules themselves are [THIRD-PARTY]/[UNVERIFIED] and must be re-read on the firm's dashboard on the day of purchase.

**Leverage multiplier k** scales the native series: k = 0.5 means TV 0.20 in the code and ~10% realized vol.

### 3.1 Breakout Classic 1-step (Kraken-owned since 1 Sep 2025; 10% target, 3% daily loss on equity vs the 00:30 UTC balance excluding open positions, 6% static max DD, no time limit, no minimum days, no consistency rule, 80% split, $999 at $100k / $275 at $25k; 0.04% per side + 0.033%/day swap on positions open at 00:00 UTC; [THIRD-PARTY]/[UNVERIFIED], sources in the addendum note)
- **Compliance:** yes for the rule as written (daily-to-weekly long/flat, no hedging, no copy trading, no HFT). Two open points to get in writing before paying: (a) whether a self-built, once-a-week, manually executed systematic rule is permitted (the "third-party/off-the-shelf strategy" clause; bots/API status is unresolved — weekly manual execution sidesteps it); (b) whether perp funding/swap counts against the daily limit (it does in the model).
- **Vol and circuit breaker:** **k = 0.5 (TV 0.20, ~10% vol) with a portfolio-level 1.5% day-stop** (flatten for the rest of the UTC day when equity is 1.5% below the day's 00:00 UTC level). The day-stop is non-negotiable here even though it costs Sharpe: without it 23% of paths breach the 3% daily limit within a year at k = 0.5 (control run), with it 0%. Because positions are ~0.01–0.03 of equity each, a per-position stop-loss cannot implement a 1.5% portfolio loss; the user needs an equity alert from the platform or a read-only watcher. **If the firm offers neither, do not buy.**
- **Pass probability and time:** 59% (full-sample bootstrap), 51% (every real 365-day window), **7% in a 2025-01..2026-09-only bootstrap**; 37–39% pass within 180 days; median time to pass 141 days (86 in the historical windows), p90 ~300 days; 23–45% of paths are still open after a year. Max-DD breaches 18% (bootstrap) / 4% (historical).
- **Funded survival (12 months):** 56% (bootstrap) / 53% (historical) / 45% (2025–26 regime). Expected payout per funded account over 12 months: 10.6% / 10.1% / 3.2% of the account.
- **Fee EV per challenge:** **+$5,260** full-sample bootstrap, **+$4,140** historical windows, **−$775** in the 2025–26 regime at $100k; **+$1,290** at $25k full-sample (≈ −$220 in the 2025–26 regime [SYNTHETIC: 0.071 × 3.15% × $25,000 − $275]). A higher k (0.75, ~15% vol) has a slightly higher EV in the full sample (+$6,632) but 3× the funded-death rate (31% survival) and is the first thing that breaks in a bad regime; a lower k (0.35) passes only 42% and leaves 39% of paths unfinished after a year.
- **Reset exposure:** the daily limit is set from the 00:30 UTC *balance excluding open positions* [UNVERIFIED], so an open loser carries its whole floating loss into the new day. D1's "realize losses before the reset" manoeuvre is not in this design (it adds a daily action and a taker cost); the 1.5% day-stop already caps the carried loss.

### 3.2 HyroTrader 1-step (10% target, 4% daily incl. unrealized, 6% max loss, mandatory stop-loss within 5 minutes of every trade, 3% max loss per trade, 25% max exposure per position, 40% consistency rule in the evaluation, 70% split, 5%-of-balance payout cap per cycle, fee $579–$849 at $100k refundable with the first payout; daily limit trailing by default with a paid "Swing" static option; max loss static or trailing depending on source [CONFLICT, UNVERIFIED])
- **Compliance:** feasible but fiddly: nine resting stop-loss orders placed within 5 minutes of each weekly rebalance (at 25% below entry each stop realizes <1% of equity, under the 3% per-trade rule); the 25% exposure cap never binds at k = 0.5 (max single-coin weight ~3%); the 40% consistency rule is satisfied (best day at k = 0.5 is +2.5%, 25% of a 10% target).
- **Numbers at k = 0.5, 2% day-stop:** if the 6% max loss is **trailing** (as modelled from the sources), pass 47%, funded 12-month survival **3%**, EV +$1,276 (the account is expected to die before the second payout); if it is **static**, pass 59%, survival 60%, EV +$4,946. **Decision: only after written confirmation that the max loss is static from the initial balance;** otherwise no.

### 3.3 Crypto Fund Trader 1-step (10% / 4% daily / 6% trailing that locks at the opening balance once +6%; bans HFT, tick scalping, latency exploitation, arbitrage; FINMA warning list since 23 Aug 2024; "educational platform" terms; documented denied-withdrawal complaints [THIRD-PARTY])
- **Numbers at k = 0.5, 2% day-stop:** pass 47%, funded survival 52%, EV +$4,490. **Decision: no, regardless of the numbers** — the counterparty evidence is the worst of the three and the terms allow payouts to be denied.

### 3.4 Sequencing and what the prop route is worth
Breakout $25k first ($275 at risk), then $100k only after a $25k payout has actually arrived in India (it tests the KYC, the USDC rail and the INR conversion with 30% VDA tax + 1% TDS on the stablecoin leg, `prop_firm_landscape.md` KQ2 [UNVERIFIED]). At most two attempts per calendar year; a failed attempt in a 2025–26-type regime is expected and is not a reason to raise k. Honest value of the whole route at $100k: roughly a coin flip to earn a few thousand dollars over a year, costing ~$1,000 per flip, plus an unquantified probability that the firm does not pay.

## 5. Failure modes, what the user will see, kill criteria

### 5.1 Failure modes and their signature

### 5.2 Kill criteria (any one is sufficient; "stop" means flatten with reduce-only orders, withdraw to ≤ 10% on-venue, write one page on why, no restart for 4 weeks)
1. **Drawdown:** strategy equity 20% below its peak at TV 0.25 *(start)* (§2.4 rule 3).
2. **Statistical:** rolling **24-month** net return ≤ 0 **while BTC's own 24-month return is > 0** (i.e., the rule failed in a market where trend should have paid), or rolling 12-month Sharpe below −1.0 (the backtest's worst calendar year was −0.56).
3. **Tracking:** live equity vs the model's unrounded weekly equity (same rebalance days, 7.31 bps/side modelled) diverges by more than 3 percentage points over any 26 weeks, or realized cost per rebalance exceeds 2× the model for 4 consecutive weeks. This is an execution failure, not an edge failure; fix the process before trading again.
4. **Operational:** two consecutive missed rebalances; any position the user cannot explain from the order list; any change to product leverage above 5x.
5. **Venue/legal:** any withdrawal taking more than 7 business days without a stated reason; Delta India losing FIU registration; a CBDT/court position that perp gains are VDA income with no loss set-off **and** the trailing 24-month pre-tax return is below 12% (below that the after-tax asymmetry makes the strategy worse than an FD [SYNTHETIC]).
6. **Research:** an in-house re-run of the pre-registered rule on data through the latest quarter showing 2022-onward Sharpe below 0.3 on all 18 grid settings (today: 0.66–0.92 for the weekly weekdays, 0.84 daily).

What would make me abandon the thesis rather than pause: criterion 2 or 6 firing after at least 104 live weeks, or a replication showing BTC/ETH TSMOM at 1–6-month lookbacks net of 10 bps costs has Sharpe ≤ 0.3 on 2022–2026 across venues.

## 8. Plain-English explanation

**What we do.** Once a week, on Monday morning, we look at nine of the biggest cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin, Ethereum and seven others that trade on Delta Exchange India). For each one we ask three simple questions: is the price higher than it was 20 days ago, 60 days ago, and 120 days ago? If the answer is "yes" to all three, we hold a full-size position in that coin; "yes" to two, two-thirds size; one, a third; none, we hold nothing in it. "Full size" is small on purpose: it is set so that a coin that swings a lot gets a smaller position than one that is calm, and so that the whole account moves only about a tenth as much as Bitcoin itself. We place the handful of orders by hand, log them in a spreadsheet, and do nothing else until next Monday. Most of the money never touches the exchange; it sits in a bank deposit, and only the slice needed as margin is on Delta.

**Why it should make money.** Crypto prices tend to keep moving in the direction they have been moving for the last one to four months more often than chance would predict. This has been found over and over in the academic research and it is the one idea in this whole project that still worked after we charged realistic fees, funding payments and slippage on Delta's own prices. Because we only ever hold or stay out (never bet on a fall), and because we shrink positions when markets get wild, at the size we run it the strategy has historically made about a third of Bitcoin's yearly gain with about a quarter of its worst fall. Sitting out the bad stretches, like 2022, is the whole point: the rule was flat in May 2022, August 2024 and February 2026.

**What can go wrong.** Markets that lurch up and down without a trend, as in 2022 and most of 2025–26, make the rule buy and sell at the wrong moments; those years lose a few percent or make almost nothing. A sudden crash while we are invested costs a bad day of three to five percent, and there is no intraday escape hatch by design. The idea itself may be fading as more professional money arrives. The exchange can have outages, the tax treatment of these contracts in India is unsettled, and a "funded trader" firm, if we ever use one, might not pay. The biggest risk, honestly, is the person: skipping a Monday, doubling the size after a good run, or adding a clever extra rule because the simple one feels boring.

**How much to expect.** On $1,000 of your own money, a realistic year is +$60 to +$110 before tax; a great year like 2024 about +$240; a bad year like 2022 about −$70. On $5,000, five times that. That is pocket money, and it is meant to be: the first six months are a test of whether you can run the rule exactly, every week. If you can, the same rule on a $100,000 prop-firm account has roughly even odds of passing their test and then paying out several thousand dollars in a normal year, for about a thousand dollars of entry fees per attempt, and poor odds in a year like 2025. If the account falls 20% from its best level, or two years pass with no profit while Bitcoin itself has gone up, we stop and rethink rather than press on.
