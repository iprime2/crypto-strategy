## 1. Thesis

The one directional effect in crypto with repeated academic support, a pre-registered after-cost result on our own venue, and a shape that fits prop-firm rules is **time-series momentum, long-only, volatility-scaled**. The repo's rule (fixed before testing: mean of sign(20/60/120-day return), 40%/N target vol over 60-day realized vol, taker 5.31 bps + 2 bps slippage, funding) earned CAGR +24.2%, Sharpe 1.25, max DD −21.9% on Binance 2020-05..2026-09 and Sharpe 1.15 on Delta prices 2024-08..2026-09, with all 18 grid settings positive (`cex-mm/artifacts/research/2026-10-08-trend-following-backtest.md`; Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen 2012 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003; Liu & Tsyvinski https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf; Han–Kang–Ryu "TSMOM strong, CS weak under realistic costs" https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf; Zarattini–Pagani–Barbon Donchian ensemble at 25% vol Sharpe ~1.5 https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5209907 [UNVERIFIED]). The prop edge is not the alpha itself but the **mismatch between the firm's rules and the population that buys challenges**: pass rates are 5–17% per attempt and ~7% of buyers are ever paid (FPFX via https://www.tradingview.com/news/financemagnates:3a251e333094b:0-exclusive-only-7-of-300-000-prop-trading-accounts-achieved-payouts/ [UNVERIFIED]), 70–79% of failures are daily-loss breaches (OneFunded, hoc-trade, prop_challenge_math.md KQ2), and the challenge-math note shows pass probability with no time limit is monotonically increasing as volatility falls (two-barrier formula, prop_challenge_math.md KQ3). Breakout Classic has no time limit, no minimum days, no consistency rule, a 3% daily loss on balance and a 6% static max DD (the firm's program-rules page as quoted in prop_challenge_math.md KQ7, https://www.breakoutprop.com/program-rules/, plus CryptoSlate/PropTradingVibes via prop_firm_landscape.md and the addendum [search snippets only; UNVERIFIED on the page]). A Sharpe-1.25 strategy run at ~10–14% realized vol, with a hard 1.5% day-stop, passes that rule set 58–63% of the time in the local simulator versus a ~14% industry base rate, which is where the positive fee EV comes from (local_evidence.md; runs A, G2, H below [SYNTHETIC]). The thesis is weakest exactly where the evidence is: 2025 (+2.7% at native vol) and 2026 YTD (+7.5%) are two weak years in a row, the Barclay Crypto Traders Index lost 12.3% in 2025 (practitioner_strategies.md KQ1 [UNVERIFIED]), and in a 2025-26-like regime the challenge is roughly zero-EV (run D). The design therefore buys the cheap option (one $275–999 fee at a time) and treats the funded account as a consumable that is expected to be lost and re-bought.

### 2.2 Signal (unchanged from `scripts/trend_backtest.py`)
At the 00:00 UTC daily close of day *d*, for each coin *i* with ≥121 daily closes:
```
s_i(d) = mean( sign(P_d / P_{d-20} - 1), sign(P_d / P_{d-60} - 1), sign(P_d / P_{d-120} - 1) )
s_i    = max(s_i, 0)                                   # long-only: values in {0, 1/3, 2/3, 1}
vol_i  = sqrt( 365 * mean over k=0..59 of ln(P_{d-k}/P_{d-k-1})^2 )    # 60-day realized vol, annualized
```
Parameters: LOOKS = (20, 60, 120), VOL_WIN = 60, vol floor 5%. **No parameter is tuned.** (The memo notes the 60-day-only lookback scored best on Delta; choosing it would be selection bias, so the fixed rule stays.)

### 2.3 Position sizing
```
TV      = 0.30 in the challenge phase, 0.20 once funded   *(start)*   # the code's TARGET_VOL parameter
                                                                      # realized vol ≈ 0.47 × TV: ~14% and ~9.4% [SYNTHETIC, full sample]
w_i     = s_i × (TV / N) / max(vol_i, 0.05)                           # fraction of equity, per coin
w_i     = min(w_i, 0.25)                                              # per-coin cap 25% of equity *(start)*; never bound historically
gross   = Σ w_i ; if gross > 1.0 scale all w_i by 1.0/gross           # gross cap 1.0x *(start)*; historical max at TV 0.30 is 0.66x
fac     = clip( (equity − F_s) / 0.06 , 0.33 , 1.0 )                  # drawdown de-leveraging toward the static floor F_s *(start)*
target_notional_i = w_i × fac × equity
contracts_i       = round( target_notional_i / (price_i × contract_value_i) )
```
- `F_s` = the firm's static max-drawdown floor = 0.94 × initial balance (Breakout Classic). `fac` is 1.0 at or above the starting balance, 0.5 at a 3% drawdown, 0.33 at a 4% drawdown and below (Grossman–Zhou surplus logic, prop_challenge_math.md KQ3 item 8; repo `resource/05` §6.2 uses the same `clip(1 − dd/dd_max, 0.25, 1)` form).
- Historical gross exposure at TV 0.40 (hourly series 2020-05..2026-09): mean 0.20x, median 0.15x, p90 0.51x, p99 0.71x, max 0.88x (2023-11-06); flat 18% of the time. At TV 0.30 multiply by 0.75, at TV 0.20 by 0.5 [SYNTHETIC]. Breakout's 5x leverage cap and HyroTrader's 25%-per-position cap are never approached.
- **Contract rounding at the user's capital levels** (Delta contract values: BTC 0.001 ≈ $83, BNB 0.1 ≈ $76, ETH 0.01 ≈ $25, LTC 0.1, LINK 1, DOGE 100, BCH 0.01, XRP 1, ADA 1; memo): at TV 0.20 on $1,000 the gross is ~$100 across 9 coins, so BTC and BNB round to zero most days — do not run TV 0.20 below ~$5,000 of own capital. $1,000–2,500: TV 0.40 (memo: $1,000 → CAGR +18.2%, Sharpe 1.08, DD −15.2% on Delta 2024-08..2026-09; rounding cost < 1.5%/yr). $5,000: TV 0.20 or 0.40 (memo: $5,000 at TV 0.40 → Sharpe 1.15, DD −13.7%). $100,000 funded at TV 0.20: ~$10k gross ≈ $1.1k per coin ≈ 13 BTC contracts; rounding negligible. Breakout's contract sizes are [UNVERIFIED].
- **No-trade band** *(start)*: skip a coin's trade if |Δcontracts| < 1 or |Δnotional| < 10% of its target notional (cuts turnover; the backtest rebalances fully every day, so this is a cost saving not modelled, expected small).

### 2.4 Risk rules
1. **Intraday day-stop 1.5%** *(start)*. Let `B` = balance and `E0` = equity at the firm's daily reset (Breakout 00:30 UTC: prop_challenge_math.md KQ7 quotes the firm's program-rules page, "recalculated at 12:30 AM (00:30) UTC, based on the balance at that moment with open positions excluded", and the addendum repeats it from PropTradingVibes/CryptoSlate but classes the time as secondary, so [UNVERIFIED until read on the page]; HyroTrader 00:00 UTC; CFT 00:05 UTC). Firm daily floor `F_d = 0.97 × B` (Breakout measures the base on **balance with open positions excluded**, enforces on equity including floating P&L). Internal stop level:
   ```
   S = max( E0 × (1 − 0.015), F_d + 0.005 × B, F_s + 0.005 × B )
   ```
   When equity < S: **flatten every position with reduce-only market orders and stay flat until the next reset.** If at the reset `E0 − S < 0.005 × B` (less than 0.5% of room, which only happens when carrying a large unrealized loss), open nothing that day. The simulator models the simpler `E0 × 0.985` form against 00:00 UTC equity; the extra terms only make it stricter.
   - Fallback when no API or no one is awake: per-position stop orders at distance `d = 0.015 / gross_fraction` below the current price (e.g. gross 0.15x → 10%). Under perfect correlation this equals the portfolio stop; under imperfect correlation it is looser. This fallback is also HyroTrader's mandatory stop-loss (set within 5 minutes of every fill; risk per trade `d × w_i ≤ 1.5% × w_i/gross ≤ 3%` ✓).
   - Why 1.5% and not 1% or 2%: full-sample simulator at k=0.5 / 0.75 gives EV $5,897 / $5,826 at 1.5% (run A), $5,647 / $6,297 at 1.0% (run B), $4,818 / $4,340 at 2.0% and $3,129 / $822 with no stop (local_evidence.md). 1.0% trips the stop ~2x as often (k=0.75: 29.6 days/yr with intraday low < −1% vs 15.4 days/yr < −1.5% [SYNTHETIC]); 1.5% leaves a 1.5% buffer to the firm's 3% for gaps. The simulator checks hourly, so in 3 single hours out of 56,256 (2020-11-26 03:00, 2021-02-23 08:00, 2024-03-05 19:00; raw hourly moves ≈ −4.7%) a path jumps from above −1.5% to below −3% between checks and is counted as a firm breach (debug run `d1_runs/debug_breach.txt`); treat that as the model's stand-in for stop slippage in a cascade.
2. **Realize losses before the reset (Breakout only)** *(start)*: at 00:20 UTC, if `(B_prev − equity) / B_prev > 1%`, close all positions and immediately re-open at target so the 00:30 balance equals equity. Rationale: Breakout's daily base is **balance** (open P&L excluded) while the limit is enforced on equity, so an open loss of L% compresses the day's room to (3 − L)%; realizing it restores 3% [SYNTHETIC reading of the rule]. Never realize gains for this purpose (an open gain makes the floor more lenient). Cost: Breakout charges 0.04% per buy or sell inside the evaluation (help-center article https://intercom.help/breakoutprop/en/articles/11647195 via the addendum [UNVERIFIED: not read]), so a close-and-re-open costs 2 × 0.04% × gross ≈ 1.2 bps of equity at the typical 0.15x gross, or ≈ 2 bps at the backtest's 7.31 bps/side [SYNTHETIC]. The 0.033%/day swap (item 6) is charged at 00:25 UTC on positions held at 00:00 UTC, so the manoeuvre neither avoids nor doubles it. Folded into the daily rebalance when it fires.
3. **Drawdown de-leveraging** `fac` (above), applied in both phases. Effect in the simulator at k=0.5 (run G2 vs A): 12-month funded survival 60.0% → 92.5%, expected payout 11.28% → 10.29% of account, challenge pass 61.1% → 58.7% with max-DD failures 15.6% → 3.0% and "still open at 365 d" 23% → 38%. At k=0.75: pass 63.1% → 63.2%, max-DD failures 26.7% → 13.5%, funded survival 26.8% → 47.2% [SYNTHETIC]. The min fraction 0.33 vs 0.5 (run G3) is a judgment call: 0.33 buys survival (92.5% vs 84.0%) for 0.17 pp of payout.
4. **Regime filter: none.** The long-only signal is itself the filter (flat 18% of hours historically). The BTC-200-day and realized-vol filters exist as options in `tools/trend_hourly.py` but were not pre-registered and were not run for this design; adding one after seeing results would be selection.
5. **Kill switches** (stop and review before any re-entry):
   - K1: equity ≤ 95.0% of initial balance (1 pp above the 6% floor) → flat, no re-entry on this account; decide whether to re-buy.
   - K2: three day-stop trips within any 10 days → TV halved for the next 30 days (chop regime).
   - K3: realized tracking error vs the model equity (the rule's unrounded weights held mid-to-mid with modelled costs, as `trend_paper.py` computes) exceeds 2× the modelled 7.31 bps per rebalance side over 20 rebalances → stop; fix plumbing or re-cost.
   - K4: the firm changes any rule the simulator assumed (daily %, base, static vs trailing, min days, instruments, automation policy, financing) → flat until re-simulated.
   - K5: a payout request is delayed beyond the firm's stated window or denied → withdraw everything allowed, do not buy another challenge from that firm.
6. **Funding / financing:** positions are carried across funding timestamps; the backtest paid 3.4%/yr of equity at TV 0.40 (≈2.55% at TV 0.30, ≈1.7% at TV 0.20) in Binance funding plus 1.1%/yr of trading fees at 7.31 bps/side. Breakout instead charges, inside the evaluation, **0.04% per buy or sell and a swap of 0.033% per day per open position held at 00:00 UTC, deducted at 00:25 UTC**, five minutes before the 00:30 reset (official help-center article via the addendum [UNVERIFIED: not read]; these are notional in the evaluation, i.e. they reduce evaluation performance, no cash is paid). Costed on the strategy's own gross exposure at 00:00 UTC (run `d1_runs/I_breakout_swap_cost.txt` [SYNTHETIC]): mean gross 0.198x at TV 0.40 (p50 0.15x, p90 0.51x, p99 0.71x, flat 17% of days) → swap **1.79%/yr of equity at TV 0.30 and 1.19%/yr at TV 0.20**, by year 0.45–2.76% (TV 0.30); the implied turnover (15x equity/yr one-way) costs 0.45%/yr at 0.04%/side vs 0.8% modelled. Breakout's cost basis is therefore ≈ 1.1%/yr (TV 0.30) and ≈ 0.75%/yr (TV 0.20) **below** what the simulator's return series already pays, which leaves that much room for terminal slippage before the §3 tables become optimistic. The swap is a pure debit, whereas exchange funding can be received; no re-simulation is needed. The debit lands in the old day's P&L before the 00:30 base is struck, so it lowers the new base by ≈ 0.5 bps of equity at typical gross (immaterial). HyroTrader passes through real Bybit funding and fees.

### 2.5 Execution
- **When:** once per UTC day, 00:00–00:25 UTC (05:30–05:55 IST): 00:00–00:05 compute targets from the completed 00:00 close; 00:05–00:20 rebalance (merge the realize-at-reset close/re-open if it fires), then place/adjust stop orders; 00:30 Breakout reset. Checked against the daily close the rule was backtested on; no intraday signal.
- **How:** reduce-only market or marketable-limit orders for the rebalance (the backtest charges taker 5.31 bps + 2 bps slippage; a post-only limit may be tried for up to 10 minutes, then cross). Day-stop exits are always taker, reduce-only. On Delta the project invariant "quotes post-only, taker only for reduce-only exits" does not apply to this strategy's entries; the entries are a daily directional rebalance costed at taker.
- **Manual vs bot:** on Breakout the terminal is proprietary ("backed by Kraken's infrastructure", DXtrade per one review) and the **bot/API policy is unresolved**: third-party guides conflict (bots allowed but no MT4/MT5 so EAs cannot run; "no bot support"; third-party bots "Restricted"; "API abuse detection"), while Breakout itself publishes "Maximize Your Returns With Algorithmic Prop Trading" (https://www.breakoutprop.com/article/algorithmic-prop-trading/, not read) (addendum; prop_firm_landscape.md Gaps [UNVERIFIED]). The program-rules page prohibits "any third-party or off-the-shelf strategy, or one marketed to pass evaluations", copy trading, latency exploitation and account sharing; secondary reviews add HFT and cross-account hedging (addendum). A self-built daily rebalance is none of these, but TSMOM is a published idea, so keep the repo's dated backtest memo and code as evidence that the strategy is the trader's own. **Design for manual daily execution from a target sheet (≈15 minutes/day) plus the per-position stop fallback, which needs no bot and sidesteps the question; ask Breakout support in writing, before paying, whether a self-built once-a-day systematic strategy executed manually or by the trader's own script is permitted (§6 step 1); automate the same sheet only with that answer in hand.** On HyroTrader bot and API access is "fully open" per its own blog (https://www.hyrotrader.com/blog/hyrotrader-vs-breakout/ via the addendum), on the trader's own Bybit sub-account API or the CLEO platform on Binance data, so the existing Python can place the orders directly.
- **Mapping onto the repo:** signal and sizing are `trend_backtest.target_weights(close, d, LOOKS, VOL_WIN, target_vol, long_only=True)`; daily paper execution with L2-walked fills and the model-equity comparison is `scripts/trend_paper.py` (running since 2026-10-08 on the Tokyo VM at $1,000 paper equity, dashboard `shadow_server.py /trend`). New, not yet written: `scripts/trend_prop.py` that (a) calls `target_weights` with TV 0.30/0.20, (b) reads account equity/balance and applies `fac`, the day-stop level `S` and the realize-at-reset flag, (c) prints the target sheet in the firm's contract units with stop prices, and (d) places orders where an API exists. The market-making stack (`mm-core`, `mm-bot`, OEMS) is **not used**.

## 7. What this design deliberately drops, and why

- **The market-making bot live.** The first real-money DISM run lost 4.46 bps per dollar traded, picked off on a fair that lagged Binance by 2–3 bps (`2026-10-13-live-run-pickoff.md`); BTC/ETH spreads on Delta are one tick (0.06/0.18 bps) vs 2.12 bps maker fee; HyroTrader's 5-minute stop mandate and CFT's HFT/tick-scalping ban are incompatible with resting quotes; the Oct 2025 cascade shows stale bids get run over. It stays in the shadow test only.
- **Daily option selling.** `options-research/README.md`: no edge after costs; out-of-sample ETH strangle −16 bps/day (t −2.0).
- **Funding carry / basis / cross-venue arbitrage.** Needs two legs; banned as "arbitrage" at CFT and incompatible with single-account prop products (funding_carry_basis.md KQ6); Sharpe collapsed from 6.45 to negative in 2025 (Borri et al. arXiv 2510.14435 [UNVERIFIED]); from India the foreign leg hits LRS/FEMA.
- **Long/short trend.** Sharpe 0.78 vs 1.25, shorts lost 7% in 2023 (memo); crypto momentum crashes live in the short leg (Grobys et al., Han–Kang–Ryu).
- **Cross-sectional alt momentum / beta-neutral baskets.** Weak after costs, alpha in illiquid shorts, negative out of sample 2025-26 in the one test found (alternative_edges.md KQ7).
- **CFT, 2-step products, time-limited products, Breakout Pro/Turbo.** Trailing 6% DD is fatal (§3.3); Breakout's 2-step is legacy; Turbo's 3% static DD vs 9% target needs daily Sharpe the rule does not have (prop_firm_landscape.md KQ6 inference).
- **Higher leverage to pass faster.** Run A: k 1.0–2.0 lowers pass to 43–51%, funded survival to ≤ 6.5%, EV to ≤ $3.2k; with no day-stop k 1.0 is −$28. The no-time-limit math says lower vol wins.
- **Parameter tuning, regime filters, ML, sentiment, on-chain, funding-as-signal.** Not pre-registered, or no after-cost evidence (academic_factor_evidence.md, alternative_edges.md KQ8). The 60-day-only lookback that scored best on Delta is explicitly not adopted.
- **Delta Scalper Offer, exchange competitions, Hyperliquid vaults, copy trading.** Irrelevant to a daily rule or not a capital source for an Indian retail trader.
- **Running the MM and the trend rule on the same prop account.** One strategy per account; the stop-loss and consistency rules make mixing them unexplainable to the firm.

## 8. Plain-English explanation

**What we do.** Every morning at 5:30 IST we look at nine big cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin, Ether, XRP, BNB, ADA, Litecoin, Chainlink, Dogecoin, Bitcoin Cash) and ask each: is the price higher than 20, 60 and 120 days ago? All three yes: full-size long; two: two-thirds; one: one-third; none: nothing. "Full size" is small on purpose: a normal day moves the account about half a percent and exposure is usually 10–20% of the account. We never bet on prices falling. If the account drops 1.5% in a day we sell everything until tomorrow, and in a drawdown we trade smaller until it recovers.

**Where the money comes from.** Two places. First, crypto trends: prices that have been rising tend to keep rising for weeks, and falling ones we simply avoid. It is the most-studied effect in crypto; our six-year test of this exact rule made about 24% a year at full size (worst fall 22%), or 12% a year (worst fall 11%) at prop-account size. Second, prop firms: for $275–$999 a firm like Breakout gives you a $25,000–$100,000 account; make 10% without ever losing 3% in a day or 6% in total and you keep 80% of future profits. Most buyers fail by trading too big and hitting the daily limit; the rules reward exactly our small, steady, no-deadline style. Our simulations say this rule passes about half the time versus one in seven for the average buyer, and a funded $100k account pays out roughly $9,000–10,000 in its first normal-market year. The edge is buying a cheap option most people waste.

**What can go wrong.** The market can go sideways for a year or two (2022-23, 2025-26); then the rule makes 2–4% a year, the challenge takes most of a year or never passes, and the fee is lost about half the time. A crash like 10 October 2025 can blow through the stop and cost 3–4%. Rules may differ from what review sites say; a firm can change them, delay a payout or shut down, with no recourse from India. Indian tax on prop payouts and crypto derivatives is unsettled and needs an accountant before the first fee. Our test is one sample: expect half to three-quarters of its returns going forward.

**How much to expect.** With $1,000 of your own money: roughly $150–250 in a good year, about $30 in a bad one, worst dip around $200; with $5,000, $400–1,200 a year depending on size. The prop route: pay $275, then $999, spend 15 minutes a day, wait 3–5 months; about half the attempts become a funded account that pays several thousand dollars over the following year before it is lost and bought again. Central case: a few thousand dollars a year of payouts for a few thousand of fees; good case three to five times that; bad case losing $3,000 of fees in a sideways market and stopping.
