## 2. Exact pysystemtrade / Carver mechanics (VERIFIED from source)

### 2.1 EWMAC rule (`systems/provided/rules/ewmac.py`)
```
fast_ewma = price.ewm(span=Lfast, min_periods=1).mean()
slow_ewma = price.ewm(span=Lslow, min_periods=1).mean()
raw_ewmac = fast_ewma - slow_ewma
forecast  = raw_ewmac / vol.ffill()        # vol = daily PRICE-UNIT vol (not %), from robust_vol_calc(price.diff(), 35)
```
Default example speeds: (32,128) and (16,32) in the example wrappers; the system variations are EWMACn = EWMAC(n, 4n) for n ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64}. Vol for the forecast denominator is a 35-day EWMA std of daily price differences (`ewmac_calc_vol`, `vol_days=35`), regardless of the rule's speed (AFTS: "EWMAs from 10–50 days, 30–35 in particular, are best at predicting future volatility").

Fixed forecast scalars (chapter-15 config `futuresconfig.yaml`): ewmac2_8 10.6, ewmac4_16 7.5, ewmac8_32 5.3, ewmac16_64 3.75, ewmac32_128 2.65, ewmac64_256 1.87; carry (90-day smooth) 30. `forecast_cap: 20.0`. AFTS notes give slightly different book values: EWMAC2 12.1, EWMAC4 8.53, EWMAC8 5.95, EWMAC16 4.10, EWMAC32 2.79, EWMAC64 1.91 (Rob's production config: momentum4 8.54, momentum8 5.95, momentum16 4.10, momentum32 2.79, momentum64 1.91 — same numbers, so the "momentum" rules in his live system are the EWMAC(n,4n) family). Scaled forecast = raw × scalar, target average absolute value 10, cap ±20 (forecast floor = −cap).

### 2.2 Breakout rule (`breakout.py`)
`roll_max/min` over `lookback` (default 10, min_periods ceil(lookback/2)); `roll_mean = (max+min)/2`; `output = 40 × (price − roll_mean)/(roll_max − roll_min)`; smoothed by `ewm(span=max(int(lookback/4),1))`. Rob's production uses breakout10/20/40/80/160/320 with forecast scalars 0.60–0.74.

### 2.3 Volatility estimator (`sysquant/estimators/vol.py`)
- `robust_vol_calc(daily_returns, days=35, min_periods=10, vol_abs_min=1e-10, vol_floor=True, floor_min_quant=0.05, floor_min_periods=100, floor_days=500)`: EWMA std (`ewm(adjust=True, span=35, min_periods=10).std()`), then floor at the rolling 5 % quantile of its own last 500 observations (first value 0, ffilled).
- `mixed_vol_calc(days=35, slow_vol_years=20, proportion_of_slow_vol=0.3)`: `vol = 0.3 × ewm(span=20×256).mean(of the 35-day vol) + 0.7 × 35-day EWMA vol`. Rob's production config: `mixed_vol_calc`, days 35, `proportion_of_slow_vol: 0.35`, `backfill: True`.
- Our rule: 60-day simple close-to-close std, annualised ×√365, absolute floor 5 %/yr (`max(vol, 0.05)`).

### 2.4 Position sizing, IDM, FDM, buffering (`docs/backtesting.md`, `systems/buffering.py`, AFTS notes)
- Subsystem position: `N = capped_forecast/10 × capital × IDM × instrument_weight × τ / (multiplier × price × FX × σ%)`. τ = `percentage_vol_target` (chapter-15 example 20 %; Rob's live system 25 %; introduction example 25 %).
- IDM = target risk / realised aggregate risk = 1/√(wΣw) on the instrument-weight vector; chapter-15 six-instrument example 1.89; Rob's ~100-instrument live system 2.75 (config), estimated on the fly (`use_instrument_div_mult_estimates: True`). For our 9 coins with equal weights and average pairwise correlation ρ: IDM = √(9/(1+8ρ)) = 1.34 (ρ 0.5), 1.25 (0.6), 1.17 (0.7), 1.10 (0.8).
- FDM: combined forecast = FDM × Σ w_j × capped_forecast_j, then re-capped at ±20. Chapter-15 config: forecast weights ewmac16_64 0.21, ewmac32_128 0.08, ewmac64_256 0.21, carry 0.50, FDM 1.31. AFTS notes FDM-by-rule-count table: 1 → 1.00, 2 → 1.02, 3 → 1.03, 4 → 1.23, 5 → 1.25, 6 → 1.27, 8 → 1.32, 10 → 1.35, 13 → 1.39 (the 3 → 1.03 entry looks like a transcription slip; treat 1.2–1.3 as the realistic FDM for 3–4 trend speeds). Rob's live per-instrument FDMs: BITCOIN 1.816 with ~40 rule variations.
- Buffering (`systems/buffering.py`): `buffer_method: position` → buffer = 0.10 × |position| (the book's "position inertia", what our 10 % band does); `buffer_method: forecast` (Carver: "more theoretically correct; since the buffer doesn't shrink as we get to zero") → buffer = 0.10 × |average position| where average position = vol_scalar × instrument_weight × IDM = the position at a forecast of +10; `buffer_trade_to_edge: True` → when outside the band trade only to the nearer edge, not to the optimum. Rounded positions: round the lower and upper edges, trade if current position is outside. AFTS formula: B = F × capital × IDM × w × τ /(multiplier × price × FX × σ%), F = 0.10; lower = round(N − B), upper = round(N + B); buy (lower − C) if C < lower; sell (C − upper) if C > upper.
- Rob's live risk overlay (config): `max_risk_fraction_normal_risk 1.75`, `max_risk_fraction_stdev_risk 4.0`, `max_risk_limit_sum_abs_risk 4.0`, `max_risk_leverage 20`. Caps only, never floors.

### 2.5 Vol attenuation (`systems/provided/attenuate_vol/vol_attenuation_forecast_scale_cap.py`, used in Rob's live `run_system.py`)
```
daily_vol      = rawdata.get_daily_percentage_volatility(instrument)
ten_year_vol   = daily_vol.rolling(2500, min_periods=10).mean()
normalised_vol = daily_vol / ten_year_vol
Q              = quantile_of_points_in_data_series(normalised_vol)   # quantile of today's value in its own history to date
multiplier     = 2 - 1.5*Q   (NaN -> 1.0)      # 2.0 at the lowest-vol quantile, 0.5 at the highest
smoothed       = multiplier.ewm(span=10).mean()
raw_forecast_attenuated = raw_forecast * smoothed   (only for rules listed in config `use_attenuation`: all breakout, momentum, normmom, assettrend, relmomentum, mrinasset rules)
```
Carver's stated reason (2021 "My trading system" post, via search summary [UNVERIFIED], https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/12/my-trading-system.html): momentum-like rules perform much worse when vol is high relative to its history. AFTS Strategy 13. Third-party replication: QuantConnect "Futures Trend Following and Carry in Different Risk Regimes" https://www.quantconnect.com/research/15989/... reports Sharpe 0.944 with attenuation vs 0.749 for the plain combined carry+trend over Jul 2020–Jul 2023 only [UNVERIFIED, short sample, vendor]. Carver's own before/after figure was not found.

### 2.6 Dynamic optimisation for small accounts (Rob's live system runs `optimisedPositions` from `systems/provided/dynamic_small_system_optimise/`)
Blog series (search summaries [UNVERIFIED]): https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/06/optimising-my-way-out-of-small-fund.html (idea), https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/06/optimising-portfolios-for-small.html ("EPIC FAIL" of the first integer-constrained version), https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/10/mr-greedy-and-tale-of-minimum-tracking.html (greedy minimum-tracking-error-variance algorithm, from Douglas Hohner), November 2021 post: adding a shadow cost helps only modestly. Method: given unrounded optimal weights w*, covariance Σ, candidate integer positions, start from zero and greedily add one contract (in the sign of w*) to the instrument that most reduces tracking error (w − w*)ᵀΣ(w − w*) + shadow cost × |trades|, stop when no improvement. Static alternative he used later: https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/06/static-optimisation-of-best-set-of.html. 2016 post https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2016/03/diversification-and-small-account-size.html: wants ≥ 4 contracts at maximum forecast per instrument; otherwise thresholded positions (stay flat until a larger forecast).

### AQR

### Newfound / Hoffstein
- Rebalance timing luck (RTL): std of returns across identically managed sub-portfolios rebalancing on different dates; declines ~1/N with N tranches; often > 100 bps/yr, 100–400 bps for semi-annual 100-stock portfolios; higher for high-turnover and concentrated portfolios. Tranching = N staggered sub-portfolios each rebalanced every N periods. https://www.thinknewfound.com/rebalance-timing-luck ; https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/11/the-dumb-timing-luck-of-smart-beta/ ; https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2020/04/tranching-trend-and-mean-reversion/ (tranching has a momentum-like payoff; 20-day tranche vs immediate on the US Trend Equity index: tranching outperformed from early 1990s to 2002); https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2018/04/diversifying-the-what-how-and-when-of-trend-following/ (diversify "when" as well as "what" and "how").
- Zarattini & Pagani (Concretum), "The Tranching Dilemma", Nov 2025: US equity momentum 1991–2024, best vs worst rebalance schedule ≈ 350 bps/yr; RTL ~1/N; for small investors the extra trading cost often outweighs the benefit. https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/The-Tranching-Dilemma.pdf ; https://quantpedia.com/the-tranching-dilemma/
- Identity used below: with N tranches of a weekly rule, the aggregate holding equals the N-day moving average of the daily target weights, so a "tranched weekly" book is a daily rebalance toward MA_N(target weights). Its return is the equal-weighted average of the N sub-books' returns, so its Sharpe is ≥ the average of their Sharpes (1.23 locally) with turnover close to weekly (each day trades 1/N of a weekly change).

### Clenow
- "Following the Trend" core: 50/100-day EMA filter (long only when 50 > 100), enter on 50-day high, exit on 3-ATR move from the peak, sizing to ~20 bps of equity per ATR move (size = 0.002 × equity / ATR), signals at the close, fills next open. Independent replication on 62 futures net of costs: SR 0.75 (1990–2013), 0.57 (2014–24); the EMA filter alone earns as much as the full model; the stop adds no edge but cuts max DD −21 % vs −28 %. https://www.followingthetrend.com/the-trading-system/trading-system-rules/ ; https://github.com/lwang-genomics/trend-following-replication . Per-position stops are on the rejected list, so Clenow's exit does not transfer; his sizing is already what the rule does.

- Quantpedia multi-timeframe BTC trend: SR 0.33, DD −23.9 %, no edge. https://quantpedia.com/how-to-design-a-simple-multi-timeframe-trend-strategy-on-bitcoin/

## 4. Candidate rule changes (exact specs)

Notation: coins i = 1..9, daily UTC closes P, 60-day vol σ_i (or the estimator in C6), TV = book target vol, N = 9, w_i current weights as in `target_weights`.

### C1. Tranched weekly book = daily rebalance to the 7-day mean of the target weights (timing_cadence)
Rule: at every 00:00 UTC close compute w_i(t) exactly as today; target weight w̄_i(t) = (1/7) Σ_{k=0..6} w_i(t−k) (for the design's Monday decision the seven sub-books are the seven weekday-candle books, so the equal-weight tranche is the 7-day MA; a 5-tranche business-week version is MA_5 but crypto trades 7 days). Execute daily with whole contracts and the C2 buffer. Equivalent to running seven weekly books of 1/7 capital each, one per weekday, netted.
Claimed effect: Hoffstein — RTL ∝ 1/N, mean return unchanged; locally the seven weekday books have Sharpe 1.12–1.34 (mean 1.23) with the design at the minimum; the tranched book's return is their equal-weighted average, so expected Sharpe ≈ 1.23–1.27 (vs 1.12 design, 1.25 daily) with turnover between weekly (192 trades/yr) and daily band 10 (538). The Concretum caveat (costs for small accounts) is weak here because the daily book already shows the cost of daily trading (fees 0.67 %/yr at TV 0.25).
Overlap with rejected: "changing the decision weekday for performance" — different: this takes the average over all weekdays instead of choosing one; it is the standard cure for the luck the memo documented and left unexploited. Daily vs weekly cadence is "understood", but this is a third option with weekly-like turnover and daily-like timing diversification.
Data: none new. Implementation: `trend_paper.py` state keeps the last 7 weight vectors; `--tranches 7`. At $1,000 whole-contract rounding of 1/7-size changes means trades occur only when MA_7 weights cross a contract boundary, which is the intended smoothing. Plausibility 4.

### C2. Carver forecast-buffer on the average position, trade to the edge (costs_execution)
Rule: average position A_i = (TV/N)/σ_i × equity/(P_i × cv_i) contracts (the position at full forecast, signal = 1); buffer B_i = 0.10 × A_i; lower = round(N_i − B_i), upper = round(N_i + B_i); if held < lower buy to lower; if held > upper sell to upper; else no trade. Keep "a close to zero always trades" (when N_i = 0 and B_i < 0.5 the rounded band collapses to {0}, so it is automatic for small accounts).
Claimed effect (Carver, VERIFIED as his stated design): the position band shrinks to nothing near zero and over-trades small positions; the average-position band does not, and trading to the edge rather than the optimum roughly halves the trade size. No Sharpe loss expected (local T3c: 0–20 % proportional band changed Sharpe by ≤ 0.01 while cutting trades 278 → 163). Plausibility 4 (cheap, 1 parameter, used in production by Carver and in AFTS Strategy 8+).
Overlap: the memo's 10 % band is Carver's `position` method; this is his `forecast` method. Data: none. Implementation: `plan_orders` in `trend_paper.py` gets the per-coin average position from `target_weights` inputs (TV/N/σ_i).

### C3. Continuous capped forecast from EWMAC speeds, long-only floor (signal)
Rule (per coin): v_i = 35-day EWMA std of daily price differences (price units). For n ∈ {16, 32, 64}: raw_n = (EWMA_n(P) − EWMA_4n(P))/v_i; scaled_n = raw_n × s_n with s_16 = 3.75, s_32 = 2.65, s_64 = 1.87 (chapter-15 values; AFTS 4.10/2.79/1.91); capped_n = clip(scaled_n, −20, 20); combined = clip(FDM × mean_n(capped_n), −20, 20) with FDM 1.2 (3 rules, pooled); long-only forecast f_i = max(combined, 0)/10 ∈ [0, 2]; weight_i = f_i × (TV/N)/σ_i; gross cap 2x as now. Optionally add n = 8 (s = 5.3) since Delta's SR cost allows it (turnover of EWMAC8 ≈ 20–25 RT/yr [UNVERIFIED, Carver's turnover table from memory]).
Claimed effect: Carver AFTS Strategy 7–9 — forecast scaling "does better than any other strategy so far" but he notes the trend-strength evidence is weak for slow EWMAC; Baltas–Kosowski — continuous TREND rule cuts turnover 24 % with no performance change; Levine–Pedersen — EWMAC and TSMOM are equivalent filters, so expect smoother sizing and lower turnover rather than new alpha. Local analogue: our signal already has 4 levels {0,1/3,2/3,1}. Expected: Sharpe ±0.05, turnover down, max forecast 2x average increases position concentration in strong trends (a long-only f = 2 doubles the per-coin budget; cap f at 1.5 if that is unwanted).
Overlap with rejected: "single lookback" and lookback grids were tested as sign rules; "ML/signal stacking" no (fixed equal weights, fixed scalars). Plausibility 3. Data: daily closes only. Implementation: new `forecast_ewmac()` in `trend_backtest.py` behind a flag; scalars must be fixed before testing (use the chapter-15 values, do not re-estimate on crypto, or estimate on 2020–21 and test on 2022–26 only).

### C4. Speed limit and holding-cost test per coin (costs_execution / universe)
Rule: for each coin compute (a) cost per round trip in SR units c_i = 0.001462/σ_i, (b) turnover T of the rule (round trips of the average position per year; 39 daily, 14 weekly from the local runs), (c) holding cost h_i = trailing-365-day mean Delta funding (annualised, as a fraction of notional)/σ_i. Trading rule allowed if c_i × T ≤ 0.15; coin kept in the long book if c_i × T + h_i ≤ 1/3 of the assumed pre-cost single-coin SR (use 0.4 → budget 0.13) — i.e. drop a coin whose own Delta funding drag exceeds ~0.10 SR for a year, re-evaluated quarterly.
Claimed effect: Carver's rule (Systematic Trading ch. 12; AFTS Strategy 3/9): cost ≤ 1/3 of pre-cost SR, 0.10 SR per instrument, 0.15 per rule. Local arithmetic: fees are 0.03–0.10 SR, funding 0.23–0.33 SR averaged over coins and years (2020–21 dominated), so funding is the only cost that can fail the test; Delta alt funding caps (XRP/DOGE 21.9 %, ADA 273.75 %/yr per the memo) are where it would bind.
Overlap with rejected: "funding carry/basis" and "funding as a directional signal" — this is a static annual cost gate (Carver's instrument selection), not a daily signal; still, a per-coin exclusion that updates with funding IS a slow funding signal, so the overlap is real and the pre-registration must fix the gate to a 365-day trailing mean updated quarterly. Plausibility 3. Data: Delta FUNDING:<sym> candles (already loaded by `load_delta`). Implementation: diagnostic script first; expected effect on Sharpe small unless one coin is dropped.

### C5. Vol attenuation multiplier (Carver Strategy 13) instead of a binary vol filter (sizing)
Rule: σ_i(t) daily vol estimate; ref_i(t) = rolling mean of σ_i over min(available, 2500) days (2-year expanding mean on crypto history); V = σ_i/ref_i; Q = empirical quantile of V(t) within V's own history to date; M = EWMA_span10(2 − 1.5 Q) ∈ [0.5, 2.0]; forecast_i × M (then re-cap). Applied per coin to the trend signal (C3) or to today's sign-average signal (f_i = min(signal_i × M, 1) if a long-only cap of 1 is kept; Carver's version allows up to 2).
Claimed effect: Carver (2021 post, [UNVERIFIED]) uses it live on all momentum/breakout rules because they "perform much worse when volatility rises"; AFTS says the gain is small; QuantConnect replication SR 0.944 vs 0.749 on Jul 2020–Jul 2023 [UNVERIFIED, vendor, short]. Local counter-evidence: binary flat-when-BTC-vol > 70/90 % cut Sharpe to 1.02/1.00 (T3j) and 1.18/1.09 on daily (D3). The continuous, per-coin, relative-to-own-history version is the only variant not yet tested; it also *increases* size in low-vol regimes, which the binary filter never did.
Overlap with rejected: "realized-vol regime filter (cuts Sharpe)" — same family; different formula (continuous multiplier 0.5–2.0 on relative vol quantile, EWMA-smoothed, per coin) and the rejected test was absolute BTC vol thresholds. Plausibility 2. Data: none new. Implementation: ~20 lines; pre-register a single parameterisation (2 − 1.5Q, span 10).

### C6. Robust vol estimator: EWMA-35 blended with a long-run average and a quantile floor (sizing)
Rule: σ_short = EWMA std of daily log returns, span 35, min_periods 10; σ_long = EWMA(span = 2 × 365)(σ_short) (Carver uses 20 years; crypto history allows ~2); σ = 0.7 σ_short + 0.3 σ_long; floor at the rolling 5 % quantile of σ over the last 500 days (min 100 obs); annualise ×√365; keep the 5 % absolute floor. Optional: Yang–Zhang OHLC estimator for σ_short (Delta and Binance daily candles carry OHLC).
Claimed effect: Carver production default (`mixed_vol_calc`, 0.35 slow in his config) — smoother sizing, fewer vol-driven trades after spikes; Baltas–Kosowski — better vol estimation cut TSMOM turnover by about a third with no significant Sharpe change; pre-cost Sharpe best at a 3-month window (ours is 60 days). Local T3e: 30/60/90-day simple std → 1.16/1.12/1.08, so the direction of a faster estimator is mildly positive while blending adds stability. Expected: Sharpe ±0.03, turnover down 10–30 %.
Overlap: "no vol targeting" rejected — this keeps vol targeting and changes only the estimator; vol window 30/60/90 was tested as simple std. Plausibility 3. Data: none (OHLC optional). Implementation: replace the `vol[c]` line in `target_weights` behind a flag.

### C7. Correlation-adjusted gross: Baltas–Kosowski CF (sizing)
Rule: ρ̄(t) = average pairwise correlation of the nine coins' daily returns over the last 60 days (or 90, Baltas–Kosowski use 3-month windows); CF(t) = √(N/(1+(N−1)ρ̄(t))); scale all weights by CF(t)/CF_ref where CF_ref = CF at the full-sample median ρ̄ (so the average gross is unchanged and only the time variation remains; otherwise the change is a pure TV rescale, which T3a shows is Sharpe-neutral). Static Carver IDM = CF at median ρ̄ ≈ 1.17 (ρ̄ 0.7) is the same thing without time variation and only rescales.
Claimed effect: Baltas–Kosowski — the correlation-adjusted TSMOM outperformed the naive one, more so post-2008, and "safeguards against tail risk" after costs [UNVERIFIED, no Sharpe numbers extracted]. Mechanism in crypto: pairwise correlations spike in sell-offs (May 2021, Nov 2022), so CF cuts gross into the crash and raises it in dispersed markets.
Overlap with rejected: "drawdown de-leveraging" (equity-based) and "realized-vol regime filter" (vol-based) — this conditions on correlation, which neither did; but all three cut exposure in stress and the local tests say such cuts cost Sharpe, so prior is modest. Plausibility 3. Data: none. Implementation: 9×9 correlation each day in `target_weights`.

### C8. Dynamic optimisation of whole contracts for the $1,000 book (sizing / small account)
Rule (Carver greedy minimum tracking error): target unrounded weights w*; covariance Σ (60-day); candidate integer contract vectors c with weight w(c) = c × cv × P/equity; start at c = 0; repeatedly add one contract to the coin (in the sign of w*_i − w_i(c), long-only so only buys from zero, and only coins with w*_i > 0) that most reduces TE = (w − w*)ᵀ Σ (w − w*) + λ × trade cost vs current holdings; stop when no step reduces TE. λ = 2 × 7.31 bps per contract traded expressed in the same return units (Carver: shadow cost helps modestly).
Claimed effect: Carver's live system runs this (`optimisedPositions`, VERIFIED in `run_system.py`); he reports it "cracked" the small-account problem [UNVERIFIED blog]. Local bound: rounding costs < 1.5 %/yr at $1,000 and about 0.04–0.07 Sharpe ($280: 1.11, $1,000: 1.08, $2,300: 1.15, $5,000: 1.15 on Delta 2024–26), so the maximum gain is that gap; at $1,000 BTC (~$80) and BNB (~$73) contracts round to zero and the optimiser would substitute correlated coins (ETH for BTC, etc.).
Overlap: none in the rejected list (universe changes were about which coins, not how to round). Plausibility 2 (small bounded gain, more code, Carver's first attempt failed). Data: none. Implementation: 50–80 lines in `plan_orders`; test at $280/$1,000/$2,300.

### C9. Baltas–Kosowski TREND signal in place of sign(return) (signal)
Rule: for each lookback L ∈ {20, 60, 120}: regress log P on time over the last L days; t_L = slope/SE(slope) (Newey–West optional); s_L = +1 if t_L > 1, −1 if t_L < −1, else t_L; signal_i = max(mean_L(s_L), 0); weight as now.
Claimed effect: turnover −24 % (TREND vs SIGN) with no significant performance change on 1974/84–2013 futures [UNVERIFIED]; sparse exposure to weak trends. Plausibility 3; overlaps C3 in purpose (continuous sizing) but with 0 new parameters beyond the ±1 thresholds. Data: none. Implementation: ~15 lines.

### C10. Dynamic slow/fast weighting at turning points (Garg–Goulding–Harvey–Mazzoleni) (signal)
Rule: SLOW = sign(P_t/P_{t−250} − 1) (12 months; or our 120-day), FAST = sign(P_t/P_{t−20} − 1). State: Bull (+,+), Correction (+,−), Bear (−,−), Rebound (−,+). Signal = w_S × SLOW + (1 − w_S) × FAST with w_S = 0.5 in Bull/Bear, w_S → 1 (trust slow, stay long) in the months after a Correction, w_S → 0 (trust fast, re-enter) after a Rebound; long-only floor at 0; paper's dynamic weights are estimated — fix them ex ante (e.g. 0.8/0.2 and 0.2/0.8) to avoid fitting.
Claimed effect: dynamic-speed Sharpe above the best static speed in most of 20 equity markets (JFE 2023); multi-asset FAJ 2023: static 7.5 %/yr 30-yr, 1.8 % last decade, dynamic 4.3 % last decade [UNVERIFIED]. Equity-centric, estimated weights, in-sample heavy. Overlap: "signal stacking" (rejected) — this is a 2-state rule with fixed weights, not ML. Plausibility 2. Data: none.

## 5. Supporting (not candidates) — practitioner results that back the current design
- Long/flat: Carver's 2018 long-only trend test (not significant but positive); Harvey et al. vol targeting helps risk assets; Clenow replication: the EMA filter alone carries the edge, stops only reshape DD (per-position stops rejected here too).
- Speed: Carver "EWMAC(16,64) historically most profitable", Winton intermediate speed best (1.12 vs 0.87/0.81), Man AHL: slower = higher Sharpe, faster = better skew. Our 20/60/120 sits in the intermediate band. The "barbell" paper (drop the medium horizon) is in-sample and contradicts Carver's equal-weight advice; do not act on it.
- Equal forecast weights across speeds, fixed scalars, no per-instrument tuning (Carver), no tuning of filter form (Zakamulin), no weekday choice (Hoffstein) — all consistent with the memo's refusals.
