## 1. Rebalance timing luck (RTL): definition, size, formula

- Hoffstein, Sibears, Faber, "Rebalance Timing Luck: The Difference between Hired and Fired", Journal of Index Investing 10(1) 2019, pp. 27-36; SSRN 3319045 (Oct 2018). https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3319045 [UNVERIFIED]. RTL = "the standard deviation in returns between identically managed investment portfolios that are rebalanced on different dates". They derive an ex-ante model and show equal weighting across N sub-indexes (tranches) reduces RTL by 1/N; overlapping portfolios are proven optimal (in the no-cost setting).
- Formula (Hoffstein LinkedIn post, https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rebalance-timing-luck-corey-hoffstein [UNVERIFIED]): **L = 0.5 x (T / F) x S**, T = annualized turnover, F = rebalances per year, S = volatility of a long/short portfolio "long what the strategy currently holds, short what it would hold if reconstructed now" (the 2018 "Quantifying Timing Luck" post, https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2018/01/quantifying-timing-luck/ [UNVERIFIED], uses the stocks-minus-bonds variance for a 60/40 example). Example: 100-stock value portfolio rebalanced semi-annually ~2.5%/yr vs a luck-neutral benchmark; multiply by sqrt(2) to compare two non-neutral portfolios. I could not confirm the origin of the 0.5.
- [SYNTHETIC] plugging the weekly book in: T (one-way) 14.2x of average gross, F = 52, S unknown; with S = 10-20% L = 1.4-2.7%/yr; observed weekday sd of CAGR is 1.1% (range 2.8%). Same order of magnitude, so the formula is at least not contradicted; S for a 9-coin long/flat book is probably below the strategy's own 12.4% vol.
- Hoffstein, Faber, Braun, "Rebalance Timing Luck: The Dumb (Timing) Luck of Smart Beta" (Feb 2020), https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/11/the-dumb-timing-luck-of-smart-beta/ , summary https://www.panoptica.com/rebalance-timing-luck-the-dumb-timing-luck-of-smart-beta-by-hoffstein-faber-and-braun/ [UNVERIFIED]: long-only value/size/momentum/quality/low-vol indices; RTL "often exceeding 100 bps annualized"; 100-stock semi-annual portfolios 100-400 bps by style; "as much as 200 bps annualized for higher turnover strategies, one-year discrepancies up to 40 points"; more concentration, less frequent rebalancing, higher turnover -> more RTL. Hoffstein thread: rebalance frequency "should be derived as a function of signal decay speed and cost; a momentum portfolio likely has to rebalance more frequently than a value portfolio" (https://x.com/choffstein/status/1196126894732664832 [UNVERIFIED]).
- Zarattini & Pagani (Concretum), "The Tranching Dilemma: A Cost-Aware Approach to Mitigate Rebalance Timing Luck in Factor Portfolios", Nov 17 2025, SSRN 5747964; https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/The-Tranching-Dilemma.pdf ; https://quantpedia.com/the-tranching-dilemma/ ; https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/the-tranching-dilemma [UNVERIFIED, all three]. US equity momentum 1991-2024: best-vs-worst schedule ~350 bps/yr CAGR at intra-month resolution; "RTL declining roughly in proportion to 1/N while annual turnover remains broadly unchanged" and average return unchanged; tranching adds trading cost (more, smaller orders), so the optimal N depends on account size: "small portfolios (e.g. $25K) are better off with minimal tranching (~2 tranches), as trading frictions dominate; institutional portfolios benefit from aggressive tranching". Vendor paper, equities, in-sample, but the mechanism and the 1/N scaling replicate Hoffstein.
- Blitz, van der Grient, van Vliet (2010), "Fundamental Indexation: Rebalancing Assumptions and Performance", JII 1(2):82-88, SSRN 1574150 [UNVERIFIED]: a fundamental index rebalanced in March beat cap-weight by >10% in 2009, one rebalanced in September lagged; divergences do not mean-revert; remedy = blend several indexes rebalanced on different dates "without raising turnover". FTSE Russell's staggered-rebalancing paper (https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/other/ftse-rafi-staggered-rebalancing-paper.pdf [UNVERIFIED]) credits them with the idea.
- J.P. Morgan "Momentum Strategies Across Asset Classes" (2015), https://www.cmegroup.com/education/files/jpm-momentum-strategies-2015-04-15-1681565.pdf [UNVERIFIED]: multi-asset 12-month time-series momentum, Sharpe 1.08 (16th working-day rebalance) to 1.26 (19th), month-end 1.21. Industry note, but the only cross-asset TSMOM rebalance-day dispersion number found; its range (0.18) matches ours (0.22).
- Crypto: no paper on RTL/tranching in crypto found. Related: Springer "Cryptocurrency momentum has (not) its moments" (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9 [UNVERIFIED]) holds one week and rebalances at the start of each week; a Medium day-of-week momentum post (308% -> 44.5%, Sharpe 0.77 after a look-ahead fix, https://medium.com/coinmonks/day-of-week-momentum-strategy-for-crypto-with-308-annual-returns-and-sharpe-2-96-but-4bd4ef0a31a8 [UNVERIFIED]) is a warning about weekday selection, not evidence for it.

## 2. Tranching / overlapping portfolios: constructions and reported effects

- Newfound practice: "implements all strategies with overlapping portfolios"; NFUSTE (US Trend Equity Index, https://www.thinknewfound.com/newfound-research-us-trend-equity-index [UNVERIFIED]) is split into 20 equal tranches, each rebalanced once every 20 trading days, one tranche per day; allocations average three trend models. Hoffstein tweet: "instead of an annual rebalance, you rebalance 1/12th of your portfolio every month (basically, you correct for 1/12th of the drift)" (https://x.com/choffstein/status/1251629040101781504 [UNVERIFIED]) -> tranching == partial adjustment of 1/K of the gap per sub-period.
- "Tranching, Trend, and Mean Reversion" (Hoffstein, Apr 27 2020, https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2020/04/tranching-trend-and-mean-reversion/ [UNVERIFIED]): tranched rebalancing's payoff "closely resembles a portfolio that combines strategic rebalancing and momentum/trend-following"; tranching works because "it uses the lead/lag effects of momentum and mean-reversion to cancel each other out" (https://twitter.com/choffstein/status/1251983036159193088 [UNVERIFIED]). Notes it "does not consider trading costs".
- "Well, you... No, you gotta do more than that" (Newfound, Apr 20 2020, https://advisoranalyst.com/2020/04/20/well-you-no-you-gotta-do-more-than-that.html/ [UNVERIFIED]): trend signals implemented immediately vs a 20-day tranche, 14 asset classes: including March 2020 only 2 of 14 (mortgage REITs, Brazilian equities) were better off implementing immediately; through February "all asset classes were worse off" implementing immediately. A follow-up on the index: immediate implementation out-performed the 20-day tranche until the early 1990s, then tranching "dramatically out-performed". Evidence not limited to US equities. Exact return/vol tables not retrievable.
- "Diversifying the What, How, and When of Trend Following" (Newfound 2018, https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2018/04/diversifying-the-what-how-and-when-of-trend-following/ [UNVERIFIED]): "when" = run variations rebalanced at different points in time; "can dramatically reduce the impact that timing luck can have on realized results".
- Allocate Smartly, "Timing Luck and Portfolio Tranching" (VAA tranched 2/4/8 ways, with transaction costs) and "Taming Excessive Timing Luck in TAA by Tranching Strategies" (https://allocatesmartly.com/timing-luck-portfolio-tranching/ , https://allocatesmartly.com/taming-excessive-timing-luck-in-taa-by-tranching-strategies/ [UNVERIFIED]): suggested 8 tranches for a timing-luck-sensitive TAA strategy; tranched portfolios averaged 14.2% vs 13.9% untranched; members use tranching ~10% of the time. Numbers from snippets only.
- Jegadeesh & Titman 1993 (https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/jegadeesh-titman93.pdf) and 2001 (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w7159/w7159.pdf) [UNVERIFIED]: in month t hold the portfolios formed in t and the previous K-1 months, equal weight per cohort, so 1/K of the book is rebalanced each month; "no significant difference in returns between overlapping and non-overlapping portfolios"; purpose is statistical power and smoother turnover.
- Baltas & Kosowski, "Momentum Strategies in Futures Markets and Trend-following Funds" (2012/2013; https://www.efmaefm.org/0efmameetings/efma%20annual%20meetings/2012-Barcelona/papers/BK_MOMF_Full.pdf [UNVERIFIED]): 71 futures 1974-2012; "follow the overlapping methodology of Jegadeesh and Titman (2001) and perform portfolio rebalancing at the end of each month/week/day ... 1/K-th of the portfolio is only rebalanced every month/week/day"; TSMOM Sharpe > 1.20 at monthly/weekly/daily frequencies with low cross-correlation between frequencies.
- Baltas & Kosowski, "Demystifying Time-Series Momentum Strategies: Volatility Estimators, Trading Rules and Pairwise Correlations" (2013/2020; https://www.cmegroup.com/education/files/demystifiing-time-series-momentum-strategies.pdf , Imperial Spiral copy [UNVERIFIED]): a continuous trend-strength (t-statistic) signal instead of sign() and a better vol estimator "reduce portfolio turnover by more than one third without statistically significant performance degradation"; one review says the TREND signal alone cuts turnover ~24%; an earlier CME-hosted version says trend-signal strategies "reduce the amount of trading by two thirds" at an insignificantly different Sharpe. Turnover of standard sign-TSMOM ~55-85% per month.

## 3. Buffers, smoothing, partial adjustment, speed limit (Carver, Garleanu-Pedersen, AQR)

- Carver, Advanced Futures Trading Strategies, strategy 8 (notes repo majkong14/advanced-futures-notes, raw README read [VERIFIED], https://github.com/majkong14/advanced-futures-notes): buffer B_i,t = F x Capital x IDM x Weight_i x tau / (Multiplier_i x Price_i,t x FX x sigma%_i,t), i.e. F times the average (full-on) position; "F can easily be 0.10"; lower/upper = round(N +/- B); no trade inside; outside, trade to the nearer edge. "Buffering slows down trading and allows a set of forecasts that is a little quicker." Smoothing: "noise can be handled by buffering or smoothing the forecast; smoothing is unnecessary for trend filters, since those are already moving averages" (applies to EWMAC, not to a sign() signal). Speed limit: 1/3 x pre-cost SR > rolls x 2 + turnover x risk-adjusted cost per trade; Carver's notional cap 0.10-0.15 SR of cost per instrument. pysystemtrade `systems/buffering.py` [VERIFIED copy in scratchpad]: `buffer_method` forecast|position|none, `apply_buffers_to_position` = position +/- buffer.
- Carver blog Nov 2021 (https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/11/ [UNVERIFIED]): "buffering reduces turnover without hurting performance, provided the buffer isn't too wide; I have usually set it at a simple 10% of the average position; the exact width is hard to calibrate". 2016 comment thread: 10% works for large positions, poorly for small ones because of the fractional-contract problem (our case at $1,000). Podcast: an expensive instrument should get a wider buffer. 2025 post (https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2025/03/very-slow-mean-reversion-and-some.html [UNVERIFIED]): with a 0.22 SR cost budget, a two-day forecast horizon (trading daily) is possible but leaves ~0.07 SR. "How fast should we trade?" (https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/04/how-fast-should-we-trade.html [UNVERIFIED]).
- [SYNTHETIC] speed limit on our rule: funding 2.15%/yr at 11.7% vol = 0.18 SR holding cost for every cadence. Forward pre-cost SR 0.74 (0.5 net + costs): budget 0.25 SR, 0.06 SR = 0.74%/yr left for fees; daily band-10 fees 0.67% = 0.057 SR (fits), weekly 0.25% = 0.021 SR. At forward net 0.3 the budget equals the funding and no cadence fits: funding, not fees, is the binding cost, and cadence choice is nearly irrelevant to Carver's test.
- Garleanu & Pedersen 2013, "Dynamic Trading with Predictable Returns and Transaction Costs", JF 68(6) (https://nbgarleanu.github.io/DynTrad.pdf , NBER w15205 [UNVERIFIED]): optimal rule under quadratic costs: x_t = x_{t-1} + lambda (aim_t - x_{t-1}), "aim in front of the target, trade partially toward the aim"; lambda from the cost parameter; slower-decaying predictors get more weight in the aim.
- AQR "Portfolio Rebalancing Part 1: Strategic Asset Allocation" (https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/Portfolio-Rebalancing-Part-1-Strategic-Asset-Allocation.pdf [UNVERIFIED]): "partial rebalancing is the more efficient cost-reduction measure if price momentum effects are ignored (bigger turnover reduction for a smaller increase in allocation drift)"; "all processes (even rebalancing only 25% toward targets) achieve similar improvements". Strategic allocation, not trend.
- Newfound timing-risk note [UNVERIFIED snippet]: "rebalancing in discrete time steps (e.g. monthly) should be avoided; rebalance partially but more continuously".
- arXiv 2607.19497 "The Science and Practice of Trend-Following Systems" (2026) [UNVERIFIED]: a discrete-trade system has about half the turnover of continuous-signal systems; Sharpe 0.47-0.55 across systems, differences insignificant; "beneficial to introduce some discretization rules".
- Stock-selection signal smoothing (arXiv 2602.00196 [UNVERIFIED]): 21-day MA cuts turnover 82%, gross Sharpe -31% - the cost of over-smoothing a fast signal.

## 4. Execution lag

- No paper isolating a one-day execution lag for TSMOM futures. Academic crypto TSMOM theses use "signal lagged by one day" as the convention (Erasmus thesis https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/44390/Wisselink-NJ-483391-BA-thesis.pdf ; AUT paper [UNVERIFIED]). Liu (NBER w24877 [UNVERIFIED]): Bitcoin daily return predicts 1-6 day ahead returns; daily-frequency momentum Sharpe 0.09, weekly 0.23, monthly 0.31. Narayan et al. 2015 / Boons & Prado 2019 / Jegadeesh et al. 2022 (cited in arXiv 2507.15876 [UNVERIFIED]): daily futures momentum mean-reverts once microstructure is included.
- Local T3k is the decisive evidence for this book: +4..+24 h lag Sharpe 1.09-1.15 vs 1.12. Execution lag is free; what is not free is which close the signal is sampled on (hour dispersion 0.20) and which weekday (0.22).

## 5. Candidate list (exact rules; details in the structured output)

C1 K=7 daily-tranched weekly book (= 7-day SMA of the daily target). Predicted (not measured) Sharpe ~1.23-1.26, trades/yr between 192 and 538, rounding limits it at $1,000.
C2 K=2 tranches for the hand book: Mon 00:05 + Thu 00:05 UTC, half the capital each; halves RTL, in-sample pair mean 1.21.
C3 Partial adjustment toward the daily target, lambda = 1/5 (EWMA position smoothing, Garleanu-Pedersen).
C4 Carver buffer on the daily rule, F = 0.10-0.30 of the full-on position, in contracts; trade to the buffer edge.
C5 Signal smoothing: 5-day overlapping (Jegadeesh-Titman) average of the sign signal before flooring.
C6 Decision-hour tranching: average the signal over the six H-aligned close series, execute once.
C7 Cadence by Carver's speed limit (a computed gate): daily is affordable at forward SR 0.5; funding is the binding cost.
C8 Asymmetric cadence: entries/increases weekly, decreases/exits daily (signal-driven, not price stops). Thin evidence.

Not proposed: picking Wed/Thu/20:00 UTC for performance (rejected, curve fit); intra-day TWAP of the Monday order (T3k shows nothing to gain and 1-contract orders cannot be split at $1,000); 20-tranche NFUSTE-style daily tranching (needs ~$20k+ for whole contracts, Concretum's cost argument).
